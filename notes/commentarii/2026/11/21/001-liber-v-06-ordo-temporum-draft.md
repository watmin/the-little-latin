# Caput LIV: Ōrdō Temporum

*Liber V, Modus Subiūnctīvus · Chapter 54*

*In which the subjunctive learns to keep time with its boss. For three chapters one quiet rule has been at work: Chapter 51's goals, Chapter 52's results and Chapter 53's tucked-in questions all picked their subjunctive by looking at the main verb. Chapter 52 said of "have done" verbs, "Chapter 54 gives this matching its full rules," and of Caesar's story told in the present, "Chapter 54 sorts it out." Chapter 53 promised the rule "its proper name and the fine print." Here they are: the name (the* sequence of tenses*), the two teams, "have done" main verbs, "going to" inside a past story, Caesar telling a war in the present, and the one kind of clause that breaks free. Along the way: Cicero on his great rival and on a dead friend, Caesar twice, and Saint Luke's story of a father who can't speak, asks for a wax tablet and writes your name on it. Speaking goal: match your tenses without thinking, in the present and in a story.*

!!! abstract "In this chapter"
    - **Saying:** *Rogābō quid fēcerīs · Nesciēbam quid factūrus essēs · Ēmī ut edāmus · Utinam scīrem ubi esset!*
    - **Grammar words, in plain English:** sequence of tenses (the matching rule) · primary sequence (the "now" team) · secondary or historic sequence (the "past" team) · historic present (a past story told in the present)
    - **Forms:** nothing new to build! Every form today is one you already have: *faciat, fēcerit, factūrus sit · faceret, fēcisset, factūrus esset*
    - **Laws:** the Law of the Two Teams · the Law of "Have Done" · the Law of the Free Result · the Law of the Matching Rule
    - **Words:** *ōrdō, ōrdinis* (order) · *pugillāris* (writing tablet)
    - **Reading:** *Avus nārrat* (day twenty-two: Grandpa tells the merchant the story of the hidden cheese, keeps his tenses in order, and wins a prize he doesn't keep)
    - **Speaking:** say the same thing now and in a story; use "have done" with the present; tell a story in the present

## 1. The rule has a name

:: Ōrdō temporum. || The order of tenses. (teaching line; the chapter title)

Rogō quid faciās… rogāvī quid facerēs.

{#g-01}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Three pairs from Chapters 51–53. *Edō ut vīvam / Ēdī ut vīverem · Avus tam fessus est ut dormiat / … erat ut dormīret · Rogō quid faciās / Rogāvī quid facerēs.* What changed in each pair? | The **main verb** moved from now to a past story, and the subjunctive moved with it: present ***vīvam, dormiat, faciās*** → imperfect ***vīverem, dormīret, facerēs***. |
| So who's the boss? | **The main verb.** Its time picks the subjunctive's time; the subjunctive matches. |
| Grammar books call this the **sequence of tenses**. Plain words? | **The matching rule**: the main verb's time picks the subjunctive's time. (*Tense*, "the time of a verb," comes from Latin *tempus*, Chapter 14; *sequence*, from Latin *sequor*, follow, Chapter 43, is "what follows.") |
| The chapter title, *Ōrdō temporum*. (*Ōrdō, ōrdinis*, m.: order, row; English *order, ordinal*.) Whose genitive is *temporum*? | *Tempus, temporis*: ***temporum***, "of the tenses." "**The order of tenses**": a Latin name for the same rule. (Grammar books written in Latin call it *cōnsecūtiō temporum*, "the following of tenses.") |
| Chapter 53's Law of Now and Earlier: was that something else? | **No.** It was the matching rule for tucked-in questions. Today it covers every subjunctive that hangs on a main verb, and you get the fine print three chapters promised. |

## 2. Two teams

:: Verbum rēgnat, subiūnctīvus sequitur. || The verb rules; the subjunctive follows. (teaching line)

Nunc… ōlim.

{#g-02}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| List the main verbs you know that point at **now or later**: "I ask," "I'll ask," "I'll have asked," "Ask!" | ***Rogō, rogābō, rogāverō, rogā!***: present, future, future perfect (Chapter 27), and commands. Call them **the now team**. |
| And the ones that tell a **past story**: "I was asking," "I asked," "I had asked." | ***Rogābam, rogāvī, rogāveram***: imperfect, perfect, pluperfect. **The past team.** |
| Which subjunctives does each team take? | The now team takes the **present** (same time) or **perfect** (earlier): *faciās, fēcerīs*. The past team takes the **imperfect** or **pluperfect**: *facerēs, fēcissēs*. |
| Old-school names? | The now team is the **primary sequence**. The past team is the **secondary sequence**, also called the **historic sequence**. ("Primary": first, the main one; "historic": for history, stories.) |
| Look at the subjunctives themselves. Which pair belongs to which team? | ***Amem, amāverim*** (present, perfect) are now-team forms. ***Amārem, amāvissem*** (imperfect, pluperfect) are past-team forms. Each team keeps to its own pair. (Section 9 has the one exception.) |

!!! success "The Law of the Two Teams"
    The **sequence of tenses** (the matching rule): a subjunctive that hangs on a main verb takes its time from that verb.

    | Team | Main verb | Same time | Earlier |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | **now** (primary) | *rogō, rogābō, rogāverō, rogā!* | present *faciās* | perfect *fēcerīs* |
    | **past** (secondary, historic) | *rogābam, rogāvī, rogāveram* | imperfect *facerēs* | pluperfect *fēcissēs* |

## 3. One rule, three kinds of clause

:: Edō ut vīvam. || I eat so that I may live. (teaching line; Chapter 51)

Ut… ut… quid…

{#g-03}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| A **goal** (Chapter 51). "I'm going to the forum to buy a cheese," then "Yesterday I went to the forum to buy a cheese." (*Eō, iī*.) | ***Ad forum eō ut cāseum emam. · Herī ad forum iī ut cāseum emerem.*** |
| A **result** (Chapter 52). "Grandpa is so hungry that he's eating the bread," then "Grandpa was so hungry that he was eating the bread." (*Ēsuriō*.) | ***Avus tam ēsurit ut pānem edat. · Avus tam ēsuriēbat ut pānem ederet.*** |
| A **tucked-in question** (Chapter 53). "I don't know where the dog is," then "I didn't know where the dog was." | ***Nesciō ubi canis sit. · Nesciēbam ubi canis esset.*** |
| Can a **goal** ever take the perfect or pluperfect subjunctive? | **No.** A goal is always still ahead of the main verb, so it never looks back: present on the now team, imperfect on the past team. |
| Which kind uses every box: same time, earlier, and later? | **The tucked-in question**: *quid faciās, quid fēcerīs, quid factūrus sīs* (Chapter 53). |

## 4. Futures and commands

:: Crās sciēmus. || Tomorrow we'll know. (teaching line)

Rogābō… Dīc mihi…

{#g-04}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "I'll ask Grandpa what he did." | ***Rogābō Avum quid fēcerit.*** A future plays on the now team: earlier = perfect subjunctive. |
| "Tomorrow we'll know who ate the cheese." | ***Crās sciēmus quis cāseum ēderit.*** |
| "Tell me why you're laughing." | ***Dīc mihi cūr rīdeās.*** A command is on the now team too. |
| "Tomorrow I'll go to the forum to buy bread." | ***Crās ad forum ībō ut pānem emam.*** |

## 5. "Have done"

:: Ego vēnī ut vītam habeant. || I have come so that they may have life. (John 10:10; Chapter 51)

Vēnī… vīxī…

{#g-05}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 51's John 10:10: *ego vēnī ut vītam habeant.* *Vēnī* is a perfect, and the perfect is on the past team. Why present ***habeant***? | *Vēnī* here means "**I have come**": he's here **now**, and the goal is still ahead. A perfect that means "have done" can play on the now team. |
| Chapter 52's Cicero, *On Old Age* 84: *ita vīxī, ut nōn frūstrā mē nātum exīstimem.* Which "have done"? | ***Vīxī***, "I **have lived**": old Cato looks back on his whole life from today, and the result is still true now, ***exīstimem***. |
| Now the story kind: "Yesterday I came to see you." | ***Herī vēnī ut tē vidērem.*** *Herī* makes *vēnī* "I came," a story: past team. |
| Back home with a cheese: "Look, I've bought a cheese so we can eat!" | ***Ecce, cāseum ēmī ut edāmus!*** The cheese is on the table now: "have done," now team. |
| Chapter 53 left *Rogāvī quid facerēs* as a story. Could *rogāvī* ever be "I have asked"? | **Yes**: *Iam rogāvī quid faciās*, "I've already asked what you're doing." But that's rarer. When in doubt, a perfect tells a **story** and takes the past team. |

!!! success "The Law of “Have Done”"
    A **perfect** main verb normally plays on the **past team**: it tells a story ("I came," "I asked"). When it clearly means "I **have** done," looking from today at something still true now, it may play on the **now team**: *vēnī ut habeant · vīxī ut exīstimem · ēmī ut edāmus.*

## 6. One form, three Englishes

:: Nesciō quid fēcerit. || I don't know what he did. (teaching line)

Fēcit? Faciēbat? Fēcit!

{#g-06}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Nesciō quid Avus fēcerit.* How many ways can you say it in English? | Three: "I don't know what Grandpa **did**," "… **was doing**," "… **has done**." After a now verb, the perfect subjunctive is the only form for "earlier," so it covers them all. |
| Cicero's Laelius on his dead friend Scipio (*On Friendship* 11): *Quam autem cīvitātī cārus fuerit, maerōre fūneris indicātum est.* (*Quam*: how; *cīvitās*: city, state; *cārus*: dear; *maeror*: grief; *fūnus, fūneris*: funeral; *indicō*: show.) Translate. | "And **how dear he was** to the city **has been shown** by the grief at his funeral." |
| Asked straight out, it's *Quam cārus erat?*, an imperfect. Why ***fuerit***, not *esset*? | The main verb *indicātum est* means "**has been** shown": everyone saw it, and still knows it now. Now team, and the dearness was earlier: the perfect subjunctive, standing in for *erat*. |
| So what does the now team do with "was doing, used to be"? | It uses the perfect subjunctive. The now team has only two subjunctives, present and perfect, and "earlier" of every kind goes to the perfect. |

## 7. "Going to," inside a past story

:: Nesciēbam quid factūrus essēs. || I didn't know what you were going to do. (teaching line)

Factūrus sit… factūrus esset.

{#g-07}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 53's Law table had a last box: *Rogāvī quid factūrus essēs.* Build "I didn't know what Grandpa **was going to** do." | ***Nesciēbam quid Avus factūrus esset.*** Chapter 39's *-ūrus* + the past-team *esset*. |
| "Grandma asked when we would come home." (*Ventūrus*.) | ***Avia rogāvit quandō domum ventūrī essēmus.*** |
| 54 BC. Gauls have wiped out a Roman army, and Caesar has written to his officer Labienus to bring his legion. But a Gallic army is camped three miles from Labienus. Caesar (*Gallic War* 5.47.4): *Labiēnus … litterās Caesarī remittit, quantō cum perīculō legiōnem ex hībernīs ēductūrus esset.* (*Litterae*: a letter; *remittō*: send back; *quantō cum perīculō*: with how great danger, like Chapter 35's *magnā cum cūrā*; *hīberna*: winter camp; *ēdūcō*: lead out.) Translate. | "Labienus sends a letter back to Caesar (saying) with how great danger he **would** lead the legion out of winter camp." |
| *Remittit* is present, but *ēductūrus **esset*** is past-team. How? | Caesar is telling a **past** story in the present. Section 8 sorts that out. |

## 8. Caesar's present: either team

:: Lēgātōs mittunt quī dīcerent. || They send envoys to say… (after Caesar, *Gallic War* 1.7.3; Chapter 52)

Mittunt… praemittit…

{#g-08}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 52 named the **historic present**: a past story told in the present, to make it vivid. Caesar, *Gallic War* 1.7.3: *lēgātōs … mittunt … quī dīcerent.* Which team? | **Past team**: ***dīcerent***. Caesar knows it's a story. |
| Same book, 1.15.1, against the same Helvetians: *equitātumque omnem … praemittit, quī videant quās in partēs hostēs iter faciant.* (*Equitātus*: cavalry; *praemittō*: send ahead; *quās in partēs*: in which direction; *hostēs*: the enemy; *iter*: journey, march.) Translate. | "And he sends all the cavalry ahead **to see** in which direction the enemy **are marching**." |
| Find the two subjunctives. Which jobs, which team? | ***Videant***: a sending *quī* (a goal, Chapter 51). ***Faciant***: a tucked-in question. Both **present**: the now team! |
| Same writer, same book, two teams. Which is right? | **Both.** After a historic present, writers go either way: the verb looks like "now" (now team) but tells a story (past team). Chapter 52's promise, paid: that's the fine print. |
| Your own rule of thumb? | When **you** tell a story in the present, match it with present subjunctives: it's simplest. When you **read**, expect either. |

## 9. Results break free

:: Ārdēbat autem cupiditāte sīc, ut in nūllō umquam flagrantius studium vīderim. || And he burned with desire so much that I have never seen keener zeal in anyone. (Cicero, *Brutus* 302)

Vīdissem? Vīderim!

{#g-09}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 52's normal result in a story: "Grandpa was so tired that he was sleeping at the table." | ***Avus tam fessus erat ut ad mēnsam dormīret.*** Past team, same time. |
| Cicero remembers his great rival in the law courts, Hortensius (*Brutus* 302): *Ārdēbat autem cupiditāte sīc, ut in nūllō umquam flagrantius studium vīderim.* (*Ārdeō*: burn; *cupiditās*: desire, here for speaking; *sīc … ut*: so … that; *in nūllō*: in no one; *flagrantius*: more burning; *studium*: zeal.) Translate. | "He burned with desire (for speaking) **so** much **that I have never seen** a more burning zeal in anyone." |
| *Ārdēbat* is past team. Why ***vīderim***, a now-team form, not *vīdissem*? | A result is a **fact**, and this one is still true as Cicero writes: he **has** never seen the like, up to today. A result may keep its own time and take the **perfect** subjunctive, even after a past verb. |
| Try it: "Grandpa slept so well that he snored all night." (*Stertō, stertuī*, Chapter 17.) | ***Avus tam bene dormīvit ut tōtam noctem stertuerit.*** The perfect stresses that it really **happened**. (*… ut stertēret*, by the rule, is right too.) |
| Do goals and tucked-in questions get this freedom? | **No.** Only results, because only results report facts. A goal hasn't happened; a tucked-in question follows the rule. |

!!! success "The Law of the Free Result"
    A **result** clause (*tam/ita/sīc … ut*) normally matches like everything else. But after a past main verb it may take the **perfect** subjunctive, to stress that the result really happened or is still true: *Ārdēbat sīc, ut … studium vīderim.* Goals and tucked-in questions never break the rule.

## 10. When the main verb is a subjunctive

:: Utinam scīrem ubi esset! || If only I knew where it was! (teaching line)

Eāmus… utinam scīrem…

{#g-10}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| The main verb can be a subjunctive itself. "Let's go to the forum to buy a cheese." (Chapter 49's *Eāmus*.) | ***Eāmus ad forum ut cāseum emāmus.*** A present subjunctive main verb plays on the now team. |
| Chapter 52's "not really" wish: *Utinam iuvenis essem!* Now "If only I knew where the cheese is!" (*Sciō, scīre*.) | ***Utinam scīrem ubi cāseus esset!*** *Scīrem* is an imperfect subjunctive, a past-team form, so ***esset***, even though the wish is about **now**. |
| So does the meaning or the form pick the team? | Here, **the form**. Imperfect and pluperfect subjunctives lead the past team, whatever time they mean. (English does the same: "If only I knew where it **was**.") |
| "If only I had known what you were going to do!" | ***Utinam scīvissem quid factūrus essēs!*** Pluperfect wish (Chapter 53), past team, "later" box. |

## 11. *Iōannēs est nōmen eius*

:: Iōannēs est nōmen eius. || John is his name. (Luke 1:63)

Quis? Quālis? Quem?

{#g-11}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Luke 1:29. The angel greets Mary, and *ipsa autem turbāta est in sermōne eius et cōgitābat quālis esset ista salūtātiō.* (*Turbātus*: troubled; *sermō*: words, speech; *cōgitō*: think, wonder; *quālis*: what kind of; *salūtātiō*: greeting.) Translate. | "But she was troubled at his words and **wondered what kind of greeting this was**." Past team, same time: ***esset***. |
| Luke 1:59–62. Old Zechariah has been struck mute. The neighbors want to name his newborn son after him; his wife says the boy will be called John. So *innuēbant autem patrī eius quem vellet vocārī eum.* (*Innuō*: nod, make signs; *vocārī*: to be called.) Translate. | "So they made signs to his father (to ask) **what he wanted him to be called**." *Innuēbant*: past team; *vellet*: same time. |
| Luke 1:63: *Et postulāns pugillārem scrīpsit dīcēns: "Iōannēs est nōmen eius."* (*Postulō*: ask for; *pugillāris*: a writing tablet, a little wax tablet like the ones in this book.) Translate. | "And asking for a writing tablet, he wrote: '**John is his name**.'" Your name, Iōannēs, in the Gospel's Latin. (The Nova Vulgata spells it *Ioannes*; other editions have *Iohannes*.) |
| Tell verse 62 in the present: "They make signs to his father (asking) what he wants him to be called." | ***Innuunt patrī eius quem velit vocārī eum.*** Now team: ***velit***. |

## 12. The whole table

:: Prīmum quaere tempus! || First look for the time! (teaching line)

Faciat · fēcerit · factūrus sit · faceret · fēcisset · factūrus esset.

{#g-12}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Grandma asks where the cheese **is** · **was** · **will be**." | ***Avia rogat ubi cāseus sit · fuerit · futūrus sit.*** |
| "Grandma asked where the cheese **was** · **had been** · **would be**." | ***Avia rogāvit ubi cāseus esset · fuisset · futūrus esset.*** |
| Two questions pick the form. What are they? | **Which team is the main verb on?** And **is the subjunctive's action at the same time, earlier, or later?** |

!!! success "The Law of the Matching Rule"
    The **sequence of tenses**, all in one table.

    | Main verb | Same time | Earlier | Later |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | **now team**: present, future, future perfect, commands, present or perfect subjunctive; a perfect meaning "have done" | *faciat* | *fēcerit* | *factūrus sit* |
    | **past team**: imperfect, perfect "did," pluperfect, imperfect or pluperfect subjunctive | *faceret* | *fēcisset* | *factūrus esset* |

    Fine print: goals use only "same time." A **historic present** may lead either team. A **result** may take the perfect subjunctive after a past verb.

## 15. Lectiō: *Avus nārrat*

:: Nārrābō tibi quid factum sit. || I'll tell you what happened. (teaching line)

Quid factum est? Audī!

Diē vīcēsimā secundā: the day after Chapter 53. Yesterday the big cheese turned up in Grandpa's box, and within the hour most of it was in Grandpa. Today there's no cheese in the house. Read each Latin line aloud. Guess the English, then tap to check. For the reverse drill, tap **Tege Latīna** and rebuild each line from the English. Sort every subjunctive into its team. New in passing: *vīcēsimus secundus*, twenty-second; *ōrdō*, order; *hahae!*, ha ha!; *hem*, hmm; *ostendō*, show; *quamdiū*, how long. Old friends: *mercātor*, merchant (Chapter 51); *dōnō*, give as a gift (Chapter 37); *grātiās agō*, I thank (Chapter 11).

::: dialogue lectio
Iōannēs | Diē vīcēsimā secundā nūllus cāseus domī est. Avia Avum et mē ad forum mittit, ut novum cāseum emāmus. || John | On the twenty-second day there's no cheese in the house. Grandma sends Grandpa and me to the forum to buy a new cheese.
Avia | Iōannēs, Avum spectā, nē cāseum in viā edat! || Grandma | John, watch Grandpa, so he doesn't eat the cheese on the road!
Iōannēs | Ad mercātōrem venīmus, quī puerīs cāseum magnum vendidit. || John | We come to the merchant who sold the boys the big cheese.
Mercātor | Salvē, senex! Num cāseum meum iam ēdistis? || Merchant | Hello, old man! You haven't already eaten my cheese, have you?
Avus | Ēheu, ēdimus! Sed nārrābō tibi quid factum sit. || Grandpa | Alas, we have! But I'll tell you what happened.
Mercātor | Nārrā! Audīre volō quid factum sit. || Merchant | Tell me! I want to hear what happened.
Avus | Herī Avia cāseum cēlāvit, nē ego eum ederem. || Grandpa | Yesterday Grandma hid the cheese, so I wouldn't eat it.
Avus | Sed tum nesciēbat ubi eum posuisset! || Grandpa | But then she didn't know where she'd put it!
Iōannēs | Avus bene nārrat: ōrdinem temporum servat. || John | Grandpa tells it well: he keeps his tenses in order.
Avus | Omnēs rogābant quis cāseum cēpisset. Mē rogābant num nocte surrēxissem! || Grandpa | Everyone was asking who had taken the cheese. They were asking me whether I had got up in the night!
Mercātor | Et surrēxerās? || Merchant | And had you got up?
Avus | Minimē! Tam bene dormīvī ut tōtam noctem stertuerim! || Grandpa | Not at all! I slept so well that I snored all night!
Iōannēs | Hoc vērum est. || John | That's true.
Avus | Tum, audī: canis surgit, ad capsam meam currit, lātrat! || Grandpa | Then, listen: the dog gets up, runs to my box, barks!
Mercātor | Cūr nunc "currit" dīcis, nōn "cucurrit"? || Merchant | Why are you saying "runs" now, not "ran"?
Iōannēs | Avus "currit" dīcit, ut omnia iterum videāmus. || John | Grandpa says "runs" so that we see it all again.
Avus | Capsam aperīmus, et ecce: Avia cāseum in capsā meā cēlāverat! || Grandpa | We open the box, and look: Grandma had hidden the cheese in my box!
Mercātor | Et ubi nunc est cāseus? || Merchant | And where's the cheese now?
Avus | Avia mihi partem dedit, ut fēlīx essem… || Grandpa | Grandma gave me a piece, so I'd be happy…
Iōannēs | …et post hōram Avia rogāvit ubi cāseus esset. Omnēs sciēbant ubi esset: in stomachō Avī! || John | …and an hour later Grandma asked where the cheese was. Everyone knew where it was: in Grandpa's stomach!
Mercātor | Hahae! Tam bene nārrāvistī ut tibi parvum cāseum dōnem! || Merchant | Ha ha! You've told it so well that I'm giving you a small cheese!
Avus | Grātiās tibi agō! Et magnum emimus, prō familiā. || Grandpa | Thank you! And we're buying a big one, for the family.
Iōannēs | Domum redīmus. Ego magnum cāseum portō, nē Avus eum tangat. || John | We go back home. I carry the big cheese, so Grandpa doesn't touch it.
Avia | Tandem! Cūr tam sērō venītis? Quid fēcistis? || Grandma | At last! Why are you so late? What did you do?
Iōannēs | Avus mercātōrī nārrāvit quid herī factum esset. Tam bene nārrāvit ut mercātor eī parvum cāseum dōnāverit! || John | Grandpa told the merchant what had happened yesterday. He told it so well that the merchant gave him a small cheese!
Avia | Parvum cāseum? Ubi est? Ostende mihi! || Grandma | A small cheese? Where is it? Show me!
Avus | Hem… nesciō ubi sit. || Grandpa | Hmm… I don't know where it is.
Avia | Ave! Iterum? || Grandma | Grandpa! Again?
Avus | Nōn ēdī! Rogā canem quid eī dederim! || Grandpa | I didn't eat it! Ask the dog what I gave him!
Iōannēs | Canis sub mēnsā sedet, laetissimus. || John | The dog is sitting under the table, very happy indeed.
Avus | Herī ille cāseum invēnit. Itaque hodiē eī parvum dedī, ut grātiās agerem. || Grandpa | Yesterday he found the cheese. So today I gave him the small one, to thank him.
Avia | Ō Ave! Tam bonus fuistī ut tibi ōsculum dem. || Grandma | Oh, Grandpa! You've been so good that I'm giving you a kiss.
Avus | Et tū mihi partem cāseī magnī? Dō ut dēs! || Grandpa | And you'll give me a piece of the big cheese? I give so that you may give!
Avia | Nē tetigerīs! || Grandma | Don't touch it!
Iōannēs | Cāseus magnus in mēnsā manet. Nesciō quamdiū mānsūrus sit… || John | The big cheese stays on the table. I don't know how long it's going to stay…
:::

{#g-15}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Grandpa's story opens: *Avia cāseum cēlāvit, nē ego eum ederem · nesciēbat ubi eum posuisset.* Team and time for each? | Both **past team**. ***Ederem***: a goal, same time. ***Posuisset***: a tucked-in question, earlier. |
| Grandma at the start says *nē cāseum in viā **edat***; Grandpa's story says *nē ego eum **ederem***. Why the difference? | Grandma gives a **command** (*spectā!*): now team, present. Grandpa tells a **story** (*cēlāvit*): past team, imperfect. |
| Find Grandpa's historic present, and say why John says he uses it. | ***Surgit, currit, lātrat, aperīmus.*** *Ut omnia iterum videāmus*: so that we see it all again. |
| Find two "have done" verbs that take the present, and two free results. | "Have done": ***nārrāvistī … ut dōnem*** (merchant), ***fuistī … ut dem*** (Grandma). Free results: ***dormīvī … ut stertuerim*** (Grandpa), ***nārrāvit … ut dōnāverit*** (John). |
| Where is the small cheese, and why? | In the **dog**. Grandpa gave it to him *ut grātiās agerem*, "to thank him," for finding the big cheese yesterday. And the last line's *mānsūrus sit*? "Later" after a now verb. |

## Speak it aloud

:: Loquāmur Latīnē! || Let's speak Latin! (teaching line)

1. In your own words: what is the sequence of tenses (the matching rule), and who picks the time?
2. Name the two teams, with three main verbs each, and the two subjunctives each team takes.
3. Say one sentence six ways: *Avia rogat / rogāvit ubi cāseus sit, fuerit, futūrus sit / esset, fuisset, futūrus esset.*
4. Make two goals: one now (*Eō ut…*), one in a story (*Herī iī ut…*).
5. Make two "have done" sentences with the present subjunctive (*Ēmī ut edāmus*).
6. Tell a three-line story in the historic present with one goal, then tell it again in the past.
7. The four Laws in your own words: the Two Teams, "Have Done," the Free Result, the Matching Rule.
8. The real lines: *ego vēnī ut vītam habeant · Quam autem cīvitātī cārus fuerit, maerōre fūneris indicātum est · Labiēnus … litterās Caesarī remittit, quantō cum perīculō legiōnem ex hībernīs ēductūrus esset · equitātumque omnem … praemittit, quī videant quās in partēs hostēs iter faciant · Ārdēbat autem cupiditāte sīc, ut in nūllō umquam flagrantius studium vīderim · cōgitābat quālis esset ista salūtātiō · innuēbant autem patrī eius quem vellet vocārī eum · Iōannēs est nōmen eius.* Then read the *Lectiō* aloud, playing every part, and rebuild it from the English.

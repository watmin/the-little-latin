# Caput XXIV: Recapitulātiō II

*Liber II, Rēs Cotīdiānae · Chapter 24*

*In which nothing is new and every daily thing comes back. The third family, the two futures, can and want, the pointing words, the third-family adjectives, looking-back pronouns, numbers and when, and the last two noun families all sit at one table. Then the cheese family holds a feast: this cheese or that, how many, whose hands, which day — and at last, cheese in every stomach.*

!!! abstract "In this chapter"
    - **Saying:** *Possum!* · *Hunc an illum?* · *Suum cuique.* · *Carpe diem.* · *Manus manum lavat.* · *Rēs pūblica.* · *Nōn omnia possumus omnēs.*
    - **Grammar words, in plain English:** nothing new. This is a **recapitulātiō**, a "re-heading": going back over the main points (English "recap")
    - **Forms:** third-family nouns (consonant, neuter, i-stem); imperfect and future of all four verb families; *possum / volō / nōlō*; *hic / ille / iste / is / īdem*; third-family adjectives; *ego–vōs*, *sē / suus*, *ipse*; numerals and *diē / hōrā*; fourth and fifth families; five-family badges
    - **Laws:** all of Book II's, side by side: Stem, Double Duty, Disguise, Neuter Twins, Little i, Sea, Time Signs, Clock, Two Futures, *Pot-*, Completing Infinitive, Pointing Words, Near and Far, Two Families, Three Shapes, Looking Back, Part, When, Long ū, Day, Five Families
    - **Words:** three new glue words for the feast: again, almost, finally
    - **Reading:** *Cāseus omnibus* (a Liber II feast in three scenes)
    - **Speaking:** sew every Liber II Law into one short story of your own

    Same as always. Say your answer **out loud**, then tap the wax tablet to check. This chapter goes fast on purpose: if a group feels shaky, its answers name the chapter to go back to. The last sections are the real exam: the feast and *Quid didicistī?*

## 1. *Rēx, corpus, ovis* — the third family

:: Nōn multa, sed multum. || Not many things, but much. (after Pliny the Younger; Chapter 12)

Rēx, rēgis. Corpus, corporis. Ovis, ovis. Mare, maris.

{#g-01}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Name the third family's first rule. How do you find the stem of *rēx*? | The Law of the Stem (Chapter 13): the genitive *-is* shows the stem. *Rēgis* → stem *rēg-*. |
| Decline *rēx* singular; then plural of *mīles* (watch Double Duty). | *Rēx, rēgis, rēgī, rēgem, rēge.* · *Mīlitēs, mīlitum, mīlitibus, mīlitēs, mīlitibus.* *-ēs* subject **and** object; *-ibus* to/for **and** with/from. |
| *Nōmen, nōminis* and *corpus, corporis*: family, gender, plural subjects? | Third-family **neuters** (Chapter 14). Neuter Twins: naming = object. Plurals *nōmina, corpora* (*-a*). Disguise: *-us, -oris* = neuter, almost always. |
| *Ovis, ovis* vs *mīles, mīlitis*: i-stem clue? Genitive plurals of *ovis, urbs, canis*? | Parisyllabic *-is* → ***-ium*** (Law of the Little i, Chapter 15). *Ovium* (equal syllables); *urbium* (stem ends in two consonants); *canum* (rebel). |
| Neuter i-stems *mare, animal*: abl. sg., plural subjects, of many? | Law of the Sea: *-ī, -ia, -ium*. *In marī · maria.* Of many: ***animālium*** (genitive plural of *mare* is rare). |
| "I give cheese to Father. I see Mother. With John." | *Patrī cāseum dō. Mātrem videō. Cum Iōanne.* (Chapter 13.) |
| Full third-family mental map: consonant / neuter / i-stem / neuter i-stem — one example each. | *Rēx* · *corpus* · *ovis* (or *urbs*) · *mare* (or *animal*). |
| *Caput mihi dolet* — what case is *mihi*, and why does it look like *nōmen mihi est*? | Dative: "to me" (pain / name). Same pattern as Chapter 11 / 14. |


## 2. *Heri cantābam, crās legam* — the clock

:: Dabit deus hīs quoque fīnem. || God will give an end to these things too. (Vergil, *Aeneid* 1.199; Chapter 16)

Heri ambulābam. Hodiē ambulō. Crās ambulābō. Crās legam.

{#g-02}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What does a **tense** name? Name the three you've met. | The time of the verb (*tempus*). Present · imperfect (was …ing / used to) · future (will). Perfect waits for Book III. |
| Chant *amō* imperfect, then future. Time Signs for 1st/2nd? | *Amābam…amābant.* · *Amābō…amābunt.* No sign = present; ***-bā-*** imperfect ("I" *-m*); ***-bi-*** future (*-bō/-bunt*). |
| *Sum* and *eō* imperfect / future? Why not *legābō*? | *Eram… / erō…* (no *b*). *Ībam… / ībō…* Third family: *legēbam* (long ē + *-bam*); future is another set. |
| Chant *legō* future; then *audiō* and *capiō*. Law of the Two Futures? | *Legam, legēs, leget, legēmus, legētis, legent.* · *Audiam…* · *Capiam…* (keep i). *-āre/-ēre* → *bō, bi, bu*; *-ere/-īre/-iō* → *am, ēs, et…*; rebels *ībō, erō*. |
| Traps: *legit/leget*; *habēs/legēs*; fix *Heri cantābō* and *Crās legēbam*. | Present vs future; 2nd-conj present vs 3rd-conj future. *Heri cantābam. Crās legam.* Law of the Clock. |
| "Yesterday Grandpa was sleeping. Tomorrow we will go to Rome." | *Heri Avus dormiēbat. Crās Rōmam ībimus.* |
| Success check: present / imperfect / future of *legō* in the "I" form. | *Legō · legēbam · legam.* The *b* marks the past; no *b* in the third-family future. |


## 3. *Possum! Volō! Nōlō!*

:: Nōn omnia possumus omnēs. || We cannot all do everything. (Vergil, *Eclogues* 8.63; Chapter 18)

Possum legere. Vīsne cāseum? Nōlō exspectāre!

{#g-03}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Possum* = ? Chant present, imperfect, future. | *Pot-* + *sum*. *Possum, potes, potest, possumus, potestis, possunt.* · *Poteram…* · *Poterō…* |
| What's a **complementary infinitive**? | The infinitive that **completes** a verb: *possum **legere***, "I can read." |
| Chant *volō* and *nōlō*. Where is *volō* wild? How does *nōlī* fit? | *Volō, vīs, vult, volumus, vultis, volunt · velle* (wild *vīs, vult, vultis*). *Nōlō… nōlunt · nōlle.* *Nōlī/nōlīte* = *nōlō*'s command (Chapter 6). |
| One-word answers; then "I want to buy cheese. Y'all cannot run. Don't wait!" | *Possum! Volō! Nōlō!* · *Cāseum emere volō. Currere nōn potestis. Nōlīte exspectāre!* |
| Traps: *Possum legō*; *volant* vs *volunt*. Motto *Nōn omnia possumus omnēs.* | Need infinitive *legere*. *Volant* = fly; *volunt* = want. "We cannot all do everything." |
| *Esse* and *posse* — where did you finally meet "to be"? | Chapter 18: *esse* taught with *posse* (*pot-* + *esse*). |


## 4. *Hic, ille, iste, is, īdem*

:: Hīc et nunc. || Here and now. (Chapter 19; watch the long ī)

Hic liber. Ille cāseus. Iste stomachus. Is venit. Īdem semper sum.

{#g-04}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is a **demonstrative**? *Hic, haec, hoc* for book / table / body. | A pointing word. *Hic liber · haec mēnsa · hoc corpus.* |
| Trap ***hic*** vs ***hīc***? Surprise genitive and dative of *hic*? | *Hic* = this; *hīc* = here. *Huius* · *huic* (one syllable). Law of the Pointing Words (*-īus / -ī*). |
| *Ille* odd bits? Law of Near and Far? | *-e / -ud*; *illīus, illī*. *Hic* near me · *iste* near you · *ille* over there · *is* points back. |
| *Is, ea, id*: him/her/it; genitive? *Īdem*? | *Eum / eam / eum* · *eius* (someone else's). *Īdem, eadem, idem* = the same (*eundem*). |
| "This cheese or that? I want the same!" Fix *Hic mālum est.* | *Hunc cāseum an illum vīs? Eundem volō!* · *Hoc mālum est.* |
| Decode *Hoc erat in prīncipiō* and *In hōc signō vincēs* (recognition). | *Hoc* neuter "this" · *hōc* ablative "in this (sign)." Vowel length + company. |


## 5. *Fortis, ācer, fēlīx* — adjectives across families

:: Fortēs fortūna adiuvat. || Fortune helps the brave. (after Terence; Chapter 20)

Rēx bonus. Fēmina fortis. Cāseus ācer. Ō fēlīx culpa!

{#g-05}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Law of the Two Families? Example *rēx bonus / fēmina fortis*. | Share gender, number, case — not letters. *Rēgis bonī* (not *bonis*). *Fēminae fortis.* |
| *Fortis, forte*: naming forms, plurals, abl. sg.? Three Shapes? | Two-ending; *-ēs/-ia*; abl. always ***-ī***. Shapes: 3 (*ācer*), 2 (*fortis*), 1 (*fēlīx, fēlīcis*) — then all like *fortis*. |
| *Ācer* vs *celer*? "With a brave father; brave bodies; of brave soldiers." | *Ācer* drops e; *celer* keeps e. *Cum patre fortī. Corpora fortia. Mīlitum fortium.* |
| Traps: *Fēmina forta; cum patre forte; mālum dulcis.* Motto *Fortēs fortūna adiuvat.* | *Fortis; fortī; dulce.* "Fortune helps the brave" (*fortēs* = brave people). |
| *Omnia mea mēcum portō* — sort *omnia*. | Neuter plural "all (things)": *omnis* payoff (Chapters 15/18/20). |
| *Quālis est?* — ask and answer about Avia. | *Quālis est Avia? Avia ācris / sapiēns est.* (Chapter 20.) |


## 6. *Ego, sē, ipse* — looking back

:: Nōsce tē. || Know yourself. (after Cicero; Chapter 21)

Ego mē lavō. Avus sē lavat. Ipse dīxit. Suum cuique.

{#g-06}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chant *ego* and *tū* singular; then *nōs / vōs* with *-cum*. | *Ego, meī, mihi, mē, mēcum.* · *Tū, tuī, tibi, tē, tēcum.* · *Nōbīscum / vōbīscum.* |
| What is a **reflexive**? Forms of *sē*? *Avus sē lavat* vs *eum lavat*? | Points back to the subject (no nominative): *suī, sibi, sē, sēcum.* Himself · him (someone else). |
| *Suus* vs *eius*? What is an **intensive** (*ipse*)? | *Suus* = his/her own (of the subject); *eius* = someone else's. *Ipse* stresses / emphasizes ("himself / the very") — not the same as *sē*. |
| "I wash myself. Grandpa washes himself. We wash him." Traps: *Ego sē lavō; cum nōbīs.* | *Ego mē lavō. Avus sē lavat. Nōs eum lavāmus.* · *Mē*; *nōbīscum.* |
| Mottos: *Suum cuique*; *Nōsce tē* (and *tē ipsum*). | "To each his own." · "Know yourself" (*ipsum* intensifies). |
| Law of Looking Back in one line. | If it points back to the subject, use *sē / suus*; if it points to someone else, use *eum / eius*. |


## 7. *Ūnus, duo, trēs* — parts and when

:: Carpe diem. || Seize the day. (Horace; Chapters 22–23)

Ūnus nauta. Duo nostrum. Hōrā tertiā. Quantī cāseus?

{#g-07}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Ūnus* surprise endings? *Duo* and *trēs* in brief? | Gen. *ūnīus*, dat. *ūnī*. *Duo, duae, duo* · *trēs, tria* (with *duōbus, tribus*…). |
| 4–10, *centum*, *mille/mīlia*? Law of the Part? | 4–10 and *centum* indeclinable; *mīlia* + gen. Partitive genitive: *duo nostrum · pars cāseī · satis pecūniae.* |
| *Quot?* vs *quantī?* Law of When? | How many? · for how much? (indefinite price gen.). Bare ablative when: *hōrā tertiā · diē tertiā · nocte.* |
| "Four cheeses for one dēnārius, at the third hour." Ordinals 1st–5th. | *Quattuor cāseī ūnō dēnāriō, hōrā tertiā.* · *Prīmus…quīntus.* |
| Trap: *In diem tertium veniō* for "on the third day." Motto *Pecūnia nōn olet.* | *Diē tertiā.* "Money doesn't smell." |
| *Ūna hirundō nōn facit vēr* — why *ūna*? | *Hirundō* is feminine; *ūnus* agrees: one swallow does not make spring. |


## 8. *Manus, diēs, rēs* — five families

:: Manus manum lavat. || One hand washes the other. (Petronius; Chapter 23)

Manūs meae. Diē hodiērnō. Quid rēī est? Puellae, hortī, rēgis, manūs, diēī.

{#g-08}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Fourth family: chant *manus* sg. + pl. Gender? | *Manus, manūs, manuī, manum, manū* · *manūs, manuum, manibus, manūs, manibus.* Feminine despite *-us.* Law of the Long ū. |
| *Cornū*? Fifth: chant *diēs* and *rēs* singular. *Reī* vs *diēī*? | Neuter 4th: *cornū* / pl. *cornua*. *Diēs, diēī, diēī, diem, diē.* · *Rēs, reī, reī, rem, rē.* Short *e* in *reī*; long *ē* in *diēī*. |
| Law of Five Families — badges and abl. sg. map? | *-ae, -ī, -is, -ūs, -ēī/-eī.* Abl.: *-ā, -ō, -e/-ī, -ū, -ē.* |
| Crack: *Manus manum lavat · Carpe diem · Rēs pūblica · Nūlla diēs sine līneā.* | One hand washes the other · seize the day · the republic · no day without a line. |
| "In Grandpa's hands, today, a big matter." Traps: *Manī; rem pūblicum; diēī longus est.* | *In manibus Avī, diē hodiērnō, rēs magna.* · *Manū; rēs pūblica; diēs longus est.* |
| *Domus* reminder from Chapter 23? | Keep *domum / domī / domō*; mixed 2nd/4th house; feminine. |


## 9. Lectiō: *Cāseus omnibus*

:: Tandem cāseus omnibus! || At last — cheese for everyone!

Three scenes: *Heri*, *Hodiē ad tabernam*, *Denique*. Read each Latin line aloud. Guess the English, then tap to check. For the reverse drill, tap **Tege Latīna** and rebuild each line from the English. New glue words: *rūrsus*, again; *ferē*, almost; *denique*, finally / at last (stronger than Chapter 12's *tandem*).

### I. *Heri*

::: dialogue lectio-1
Avia | Heri quattuor cāseōs emēbāmus. Ubi sunt? || Grandma | Yesterday we were buying four cheeses. Where are they?
Avus | Ūnus in stomachō canis erat… || Grandpa | One was in the dog's stomach…
Marcus | Et ego rūrsus manūs lavō! || Marcus | And I'm washing my hands again!
Cornēlia | Sed ōs tuum ferē semper olet, Marce! || Cornēlia | But your mouth almost always smells, Marcus!
Quīntus | Ego cāseum nōn edēbam! Ego crustula volēbam! || Quīntus | I wasn't eating cheese! I wanted cookies!
Iōannēs | Possumusne hodiē cāseum novum emere? || John | Can we buy new cheese today?
Pater | Possumus. Sed quantī? || Father | We can. But for how much?
Māter | Hōrā tertiā ad tabernam ībimus. || Mother | At the third hour we'll go to the shop.
Avus | Ego quoque veniam! Exspectāre nōlō! || Grandpa | I'll come too! I don't want to wait!
Cāseus | Bau! || Cheese (the dog) | Woof!
Avia | Canis, tū nōn veniēs. Domī manēbis! || Grandma | Dog, you will not come. You'll stay home!
:::

### II. *Hodiē ad tabernam*

::: dialogue lectio-2
Tabernārius | Salvēte! Hunc cāseum an illum vultis? || Shopkeeper | Hello, y'all! Do you want this cheese or that one?
Avia | Illum magnum volō — et hunc parvum ācrem. || Grandma | I want that big one — and this small sharp one.
Tabernārius | Duōbus dēnāriīs. || Shopkeeper | For two dēnāriī.
Pater | Nimis! Ūnō dēnāriō! || Father | Too much! For one dēnārius!
Tabernārius | Bene. Sēstertiīs quattuor — id est, ūnō dēnāriō. || Shopkeeper | Fine. For four sēstertiī — that is, one dēnārius.
Quīntus | Et crustula nōbīs? || Quīntus | And cookies for us?
Māter | Nōlī, Quīnte. Cāseus familiae est. || Mother | Don't, Quīntus. The cheese is the family's.
Iōannēs | Ecce pecūnia. Grātiās tibi agō! || John | Here's the money. Thank you!
Marcus | Iste cāseus ācer est! || Marcus | That cheese of yours is sharp!
Tabernārius | Nōn iste — hic! Meus est hic; vester erit ille. || Shopkeeper | Not "that of yours" — this! This one is mine; that one will be yours.
Cornēlia | Fortēs sumus. Age, domum īmus! || Cornēlia | We're brave. Come on, we're going home!
Avus | Ambulāre iam nōn possum. Sed cāseum portāre possum! || Grandpa | I can't walk anymore. But I can carry cheese!
:::

### III. *Denique*

::: dialogue lectio-3
Avia | Domī sumus! Manūs lavāte, līberī! || Grandma | We're home! Wash your hands, kids!
Marcus | Ego mē lavō. || Marcus | I'm washing myself.
Quīntus | Ego quoque mē lavō… || Quīntus | I'm washing myself too…
Cornēlia | Et canis sē… nōn lavat. || Cornēlia | And the dog is not washing himself.
Avus | Nunc: quis cāseum suum vult? || Grandpa | Now: who wants his own cheese?
Iōannēs | Ego meum volō! || John | I want mine!
Avia | Suum cuique — et canī suum! || Grandma | To each his own — and to the dog his own!
Cāseus | Bau! Bau! || Cheese (the dog) | Woof! Woof!
Pater | Diē hodiērnō rēs bona est. || Father | Today the matter is good.
Māter | Liber Secundus ferē tuus est, Iōannēs. || Mother | Book Two is almost yours, John.
Iōannēs | Latīnē legō et scrībō… et cāseum edō! || John | I read and write in Latin… and I eat cheese!
Omnēs | In stomachīs… || Everyone | In the stomachs of…
Avus | …omnium! Denique! Nōn sōlum Avī, nōn sōlum canis — omnium! || Grandpa | …everyone! At last! Not only Grandpa's, not only the dog's — everyone's!
Quīntus | Crāsne rūrsus Rōmam ībimus? || Quīntus | Tomorrow are we going to Rome again?
Pater | Minimē! Crās domī manēbimus. Sed cāseum rūrsus edēmus! || Father | No way! Tomorrow we'll stay home. But we'll eat cheese again!
:::

{#g-09}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Scene I: find imperfect, future, and *possum / volō / nōlō*. | *Emēbāmus, edēbam, volēbam.* · *Ībimus, veniam, veniēs, manēbis.* · *Possumusne…emere? Exspectāre nōlō.* |
| *Rūrsus · ferē · denique* in the story? | Again (washing) · almost (smells; Book II almost yours) · finally (cheese in everyone's stomach). |
| *Hunc an illum? · Iste cāseus* — Near and Far? | *Hunc/illum* near me / over there. *Iste* = that of yours (Marcus; shopkeeper corrects to *hic/ille*). |
| Prices and when: *duōbus dēnāriīs · ūnō dēnāriō · hōrā tertiā*. | Ablative of definite price; ablative of time (Chapter 22). |
| *Manūs lavāte · Ego mē lavō · canis sē* · *Suum cuique — et canī suum!* | Y'all command · reflexive *mē* · *sē* for the dog · Chapter 21 motto paid off for everyone, even the dog. |
| *Diē hodiērnō rēs bona est.* End gag vs Chapter 23? | Time abl. + *rēs* + agreement. Ch23: dog's stomach; today: *in stomachīs omnium* — Liber II's cheese arc closes. |
| Untaught / recycled? | *Id est* Ch19; *nimis, iam nōn* Ch18; *tabernārius* Ch12; *age* encouragement chunk; *nōn sōlum* from Ch22 *sōlus*. |
| *Liber Secundus ferē tuus est* — who says it, and what's *ferē*? | Mother to John: "Book Two is almost yours." New glue word. |

## Speak it aloud

:: Dīcendō discimus. || We learn by speaking.

1. Decline *rēx*, *nōmen*, *ovis*, *mare* through the cases you know; then *manus*, *diēs*, *rēs*.
2. Chant three tenses of *amō* and of *legō* (present / imperfect / future). Then *possum*, *volō*, *nōlō*.
3. Point around the room: *hic / haec / hoc · ille / illa / illud · is / ea / id*. Add *īdem*.
4. Describe your family with third-family adjectives: *fortis, celer, ācer, brevis, sapiēns*.
5. Reflexive drill: *Ego mē lavō · Tū tē lavās · Avus sē lavat · Nōs nōs lavāmus*.
6. Numbers and when: count 1–10; say *hōrā tertiā · diē hesternō · quantī? · duo nostrum*.
7. Five-family badges out loud: *puellae, hortī, rēgis, manūs, diēī*.
8. Read the *Lectiō* aloud, all three scenes, playing every part. Then hide the Latin and rebuild it.

# Caput LIII: Rogō Quid Faciās

*Liber V, Modus Subiūnctīvus · Chapter 53*

*In which questions learn to hide inside sentences, and the subjunctive learns to look further back. Three old promises come due today. Chapter 49 found Horace's* sit *in* Quid sit futūrum crās, fuge quaerere *and said: "Why a question hidden inside a sentence wants it: Chapter 53." The same chapter found Catullus's* faciam *in* Quārē id faciam, fortasse requīris *and said: "That job is Chapter 53's." And Chapter 47's* Nē timuerītis *was "explained in Chapter 53." Here are all three. Today: the tucked-in question ("I ask* what you're doing*"); two last sets of subjunctive forms, for things that happened* earlier*; wishes about the past that can never come true; and classical prose's other "don't." Along the way: Horace twice, Catullus in love, Cicero thundering at Catiline, Jesus on the wind and on a mother's request, the Israelites in the desert, and a healed man telling everyone what was done for him. Speaking goal: ask what someone is doing, say you don't know where something is, and tell what you asked and what you found out.*

!!! abstract "In this chapter"
    - **Saying:** *Rogō quid faciās · Nesciō ubi sit · Dīc mihi quid fēcerīs · Rogāvī quid facerēs · Utinam nē ēdissem! · Nē timuerīs!*
    - **Grammar words, in plain English:** direct question (a question asked straight out) · indirect question (the "tucked-in" question) · perfect subjunctive (the "*-erim*" subjunctive) · pluperfect subjunctive (the "perfect infinitive + ending" subjunctive)
    - **Forms:** perfect subjunctive *amāverim, fuerim, fēcerim; amātus sim, locūtus sim* · pluperfect subjunctive *amāvissem, fuissem, fēcissem; amātus essem, mortuus essem*
    - **Laws:** the Law of the Tucked-In Question · the Law of *-erim* · the Law of the Infinitive Plus, Again · the Law of Now and Earlier
    - **Words:** *num, -ne* (new job: "whether") · *an* (or, in a question) · *cēlō*
    - **Reading:** *Ubi est cāseus?* (day twenty-one: the big cheese vanishes, Grandpa is innocent for once, and Grandma forgets where she hid it)
    - **Speaking:** ask what someone is doing or did; say you don't know where, who, or why; tell what you asked yesterday; wish you hadn't

!!! tip "How to read this chapter"
    Same as always. Say your answer **out loud**, then tap the wax tablet to check. Keep four chants close: Chapter 49's present subjunctive (*amem, habeam, dūcam*), Chapter 52's *amārem, essem*, Chapter 25's perfect *amāvī, fuī, fēcī*, and Chapter 41's perfect infinitive *amāvisse, fuisse*. Today's new forms are built from those last two.

## 1. A question inside a sentence

:: Rogō quid faciās. || I ask what you're doing. (teaching line; the chapter title)

Quid facis? Rogō quid faciās.

{#g-01}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 6's question: *Quid facis?* | "**What are you doing?**" Asked straight out, with its own question mark. Grammar books call that a **direct question**: a question asked straight out. |
| Now tuck it inside a sentence: "I ask what you're doing." Which word changes? | ***Rogō quid faciās.*** (*Rogō*, ask, Chapter 29.) The question word *quid* stays; the verb slides from *facis* to the subjunctive ***faciās***. |
| English didn't change "you're doing." Why does Latin? | Inside the bigger sentence the question is no longer a fact you state. It's a thing asked about, wondered about, not known. Latin marks that with the **subjunctive**. |
| Grammar books call *quid faciās* here an **indirect question**. Plain words? | **A tucked-in question**: a question that has moved inside a bigger sentence, after a verb of asking, knowing, telling, or seeing. (Old school: "indirect" or "dependent question.") |
| *Ubi est Avus?* Tuck it after *Nesciō*, "I don't know." (*Nesciō*, Chapter 6.) | ***Nesciō ubi sit Avus.*** "I don't know where Grandpa is." Fact *est* → subjunctive ***sit***. |

## 2. Every question word can hide

:: Nesciō ubi sit. || I don't know where it is. (teaching line)

Quis? Quid? Ubi? Cūr? Quandō?

{#g-02}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Quis venit?* → "Tell me who's coming." (*Dīc mihi*.) | ***Dīc mihi quis veniat.*** |
| *Cūr rīdēs?* → "I know why you're laughing." | ***Sciō cūr rīdeās.*** Even when I *know*, the tucked-in question is subjunctive. |
| *Quandō Avus expergīscētur?* → "We don't know when Grandpa will wake up." Careful: "will." | ***Nescīmus quandō Avus expergīscātur.*** The present subjunctive covers "now" and "later" both; context decides. (A sharper "later" is coming in section 12.) |
| *Quot cāseī sunt in mēnsā?* → "Count how many cheeses are on the table!" (*Numerā*.) | ***Numerā quot cāseī in mēnsā sint!*** |
| *Quōmodo valēs?* ("How are you?"; *valeō*, be well, Chapter 50.) → "Grandma asks how you're doing." | ***Avia rogat quōmodo valeās.*** |

!!! success "The Law of the Tucked-In Question"
    A **direct question** asked straight out keeps its normal verb: *Ubi est?* An **indirect question** (the tucked-in question), hanging on a verb of asking, knowing, telling, or seeing, puts its verb in the **subjunctive**: *Nesciō ubi sit.* The question word stays the same; English doesn't change, so you must.

    | Straight out | Tucked in |
    |---|---|
    | *Quid facis?* | *Rogō quid faciās.* |
    | *Ubi est Avus?* | *Nesciō ubi sit Avus.* |
    | *Cūr rīdēs?* | *Sciō cūr rīdeās.* |

## 3. Three old promises

:: Ōdī et amō. Quārē id faciam, fortasse requīris. || I hate and I love. Why I do that, perhaps you ask. (Catullus 85.1; Chapters 46 and 49)

Sit. Faciam. Petātis.

{#g-03}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Catullus 85.1: *Quārē id faciam, fortasse requīris.* Chapter 49 said *faciam* was "a question tucked inside a sentence." The straight-out question? | ***Quārē id faciō?*** "Why do I do that?" Tucked after *requīris* (you ask), *faciō* becomes ***faciam***. |
| Line 2: *Nesciō, sed fierī sentiō et excrucior.* (*Sentiō*: feel; *excrucior*: I am tortured.) | "**I don't know**, but I feel it happening, and I'm in torment." (*Fierī sentiō*: Chapter 42's accusative-and-infinitive, "I feel (it) to happen.") |
| Matthew 20:22. A mother asks Jesus to seat her two sons at his right and left in his kingdom. He answers them: *Nescītis quid petātis.* (*Petō*: ask for.) | "**Y'all don't know what you're asking for.**" *Quid petitis?* tucked after *nescītis* → ***petātis***. |
| John 3:8: *Spīritus ubi vult spīrat, et vōcem eius audīs, sed nōn scīs unde veniat et quō vādat.* (*Spīritus*: wind, spirit; *vādō*: go.) | "The wind blows where it wants, and you hear its voice, but you don't know **where it comes from** or **where it goes**." Two tucked-in questions, ***veniat*** and ***vādat***. (Nova Vulgata.) |

## 4. "Whether": *num, -ne, an*

:: Rogā Avum num cāseum ēderit. || Ask Grandpa whether he's eaten the cheese. (teaching line)

Num? -ne? An?

{#g-04}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| A yes-or-no question: *Venitne Marcus?* (Chapter 2's *-ne*.) Tuck it in: "I ask **whether** Marcus is coming." | ***Rogō num Marcus veniat*** or ***Rogō veniatne Marcus.*** Tucked in, *num* and *-ne* both just mean "**whether**." |
| Chapter 2 said *num* expects "no." Still true here? | **No.** Inside a sentence, *num* is a plain "whether," with no hint of the answer. |
| "Whether … or": *an*, "or," the word in Chapter 18's *Cāseum parvum an magnum vīs?* "I don't know whether Marcus or Quintus is coming." | ***Nesciō Marcusne an Quīntus veniat.*** *-ne* on the first choice, *an* before the second. |
| "Tell me whether you're hungry." (*Ēsuriō*, Chapter 39.) | ***Dīc mihi num ēsuriās.*** (Or *ēsuriāsne*.) |

## 5. Tucked into a past story

:: Rogāvī quid facerēs. || I asked what you were doing. (teaching line)

Rogō quid faciās… rogāvī quid facerēs.

{#g-05}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 52: a goal in a past story takes the imperfect subjunctive (*Dedī ut darēs*). Guess: "I **asked** what you **were doing**." | ***Rogāvī quid facerēs.*** Past story, same time: Chapter 52's ***facerem*** form. |
| "I didn't know where Grandpa was." | ***Nesciēbam ubi esset Avus.*** |
| "Grandma asked why we were laughing." | ***Avia rogāvit cūr rīdērēmus.*** |
| "Marcus didn't know whether the shop was open." (*Aperta*.) | ***Marcus nesciēbat num taberna aperta esset.*** |

## 6. "I know what you did"

:: Sciō quid fēcerīs. || I know what you did. (teaching line)

Fēcistī… fēcerīs.

{#g-06}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Quid fēcistī?* "What did you do?" Tuck it after *Sciō*. *Faciās* won't do: that's "what you're doing." We need a subjunctive for something done **earlier**. | ***Sciō quid fēcerīs.*** "I know what you **did** (have done)." |
| Take it apart: *fēc- + -erīs*. Where's *fēc-* from? | Chapter 25's perfect ***fēcī***: drop the *-ī*, and you have the **perfect stem**. |
| Build "I" on *amō*'s stem *amāv-*: the endings are ***-erim, -erīs, -erit, -erīmus, -erītis, -erint***. | ***Amāverim, amāverīs, amāverit, amāverīmus, amāverītis, amāverint.*** |
| Grammar books call this the **perfect subjunctive**. Plain words? | **The "*-erim*" subjunctive**: the subjunctive for something that happened **earlier** than a "now" main verb ("I know what you **did**"). |
| *Sum*: perfect *fuī*. "I don't know where you **were**." | ***Nesciō ubi fuerīs.*** |

## 7. *-erim* or *-erō*?

:: Nē timuerītis! || Don't be afraid! (Matthew 10:26; Chapter 47)

Amāverō? Amāverim?

{#g-07}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 27's future perfect: *amāverō, amāveris, amāverit, amāverimus, amāveritis, amāverint*. Line it up with today's *amāverim…*. Where do they differ? | "I": ***-erō*** vs ***-erim***. And today's *ī* is long in ***-erīs, -erīmus, -erītis***. "He" and "they" are spelled the same: *amāverit, amāverint*. |
| So *amāverit* alone is…? | **Either.** "He will have loved" or "(that) he loved." The sentence decides: after *Nesciō num…* it's the subjunctive. (Romans sometimes blurred the long and short *i* themselves; this book marks the usual lengths.) |
| Build "(that) we were" and "(that) they could." (*Fuī; potuī.*) | ***Fuerīmus*** and ***potuerint***. |
| *Dō* (perfect *dedī*), *edō* (*ēdī*), *videō* (*vīdī*): "I," "you," "he." | ***Dederim, ēderīs, vīderit.*** Every verb, every family: just the perfect stem. |

!!! success "The Law of *-erim*"
    The **perfect subjunctive** (the "*-erim*" subjunctive) is the **perfect stem** plus ***-erim, -erīs, -erit, -erīmus, -erītis, -erint***. No families, no rebels: if you know the perfect, you know it. Don't confuse it with the future perfect.

    | | I | you | he, she | we | y'all | they |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | perfect subj. | *amāverim* | *amāverīs* | *amāverit* | *amāverīmus* | *amāverītis* | *amāverint* |
    | future perfect | *amāverō* | *amāveris* | *amāverit* | *amāverimus* | *amāveritis* | *amāverint* |

## 8. The passive and the deponents

:: Nesciō quōmodo factum sit. || I don't know how it happened. (teaching line)

Amātus sum… amātus sim.

{#g-08}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 31's perfect passive: *amātus sum*, "I was loved." Swap *sum* for its subjunctive. | ***Amātus sim, amātus sīs, amātus sit; amātī sīmus, amātī sītis, amātī sint.*** The participle still agrees: *amāta sim*, said by a woman. |
| *Quōmodo factum est?* (Chapter 32: *factum est*, it happened.) Tuck it after *Nesciō*. | ***Nesciō quōmodo factum sit.*** "I don't know how it happened." |
| Deponents work the same: *locūtus sum* (Chapter 43). "I don't know what Grandpa said." | ***Nesciō quid Avus locūtus sit.*** |

## 9. Cicero's thunder

:: Quō usque tandem abūtēre, Catilīna, patientiā nostrā? || How long, Catiline, will you go on abusing our patience? (Cicero, *Against Catiline* 1.1)

Ubi fuerīs…

{#g-09}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 63 BC. Catiline, who has plotted to seize Rome, sits in the Senate as if nothing happened. Cicero opens fire (*Against Catiline* 1.1): *Quid proximā, quid superiōre nocte ēgerīs, ubi fuerīs, quōs convocāverīs, quid cōnsiliī cēperīs, quem nostrum ignōrāre arbitrāris?* (*Proximus*: last; *superior*: the one before; *agō, ēgī*: do; *convocō*: call together; *cōnsilium*: plan; *ignōrō*: not know; *nostrum*: of us; *arbitror*: think.) Find the tucked-in questions. | Four: ***ēgerīs, fuerīs, convocāverīs, cēperīs***, all perfect subjunctives, all after "who of us doesn't know." |
| Translate. | "**What you did** last night and the night before, **where you were**, **whom you called together**, **what plan you made**: which of us do you think doesn't know?" |
| Why perfect, not present? | The main verb is **now** (*arbitrāris*, "you think"), and the deeds were **earlier**. Now + earlier = the *-erim* form. |
| *Ēgerīs* (*agō, ēgī*) and *cēperīs* (*capiō, cēpī*): check the stems. | ***Ēg-*** + *-erīs*; ***cēp-*** + *-erīs*. Long *ē* in both, from the perfect. |

## 10. Earlier still, in a past story

:: Utinam nē ēdissem! || If only I hadn't eaten it! (teaching line)

Amāvisse + m.

{#g-10}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 52's Law of the Infinitive Plus: present infinitive + *-m* = *amārem*. Chapter 41 gave a **perfect** infinitive: *amāvisse*, "to have loved." Stick on *-m*. | ***Amāvissem.*** Same trick, one step further back. |
| All six. | ***Amāvissem, amāvissēs, amāvisset, amāvissēmus, amāvissētis, amāvissent.*** Long *ē* except before a final *-m, -t, -nt*, as in Chapter 52. |
| Grammar books call this the **pluperfect subjunctive**. Plain words? | **The "perfect infinitive + ending" subjunctive.** Its jobs: something that happened **earlier** than a **past** main verb, and wishes about the past that can't come true. |
| *Fuisse, fēcisse, ēdisse* + *-m*. | ***Fuissem, fēcissem, ēdissem.*** |
| Passive: Chapter 31's *amātus eram*, with *essem* for *eram*. | ***Amātus essem, amātus essēs, amātus esset…*** |

!!! success "The Law of the Infinitive Plus, Again"
    The **pluperfect subjunctive** (the "perfect infinitive + ending" subjunctive) is the **perfect infinitive** plus ***-m, -s, -t, -mus, -tis, -nt***: *amāvisse* → *amāvissem*. Passive and deponents: the participle + ***essem***: *amātus essem, locūtus essem*. The perfect subjunctive passive is the participle + ***sim***: *amātus sim*.

    | | active | passive |
    |---|---|---|
    | perfect subj. | *amāverim* (perfect stem + *-erim*) | *amātus sim* |
    | pluperfect subj. | *amāvissem* (*amāvisse* + *-m*) | *amātus essem* |

## 11. "What he had done"

:: Nesciēbam quis cāseum ēdisset. || I didn't know who had eaten the cheese. (teaching line)

Rogāvī quid fēcissēs.

{#g-11}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "I asked what you **had done**." | ***Rogāvī quid fēcissēs.*** Past story; the deed is earlier still. |
| "I didn't know who had eaten the cheese." | ***Nesciēbam quis cāseum ēdisset.*** |
| Luke 8:39. A man freed from demons goes off *praedicāns quanta illī fēcisset Iēsus.* (*Praedicō*: proclaim; *quanta*: how many things, how much.) | "Proclaiming **how much Jesus had done** for him." He's telling it after the fact, so ***fēcisset***. (Earlier in the same verse, Jesus tells him *narrā quanta tibi fēcit Deus*, "tell how much God has done for you," with a plain *fēcit*: the Bible's Latin often skips the subjunctive. Classical Latin wouldn't.) |

## 12. Now, earlier, and later

:: Quid sit futūrum crās, fuge quaerere. || Don't ask what tomorrow will bring. (Horace, *Odes* 1.9.13; Chapters 17, 39, 49)

Faciās · fēcerīs · facerēs · fēcissēs.

{#g-12}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Line up four. *Rogō quid faciās · Rogō quid fēcerīs · Rogāvī quid facerēs · Rogāvī quid fēcissēs.* | "I ask what you're doing · I ask what you did · I asked what you were doing · I asked what you had done." |
| What two questions pick the form? | **Is the main verb now or past?** And **is the tucked-in action at the same time, or earlier?** |
| And "later"? Chapter 39's *-ūrus sum*, "I'm going to." Swap *sum* for *sim*: "I ask what you're going to do." | ***Rogō quid factūrus sīs.*** |
| Now Horace, at last. *Quid sit futūrum crās, fuge quaerere.* Find the "later." | ***Futūrum sit*** = *-ūrus* + *sim*: "**what is going to be** tomorrow," tucked after *quaerere*, "to ask." Chapter 39 cracked *futūrum*; today, *sit*. "Avoid asking what tomorrow will bring." |
| "Have done" main verbs: Chapter 51 counted *vēnī*, "I have come," as now. *Rogāvī*, "I have asked," can count as now too. Do you need to worry? | **Not yet.** Read *rogāvī* as a past story unless it clearly means "have." Chapter 54 gives this whole matching game its proper name and the fine print. |

!!! success "The Law of Now and Earlier"
    Pick the subjunctive in a tucked-in question by two questions: **now or past?** and **same time or earlier?**

    | Main verb | Same time | Earlier | Later |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | **now** (also "later," commands) | *Rogō quid faciās* | *Rogō quid fēcerīs* | *Rogō quid factūrus sīs* |
    | **past** story | *Rogāvī quid facerēs* | *Rogāvī quid fēcissēs* | *Rogāvī quid factūrus essēs* |

## 13. Wishes too late

:: Utinam mortuī essēmus in terrā Aegyptī! || If only we had died in the land of Egypt! (after Exodus 16:3)

Utinam … ēdissem!

{#g-13}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 50: *Utinam veniat!* (may still happen). Chapter 52: *Utinam venīret!* (not so now). Guess: "If only he **had** come!" | ***Utinam vēnisset!*** A wish about the **past**, too late to come true: the pluperfect subjunctive. |
| Grandpa, holding his stomach: "If only I hadn't eaten the cheese!" | ***Utinam nē cāseum ēdissem!*** *Nē* still makes the "not" wish. |
| Exodus 16:3. Hungry in the desert, the Israelites grumble: *Utinam mortuī essēmus per manum Dominī in terrā Aegyptī!* (*Morior, mortuus sum*: die; *per manum*: by the hand.) | "**If only we had died** by the hand of the Lord in the land of Egypt!" A deponent: *mortuī* + *essēmus*. (They go on: back there they sat by the meat pots and ate their fill of bread.) |
| Three wishes, three times: "If only it rains!" (may still) · "If only it were raining!" (it isn't) · "If only it had rained!" (it didn't). (*Pluit, pluere, pluit*, Chapter 50.) | ***Utinam pluat! · Utinam plueret! · Utinam pluisset!*** |

## 14. The other "don't"

:: Tū nē quaesierīs, scīre nefās, quem mihi, quem tibi fīnem dī dederint. || Don't you ask (it's wrong to know) what end the gods have given to me, to you. (Horace, *Odes* 1.11.1–2)

Nē timuerīs!

{#g-14}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 47's Matthew 10:26: *Nē ergō timuerītis eōs.* Chapter 47 said *timuerītis* "looks almost like the future perfect." Name it now. | The **perfect subjunctive** of *timeō* (*timuī*): *timu-* + *-erītis*. Long *ī*, as today's Law says. |
| It means "So **don't be afraid** of them." Is anything past about it? | **No.** With *nē*, this form is simply classical prose's other "don't," aimed at "you" or "y'all": ***Nē timuerīs! Nē timuerītis!*** The polite-firm cousin of *Nōlī timēre!* |
| "Don't eat the whole cheese!" two ways. (*Tōtus*: whole.) | ***Nōlī tōtum cāseum edere!*** and ***Nē tōtum cāseum ēderīs!*** |
| Horace, *Odes* 1.11, to a woman named Leuconoē: *Tū nē quaesierīs (scīre nefās) quem mihi, quem tibi fīnem dī dederint.* (*Quaerō, quaesīvī* or *quaesiī*: ask; *nefās*: wrong, forbidden; *fīnis*: end; *dī*: gods.) Find both jobs of the *-erim* form. | ***Nē quaesierīs***: "don't ask" (the other "don't"). ***Dederint***: "what end the gods **have given**," a tucked-in question, earlier than now. |
| The poem goes on (lines 2–3): *nec Babylōniōs temptāris numerōs.* (*Temptō*: try; *Babylōnius*: Babylonian; *numerī*: numbers, horoscopes.) *Temptāris* is short for *temptāverīs* (like Chapter 41's *amāsse*). | "And don't try the Babylonian numbers": don't consult the astrologers. The poem's last line brings Chapter 6's *carpe diem*. |

## 16. Traps and fix-its

:: Errāre licet; corrigere melius. || Making a mistake is allowed; correcting it is better. (teaching line; Chapter 50)

Faciās! Fēcerīs!

{#g-16}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "I know what you're doing": *Sciō quid facis.* | ***Sciō quid faciās.*** A tucked-in question is subjunctive, even after "I know." |
| Luke 23:34, from the cross: *Pater, dīmitte illīs, nōn enim sciunt quid faciunt.* (*Dīmittō*: forgive; *enim*: for.) Is *faciunt* a mistake? | "Father, forgive them, for they don't know what they're doing." Not a mistake in the Bible's Latin, which often keeps the plain verb. In **Cicero's** Latin it would be ***quid faciant***. Write the classical way. |
| "I don't know where you were": *Nesciō ubi erās.* | ***Nesciō ubi fuerīs.*** Subjunctive, and earlier than now: the *-erim* form. |
| "I asked what you had done": *Rogāvī quid fēcerīs.* | ***Rogāvī quid fēcissēs.*** Past story + earlier = pluperfect. |
| "If only I had stayed home": *Utinam domī manērem.* (*Maneō, mānsī*.) | ***Utinam domī mānsissem!*** It's too late: pluperfect. (*Manērem* would be "if only I were staying home.") |

## 17. Lectiō: *Ubi est cāseus?*

:: Nesciō ubi eum posuerim! || I don't know where I put it! (teaching line)

Quis? Ubi? Nesciō!

Diē vīcēsimā prīmā: the morning after Chapter 52. Last night the big cheese was sitting on the table. Read each Latin line aloud. Guess the English, then tap to check. For the reverse drill, tap **Tege Latīna** and rebuild each line from the English. Hunt every tucked-in question and every new *-erim* and *-issem* form. New in passing: *vīcēsimus prīmus*, twenty-first; *cēlō, cēlāre*, hide; *proximus*, last (section 9); *enim*, for; *alter*, a second. ## Speak it aloud

:: Loquāmur Latīnē! || Let's speak Latin! (teaching line)

1. In your own words: what is a tucked-in question, and why does Latin give it the subjunctive when English doesn't change?
2. Chant *amāverim, fuerim, fēcerim* and *amāvissem, fuissem, fēcissem* in all six persons; then *amātus sim* and *amātus essem*.
3. Ask five tucked-in questions about the people around you (*Rogō quid…, Nesciō ubi…, Dīc mihi cūr…, Sciō quis…*), one with *num*.
4. Tell what you asked yesterday and what someone had done (*Rogāvī quid facerēs · Nesciēbam quis … ēdisset*).
5. Make three wishes about the past that can't come true (*Utinam … vēnisset!*), one with *nē*.
6. Say "don't" three ways: *Nōlī timēre · Nē timuerīs · Nē timuerītis*.
7. The four Laws in your own words: the Tucked-In Question, *-erim*, the Infinitive Plus Again, Now and Earlier.
8. The real lines: *Quārē id faciam, fortasse requīris. Nesciō, sed fierī sentiō et excrucior · Nescītis quid petātis · nōn scīs unde veniat et quō vādat · Quid proximā, quid superiōre nocte ēgerīs, ubi fuerīs, quōs convocāverīs, quid cōnsiliī cēperīs, quem nostrum ignōrāre arbitrāris? · praedicāns quanta illī fēcisset Iēsus · Quid sit futūrum crās, fuge quaerere · Utinam mortuī essēmus per manum Dominī in terrā Aegyptī · Nē ergō timuerītis eōs · Tū nē quaesierīs, scīre nefās, quem mihi, quem tibi fīnem dī dederint.* Then read the *Lectiō* aloud, playing every part, and rebuild it from the English.

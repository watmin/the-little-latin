# Caput XXVII: Iam Fēceram

*Liber III, Fābulae · Chapter 27*

*In which Grandpa looks at the table and the cheese is already gone. Chapter 26 gave you two cameras, the film and the snapshot. Today you get a third look: the snapshot that was already in the album before this one, "had eaten," and its twin from the future, "will have eaten." Both are built from parts you already own: the perfect stem from Chapter 25 and* eram *and* erō *from Chapter 16. Then the family holds an investigation, and for once Grandpa's old excuse turns out to be true. Speaking goal: put events in order.*

!!! abstract "In this chapter"
    - **Saying:** *Iam ēderat!* · *Nōndum vēnerat.* · *Quid fēcerās?* · *Sī cāseum ēderis, Avia clāmābit!* · *Prīmum… deinde… posteā… tandem.* · *Sērō!*
    - **Grammar words, in plain English:** pluperfect (old-school: past perfect; "had …ed," finished before another past moment); future perfect ("will have …ed," finished before a future moment); perfect system (the three tenses built on key 3)
    - **Forms:** perfect stem + *eram, erās, erat…* (*amāveram*, *fueram*, *ieram*); perfect stem + *erō, eris, erit… erint* (*amāverō*, *fuerō*, *potuerō*); the *-ērunt / -erint / -erant* look-alikes
    - **Laws:** the Law of Eram and Erō; the Law of the Earlier Step
    - **Words:** *nōndum, anteā, posteā, prīmum, sērō*; *lectus*; *afferō, afferre, attulī, allātum*
    - **Reading:** *Iam ēderat!* (the morning after Grandpa's story, a cheese goes missing)
    - **Speaking:** tell what happened first, what had already happened, and what will have happened by then

!!! tip "How to read this chapter"
    Same as always. Say your answer **out loud**, then tap the wax tablet to check. Keep three chants handy: Chapter 16's *eram, erās, erat…* and *erō, eris, erit…*, and Chapter 25's *amāvī, amāvistī, amāvit…*. Today you glue the first two onto the third.

## 1. *Iam ēderat!*

:: Avia cāseum iam ēderat. || Grandma had already eaten the cheese. (Chapter 26, looking back)

Avus fābulam nārrāvit. Deinde mēnsam spectāvit. Sed Avia cāseum iam ēderat!

{#g-01}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 26 ended with Grandma eating the cheese while Grandpa told his story. Say it in two steps: "Grandpa told the story. Then he looked at the table." | ***Avus fābulam nārrāvit. Deinde mēnsam spectāvit.*** Two perfects, two steps. |
| When he looked, the cheese was gone. When did Grandma eat it: after he looked, or before? | **Before.** The eating was finished before the looking. It's a step that sits **behind** the other step. |
| Latin has a tense for that: *Avia cāseum iam ēderat.* Find the stem you know from Chapter 25. | ***Ēd-***, from *edō, edere, ēdī, ēsum*. Long ē: the **perfect** stem. |
| What's glued on after *ēd-*? Where have you seen it before? | ***-erat***: Chapter 16's *erat*, "was," the imperfect of *sum*. So *ēd- + erat*. |
| Guess the English: "Grandma ___ already eaten the cheese." (*Iam*: already, Chapter 16.) | "Grandma **had** already eaten the cheese." |
| Plain English: this tense is the **pluperfect**: an action already finished before some other past moment. "Had …ed." Where does the name come from? | Latin *plūs quam perfectum*, "more than perfect": one step further back than the perfect. English school grammar calls it the **past perfect**. Think "the past of the past." |
| Chapter 26 gave you film (imperfect) and snapshot (perfect). What is the pluperfect? | An **older snapshot**: a photo already in the album before this one was taken. You hold it up to say "by then, this had already happened." |
| Say it: "Grandpa came into the kitchen. The dog had already eaten the cheese." | ***Avus in culīnam vēnit. Canis cāseum iam ēderat.*** Step, then the older snapshot. |

## 2. The "had" chant

:: Sīc māternus avus dīxerat atque avia. || That's how his grandfather on his mother's side had said it, and his grandmother. (Catullus 84.6)

Eram, erās, erat… amāveram, amāverās, amāverat…

{#g-02}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 16: chant *sum* in the imperfect. | *Eram, erās, erat, erāmus, erātis, erant.* "I was, you were, he/she was, we were, y'all were, they were." |
| The pluperfect is the perfect stem + those six. Build it on *amāv-*. | ***amāveram, amāverās, amāverat, amāverāmus, amāverātis, amāverant***: "I had loved, you had loved, he/she had loved, we had loved, y'all had loved, they had loved." |
| Where are the long vowels? Why is the a short in *amāveram, amāverat, amāverant*? | Long ā in *-erās, -erāmus, -erātis*, exactly where *erās, erāmus, erātis* have it. Short before final *-m, -t, -nt* (Chapter 16's rule). The e of *-er-* is always short. |
| Other families. Take key 3 of *habeō, legō, capiō, audiō* and make "I had …". | *habuī →* ***habueram*** · *lēgī →* ***lēgeram*** · *cēpī →* ***cēperam*** · *audīvī →* ***audīveram***. Drop the *-ī*, add *-eram*. No family differences at all. |
| "We had done" (*faciō*), "they had come" (*veniō*), "you had seen" (*videō*)? | ***fēcerāmus*** · ***vēnerant*** · ***vīderās*** |
| Chapter 25's irregulars: *fuī, iī, tulī, dedī, potuī*. Say "I had been, I had gone, I had carried, I had given, I had been able." | ***fueram, ieram, tuleram, dederam, potueram*** (also *īveram*, from *īvī*). Wild in key 3, perfectly tame after it. |
| *Legeram* or *lēgeram*? *Edēbat* or *ēderat* for "had eaten"? | ***Lēgeram***, ***ēderat***. The pluperfect is built on the **perfect** stem, so it keeps the perfect's long vowel (*lēg-, ēd-*). Short *leg-, ed-* belong to the present stem (*legēbam, edēbat*). |
| Catullus mocks Arrius, who said *hīnsidiās* for *īnsidiās* ("ambush"), with an extra h, to sound posh. Where did he learn it? *Sīc māternus avus dīxerat atque avia.* (*Sīc*: so, that way. *Māternus*: on the mother's side.) | "That's how his grandfather on his mother's side **had said** it, and his grandmother." *Dīx-* (*dīxī*) + *-erat*. Catullus guesses the habit ran in the family. (Catullus 84.6; the verb is singular, agreeing with the nearer speaker, *avus*.) |
| Translate: *Quid fēcerās?* | "What **had** you done?" Chapter 25's *Quid fēcistī?* ("What did you do?") pushed one notch further back. |

## 3. The step behind the step

:: Nōndum vēnerat hōra eius. || His hour had not yet come. (John 7:30, Vulgate)

Pater domum vēnit. Avia cēnam iam parāverat.

{#g-03}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Pater domum vēnit. Avia cēnam iam parāverat.* (*Parō*, Chapter 4.) Which happened first? | Grandma made dinner first. Then Dad came home. "Dad came home. Grandma had already made dinner." |
| Chapter 26's Law: imperfect = stage, perfect = steps. Where does the pluperfect stand? | **Behind** a step. It reaches back to something already done before that step. The story's main line still moves with perfects. |
| Can a whole story run in pluperfects? | Rarely. A pluperfect needs a later past moment to lean on. Tell the main line in perfects; reach back with the pluperfect only when you mean "already, before then." |
| *Nōndum*: not yet (*nōn* + *-dum*). *Pater domum vēnit. Avia cēnam nōndum parāverat.* | "Dad came home. Grandma **hadn't yet** made dinner." *Nōndum* + pluperfect = "had not yet." |
| John's Gospel: they wanted to arrest Jesus, but no one laid a hand on him, *quia nōndum vēnerat hōra eius.* (*Quia*: because. *Hōra*: hour, Chapter 22.) | "…because his hour **had not yet come**." *Vēn-* + *-erat*. (John 7:30, Vulgate) |
| *Anteā*: before, earlier. *Posteā*: afterwards, later. "Earlier Grandpa had eaten a lot; afterwards he slept." | ***Anteā Avus multum ēderat; posteā dormīvit.*** Pluperfect for the earlier thing, perfect for the step. |
| English says "Grandma ate the cheese before Grandpa looked." Latin "before" clauses come later. How do you say it today? | Two sentences: ***Avus mēnsam spectāvit. Avia cāseum iam ēderat.*** *Iam* and the pluperfect carry the "before." |
| Retell Grandpa's Chapter 26 story with the older snapshot: "My mother came. I had already eaten the whole cheese." | ***Māter mea vēnit. Ego cāseum tōtum iam ēderam.*** |
| *Ubi fuerās?* vs Chapter 26's *Ubi fuistī?* | *Ubi fuerās?* "Where **had** you been (before that moment)?" Say, before you walked in muddy. *Ubi fuistī?* "Where were you? Where have you been?" |

## 4. Will have

:: Tempora sī fuerint nūbila, sōlus eris. || If the times turn cloudy, you will be alone. (Ovid, *Tristia* 1.9.6)

Erō, eris, erit… amāverō, amāveris, amāverit…

{#g-04}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 16: chant *sum* in the future. | *Erō, eris, erit, erimus, eritis, erunt.* "I will be, you will be…" |
| Same trick: perfect stem + *erō*. Build it on *amāv-*. | ***amāverō, amāveris, amāverit, amāverimus, amāveritis, amāverint***: "I will have loved, you will have loved, he/she will have loved, we…, y'all…, they will have loved." |
| Compare the two charts slot by slot. Where does the new one break the pattern? | Only "they": *erunt* but ***-erint***. Five slots copy *erō*; the sixth goes its own way. (Handy: it keeps "they will have loved" apart from Chapter 25's perfect *amāvērunt*.) |
| Plain English: the **future perfect** is an action that will be finished by some point in the future: "will have …ed." | A snapshot you **will have** in the album by then: you stand in tomorrow and look back. English school grammar uses the same name. |
| "By evening I will already have eaten the cheese." (*Vesperī*: in the evening, Chapter 16.) | ***Vesperī cāseum iam ēderō.*** |
| "You will have read," "he will have heard," "we will have done," "they will have come." | ***lēgeris*** · ***audīverit*** · ***fēcerimus*** · ***vēnerint*** |
| "I will have been," "I will have gone," "you will have carried," "they will have given." | ***fuerō*** · ***ierō*** · ***tuleris*** · ***dederint*** |
| Chapter 18: *poterō*, "I will be able." Now *potuerō*. What's the difference? | *Poterō*: future, "I'll be able." *Potuerō*: future perfect, "I'll have been able" (already done by then). *Pot-* + *erō* vs *potu-* (key 3, *potuī*) + *erō*. |
| Ovid, in exile, on fair-weather friends: *Dōnec eris sospes, multōs numerābis amīcōs; tempora sī fuerint nūbila, sōlus eris.* (*Dōnec*: as long as. *Sospes*: safe and sound. *Numerō*: count. *Nūbilus*: cloudy, from Chapter 15's *nūbēs*.) Find the future perfect. | ***Fuerint***: *fu-* + *-erint*. "As long as you're safe and sound, you'll count many friends; if the times turn cloudy, you'll be alone." (*Tristia* 1.9.5–6) |

!!! success "The Law of Eram and Erō"
    Perfect stem (key 3 without its *-ī*) + ***eram, erās, erat, erāmus, erātis, erant*** = the **pluperfect**, "had …ed": *amāveram, fueram, ieram*.
    Perfect stem + ***erō, eris, erit, erimus, eritis, erint*** = the **future perfect**, "will have …ed": *amāverō, fuerō, ierō*.
    Watch "they": future perfect ***-erint***, never *-erunt*.
    Every family and every irregular works the same way. Only key 3 matters.

## 5. If, and by the time

:: Ut sēmentem fēceris, ita metēs. || As you sow, so shall you reap. (Cicero, *Dē Ōrātōre* 2.261)

Sī cāseum ēderis, Avia clāmābit!

{#g-05}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 18 gave you *sī*, "if." *Sī cāseum ēderis, Avia clāmābit.* (*Clāmō*, Chapter 26.) Which happens first, the eating or the shouting? | The eating. Then Grandma shouts. Both lie in the future. |
| So which tense is *ēderis*? And how does English say it? | Future perfect: *ēd-* + *-eris*. English says "**If you eat** the cheese, Grandma will shout": a present tense. Latin keeps the clock honest: by the time she shouts, you **will have eaten** it. |
| Is *ēderis* "you ate"? | No. "You ate" is the perfect, *ēdistī*. *Ēderis* points forward: "you will have eaten." |
| "If you come tomorrow, we will eat cheese." | ***Sī crās vēneris, cāseum edēmus.*** (*Edēmus*: Chapter 17's future.) |
| Cicero tells of a quick comeback. A man was asked, "If I speak against you (*sī contrā tē dīxerō*), will you badmouth me?" He answered: *Ut sēmentem fēceris, ita metēs.* (*Ut… ita*: as… so. *Sēmentem facere*: to sow. *Metō*: reap.) | "As you sow, so shall you reap." Literally "As you **will have made** your sowing, so you **will reap**." Sowing comes first, so: future perfect. The question had one too: *dīxerō*. (*Dē Ōrātōre* 2.261) |
| Catullus invites a friend to dinner (Chapter 10's purse full of cobwebs): *Cēnābis bene, mī Fabulle, apud mē… sī tēcum attuleris bonam atque magnam cēnam.* (*Cēnābis*: you'll dine. *Apud mē*: at my place. *Afferō, afferre, attulī, allātum*: bring along, *ad* + *ferō*, Chapter 25's *tulī*.) | "You'll dine well at my place, my Fabullus… **if you bring** a good big dinner with you." *Attuleris*: future perfect of *afferō*. The joke: the guest brings the whole dinner, because the host's purse is full of cobwebs. (Catullus 13.1–4) |
| Not just "if": "by the time." "Dad will come home in the evening. We will already have eaten dinner." | ***Pater vesperī domum veniet. Nōs cēnam iam ēderimus.*** |
| Ovid's other half: *Dōnec eris sospes…* Why plain future *eris*, not *fueris*? | Being safe and counting friends happen **at the same time**. Same time → future; earlier → future perfect. English says "are" for both. |
| Catullus 5 counts kisses: *dein, cum mīlia multa fēcerimus…* (*Dein*: then. *Cum* here means "when"; that kind of clause comes later.) Which tense, and why? | ***Fēcerimus***, future perfect: "then, when we **have made** many thousands…" Only after the kissing is done comes the next step: *conturbābimus illa*, "we'll scramble the count." (Catullus 5.10) |

!!! success "The Law of the Earlier Step"
    When one event comes **before** another, Latin moves the earlier one back a notch.
    Before a **past** step → **pluperfect**: *Avus vēnit. Canis cāseum iam ēderat.*
    Before a **future** step → **future perfect**: *Sī cāseum ēderis, Avia clāmābit.*
    English often hides the second kind behind a present ("if you eat"). Latin usually keeps the clock. Ask: **which comes first?**

## 6. Six tenses, two stems

:: Omnia tempus habent. || Everything has its time. (Ecclesiastes 3:1, Vulgate)

Amō, amābam, amābō… amāvī, amāveram, amāverō.

{#g-06}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 25 said every verb has two stems. Which three tenses use the present stem *amā-*? | Present *amō*, imperfect *amābam*, future *amābō*: the **present system**. |
| And the perfect stem *amāv-*? | Perfect *amāvī*, pluperfect *amāveram*, future perfect *amāverō*. Plain English: the **perfect system**, the three tenses of finished action, all built on key 3. |
| Pair them: present/perfect, imperfect/pluperfect, future/future perfect. What does each pair share? | The **time** it hangs on: now (*amō* / *amāvī*, "I have loved"), then (*amābam* / *amāveram*), later (*amābō* / *amāverō*). The second of each pair is the "already finished" version. |
| Three look-alikes: *amāvērunt · amāverint · amāverant.* | Perfect "they loved" (long ē, *-ērunt*) · future perfect "they will have loved" (*-erint*) · pluperfect "they had loved" (*-erant*). Look at the vowel after the r: *u*, *i*, or *a*. |
| And *erunt · fuerint · fuērunt*? | "They will be" (Chapter 16) · "they will have been" · "they were / have been" (Chapter 25). |
| Sort into past, now, and future: *edet, ēderat, edit, ēdit, ēderit*. | Past: ***ēdit*** (ate) and ***ēderat*** (had eaten, before another past moment). Now: ***edit*** (eats). Future: ***edet*** (will eat) and ***ēderit*** (will have eaten, before another future moment). |
| *Avus fessus erat* vs *Avus fessus fuerat*? | *Erat*: "Grandpa was tired" (at that moment, the stage). *Fuerat*: "Grandpa had been tired" (earlier, before then). |

!!! success "Six tenses, one verb"
    | | Present system (key 1–2) | Perfect system (key 3) |
    |---|---|---|
    | now | *amō* · *sum* | *amāvī* · *fuī* |
    | then | *amābam* · *eram* | *amāveram* · *fueram* |
    | later | *amābō* · *erō* | *amāverō* · *fuerō* |

!!! warning "Look-alikes"
    *amāvērunt* they loved · *amāverint* they will have loved · *amāverant* they had loved · *erunt* they will be.
    *ēdit* he ate · *ēderit* he will have eaten · *ēderat* he had eaten · *edit* he eats · *edet* he will eat.

## 7. First, then, afterwards

:: Prīmum vīvere, deinde philosophārī. || First live, then philosophize. (Latin proverb; *philosophārī* is an odd-looking infinitive you'll meet later)

Prīmum… deinde… posteā… tandem.

{#g-07}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Prīmum*: first, at first (Chapter 22's *prīmus*). Put in order: *tandem, prīmum, posteā, deinde*. | ***Prīmum… deinde… posteā… tandem.*** First, next, afterwards, finally. |
| Grandpa's day, step by step: first he ate, then he slept, afterwards he swam, finally he ate again. | ***Prīmum Avus ēdit. Deinde dormīvit. Posteā natāvit. Tandem iterum ēdit.*** |
| Same day, seen from one moment: "Grandma came. Grandpa had already eaten and slept." | ***Avia vēnit. Avus iam ēderat et dormīverat.*** |
| Plan tomorrow with *sī*: "If I work first, afterwards I'll swim." | ***Sī prīmum labōrāverō, posteā natābō.*** |
| "Mom will come home. We will already have read the book." | ***Māter domum veniet. Nōs librum iam lēgerimus.*** |
| *Sērō*: late, too late. Quīntus runs to the table. "Too late! Marcus had already eaten the apple." | ***Sērō! Marcus mālum iam ēderat.*** |
| "You'll come at the third hour. Too late! The dog will already have eaten it." (Chapter 22's *hōrā tertiā*.) | ***Hōrā tertiā veniēs. Sērō! Canis eum iam ēderit.*** |
| Your turn: three lines about this morning with *prīmum*, *deinde*, and one pluperfect. | E.g. ***Prīmum cibum ēdī. Deinde librum lēgī. Posteā in culīnam iī: canis cibum meum iam ēderat!*** |

## 8. Fix it

:: Errāre hūmānum est. || To err is human. (proverb)

Ēderat an ēderit? Amāvērunt an amāverint?

{#g-08}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "They will have loved": *amāverunt.* | ***Amāverint.*** *-erunt* belongs to *sum*'s future (*erunt*); the future perfect says *-erint*. (The perfect is *amāvērunt*, with long ē.) |
| "We had read": *legerāmus.* | ***Lēgerāmus.*** Perfect stem *lēg-*, long ē. |
| "He had been": *erāverat*. | ***Fuerat.*** Perfect stem *fu-* + *-erat*. *Er-* and *fu-* never mix (Chapter 26). |
| "If you eat the cheese, Grandma will shout": *Sī cāseum edis, Avia clāmābit.* | ***Sī cāseum ēderis…*** The eating comes first, in the future: future perfect. (*Edis* is "you eat," right now.) |
| "Dad came home. Grandma had made dinner": *Avia cēnam parābat.* | That says she **was making** it (stage, still going). For "had made": ***parāverat***. |
| "I had been able": *poteram.* | ***Potueram.*** *Poteram* is the imperfect, "I could / I was able." Key 3 *potuī* gives *potueram*. |
| "Grandpa looked; Grandma had already eaten it": *Avus mēnsam spectāverat. Avia cāseum ēdit.* | Swapped. The earlier event takes the pluperfect: ***Avus mēnsam spectāvit. Avia cāseum iam ēderat.*** |

## 9. Lectiō: *Iam ēderat!*

:: Canis ēdit! || The dog ate it! (Grandpa's old excuse, Chapter 26)

Māne est. Heri Pater cāseum novum ēmit. Nunc… ubi est?

Read each Latin line aloud. Guess the English, then tap to check. For the reverse drill, tap **Tege Latīna** and rebuild each line from the English. As you read, sort every past verb: **stage** (imperfect), **step** (perfect), or **step behind the step** (pluperfect). At the end, watch for "will have."

::: dialogue lectio
Māter | Heri Pater cāseum novum ēmit. Ubi est? || Mother | Yesterday Dad bought a new cheese. Where is it?
Pater | Heri vesperī eum in culīnam portāvī. In mēnsā erat. || Father | Last night I carried it into the kitchen. It was on the table.
Māter | Hodiē māne in culīnam vēnī. Mēnsa iam vacua erat! Quis cāseum ēdit? || Mother | This morning I came into the kitchen. The table was already empty! Who ate the cheese?
Omnēs | Nōn ego! || Everyone | Not me!
Māter | Ave? Tū? || Mother | Grandpa? You?
Avus | Nōn ego! Fābulam meam iam nārrāveram, et in lectō stertēbam. || Grandpa | Not me! I had already told my story, and I was snoring in bed.
Avia | Ita est. Ego quoque iam in lectum ieram. || Grandma | That's right. And I had already gone to bed too.
Quīntus | Ego cāseum nōn ēdī! Iam dormiēbam. || Quīntus | I didn't eat the cheese! I was already asleep.
Marcus | Nōn ego! Sed audīte: canis nōndum in cubīle ierat… || Marcus | Not me! But listen, y'all: the dog hadn't gone to his bed yet…
Cornēlia | Ita! Ego librum legēbam. Subitō canem audīvī. || Cornēlia | Right! I was reading a book. Suddenly I heard the dog.
Līberī | Quid tum? || The kids | And then what?
Cornēlia | In culīnam iī. Sed sērō! Canis cāseum iam ēderat! || Cornēlia | I went into the kitchen. But too late! The dog had already eaten the cheese!
Cāseus | Bau! || Cheese | Woof!
Avus | Ecce! Ōlim dīxī: "Canis ēdit!" Et nunc tandem canis ēdit! || Grandpa | See! Once upon a time I said, "The dog ate it!" And now, at last, the dog really ate it!
Omnēs | In stomachō… || Everyone | In the stomach of…
Cornēlia | …Cāseī! || Cornēlia | …Cheese!
Pater | Hodiē cāseum novum emam. || Father | Today I'll buy a new cheese.
Māter | Bene. Sed audī, canis: sī cāseum rūrsus ēderis, in hortō dormiēs! || Mother | Good. But listen, dog: if you eat the cheese again, you'll sleep in the garden!
Cāseus | Bau… || Cheese | Woof…
Quīntus | Et sī Avus cāseum ēderit? || Quīntus | And if Grandpa eats the cheese?
Avia | Avus quoque in hortō dormiet! || Grandma | Grandpa will sleep in the garden too!
Avus | Ēheu! || Grandpa | Oh no!
Iōannēs | Ego cum Patre ībō. Sī cāseum ēmerimus, statim domum veniēmus. || John | I'll go with Dad. If we buy the cheese, we'll come straight home.
Avus | Bene, Iōannēs. Sed sī vōs domum vēneritis… || Grandpa | Good, John. But if y'all get home…
Avia | …tū eum edēs? || Grandma | …you'll eat it?
Avus | Minimē! Canis eum iam ēderit! || Grandpa | No way! The dog will already have eaten it!
Cāseus | Bau! || Cheese | Woof!

{#g-09}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Hodiē māne in culīnam vēnī. Mēnsa iam vacua erat!* Step or stage? | *Vēnī* is the step. *Erat* is the stage: what she found. *Iam* + imperfect: "was already empty." The pluperfects come with the alibis. |
| Grandpa's alibi: *Fābulam meam iam nārrāveram, et in lectō stertēbam.* What does each verb do? | *Nārrāveram*: the step behind the step (the story was already over). *Stertēbam*: the stage (snoring was going on). (*Lectus*: bed.) |
| *Ego quoque iam in lectum ieram.* Take *ieram* apart. | *I-* (key 3 *iī*) + *-eram*: "I had gone." *In lectum*: into bed (*in* + accusative, Chapter 8). |
| *Canis nōndum in cubīle ierat…* What is Marcus hinting? | "The dog **hadn't yet** gone to his bed." He was still up: suspect number one. (*Cubīle*, Chapter 15.) |
| Sort Cornēlia's verbs: *legēbam · audīvī · iī · ēderat.* | *Legēbam*: stage · *audīvī, iī*: steps · *ēderat*: the step behind the step. By the time she got there, it was done. *Sērō!* |
| *Ōlim dīxī: "Canis ēdit!" Et nunc tandem canis ēdit!* | Chapter 26's payoff: Grandpa's boyhood lie has finally come true. |
| *Sī cāseum rūrsus ēderis, in hortō dormiēs!* Which comes first? | The eating (future perfect *ēderis*), then the night in the garden (future *dormiēs*). English: "If you eat…" (*Rūrsus*: again, Chapter 24.) |
| *Canis eum iam ēderit!* vs Chapter 26's *Canis ēdit!* | *Ēdit*: "the dog ate it" (past). *Ēderit*: "the dog **will have eaten** it" (by the time you're home). One syllable, a whole day apart. |

## Speak it aloud

:: Sī dīxeris, vīceris. || Say it, and you'll have won. (built on Chapter 25's *vīcī*)

1. Chant both new tenses of *amō*: *amāveram…amāverant*, then *amāverō…amāverint*. Lean on *eram* and *erō*.
2. Say the Law of Eram and Erō in your own words. Then the "they" trap: *amāvērunt · amāverint · amāverant · erunt*.
3. Irregulars, fast: *fueram, ieram, tuleram, dederam, potueram* · *fuerō, ierō, tulerō, dederō, potuerō*.
4. Step behind the step: *Avus in culīnam vēnit. Canis cāseum iam ēderat.* Make three more pairs of your own.
5. "If" with the future perfect: *Sī cāseum ēderis, Avia clāmābit.* · *Ut sēmentem fēceris, ita metēs.*
6. Order your day: *Prīmum… deinde… posteā… tandem…*, then one *iam …-eram* and one *nōndum …-eram*.
7. Read the *Lectiō* aloud, playing every part (the dog too). Then hide the Latin and rebuild it.

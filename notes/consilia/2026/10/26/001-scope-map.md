# Scope Map: what a complete Latin course covers

Purpose: the full inventory The Little Latin must eventually teach. Each item is tagged with where the three reference courses treat it, so we know nothing is missing.
Goal lens: **conversational Latin + reading real texts**, classical pronunciation, macrons always.

Source keys:
- **W** = Wheelock's Latin, 7th ed., chapters 1–40 (wheelockslatin.com; LoC TOC catdir.loc.gov/catdir/toc/fy0607/2005284111.html)
- **LL** = Ørberg, *Lingua Latina per se Illustrata, Familia Romana*, capitula I–XXXV (via-neolatina.fr "Progressio grammaticalis"; whsnyderjr.com grammar-by-lesson; Fleming *Companion*)
- **CLC** = Cambridge Latin Course 5th ed., Stages 1–48, Books 1–4 (cambridgelatin.org 5e Scope and Sequence PDF)

Chapter refs are approximate where the sources spread a topic across chapters.

## 3. Cases and their uses
- **Nominative**: subject, predicate nominative (W4; LL I; CLC 1)
- **Vocative**: direct address (W2–3; LL IV)
- **Genitive**: possession (W2; LL II), partitive / of the whole (W15; LL XII), description/quality (W40; LL XIX), objective & subjective (LL XXV), value and charge (LL XXIX+), with adjectives/verbs (meminī, oblīvīscor, plēnus)
- **Dative**: indirect object (W2; LL VII; CLC 9), with special verbs (W35; LL XII), with compounds (W35), with adjectives (W35), possession (est mihi; LL VII-ish), reference (W38), purpose & double dative, agent with passive periphrastic (W24)
- **Accusative**: direct object (W2; LL III; CLC 2), place to which (W37; LL VI), extent of time/space (W37), subject of infinitive (W25; LL XI), exclamation, double accusative, accusative of respect (poetic)
- **Ablative**: means/instrument (W14; LL VI), manner, accompaniment (W14), agent with ā/ab (W18; LL VI), place where / from which, separation (W20, 22; LL V–VI), time when / within which (W15), comparison (W26; LL XVI/XXIV), cause, degree of difference, description, respect/specification (LL XI), price, absolute (W24; LL XVI), with special deponents ūtor fruor fungor potior vescor (W34; LL XXVII), with prepositions (W2+; LL V)
- **Locative**: Rōmae, domī, rūrī, humī (W37; LL VI)
- Prepositions with acc. (ad, ante, apud, circum, contrā, inter, ob, per, post, prope, propter, trāns) and abl. (ā/ab, cum, dē, ē/ex, prō, sine), in/sub with both (W, LL V–VII, CLC)

## 4. Adjectives and adverbs
- Agreement in gender, number, case (W2; LL II–V; CLC); substantive adjectives (W4)
- 1st/2nd decl adjectives (bonus, līber, pulcher) (W2–5)
- -īus adjectives: ūnus, sōlus, tōtus, nūllus, ūllus, alius, alter, uter, neuter (W9; LL XIV/XIX)
- 3rd decl adjectives, 1/2/3 terminations, i-stem rules; present participle declension (W16; LL XII)
- Comparison: -ior / -issimus, -errimus, -illimus; quam vs abl. of comparison; irregular (bonus melior optimus, etc.) (W26–27; LL XII, XVIII; CLC 8, 10)
- Adverbs: formation (-ē, -iter, -ō), comparison (-ius, -issimē), irregular (bene, male, magis/maximē, diū) (W32; LL XXIV)
- Possessive adjectives meus tuus suus noster vester (W13; LL II, IV)

## 5. Pronouns
- Personal ego/tū/nōs/vōs, is ea id (W11; LL III–V; CLC 9)
- Reflexive sē, suus; reflexive vs eius (W13; LL IV, VII)
- Demonstratives hic, ille, iste, is, īdem (W9, 11; LL VII–VIII, XVIII)
- Intensive ipse (W13; LL IX)
- Relative quī quae quod; antecedent agreement (W17; LL III–VIII; CLC 11)
- Interrogative quis quid / quī quae quod (W19; LL I–III)
- Indefinites: aliquis, quīdam, quisque, quisquam, quis after sī/nisi/num/nē, quīvīs/quīlibet (LL XXVI+; W supplementary)
- Correlatives: tantus…quantus, tālis…quālis, tot…quot (LL VIII)

## 6. Numerals
- Cardinals 1–1000, declinable ūnus duo trēs, mīlle/mīlia + gen. (W15; LL I, XII–XIII)
- Ordinals, distributives (singulī, bīnī), numeral adverbs (semel, bis) (LL XIII, XXX)
- Roman numerals, dates and the calendar (Kalendae, Nōnae, Īdūs) (LL XIII)

## 7. Verbs: forms
- Person, number, tense, mood, voice: concepts; principal parts (W1, 12)
- Conjugations 1, 2, 3, 3-iō, 4 (W1, 8, 10; LL III–IV, VIII; CLC 1–5)
- Present system active: present, imperfect, future (W1, 5, 8, 10; LL III–XX; CLC 1–6)
- Perfect system active: perfect, pluperfect, future perfect (W12; LL XXI–XXIV; CLC 6–7)
- Passive voice, present system and perfect system (W18–21; LL VI, XVII–XXII)
- Irregulars: sum, possum (W4, 6), eō (W37), ferō (W31), volō/nōlō/mālō (W32), fīō (W36), edō, dō, compounds of sum
- Deponents and semi-deponents (audeō, gaudeō, soleō, fīdō) (W34; LL XXIV–XXV)
- Defective verbs: meminī, ōdī, coepī, inquit, aiō
- Impersonal verbs: licet, oportet, decet, placet, pudet, taedet, miseret; impersonal passive (pugnātur)
- Imperatives: present 2nd sg/pl active (W1; LL IV–V; CH1 here), negative nōlī/nōlīte + inf. and nē + perf. subj., irregular dīc dūc fac fer, deponent imperatives (LL XXV), future imperative -tō (LL XXVI)
- Infinitives: present/perfect/future, active/passive; complementary infinitive (W6, 25; LL X–XI, XXI)
- Participles: present active, perfect passive, future active, future passive (gerundive); as adjectives and as clauses (W23; LL XXI–XXIII; CLC 13+)
- Gerund and gerundive; gerundive of obligation (passive periphrastic) (W24, 39; LL XXVI, XXXI)
- Supine in -um (purpose after motion) and -ū (respect: mīrābile dictū) (W38)
- Subjunctive forms: present, imperfect, perfect, pluperfect, active & passive (W28–30; LL XXVII–XXIX)
- Periphrastics: active (-ūrus sum) and passive (-ndus est) (W24)

## 8. Syntax: clauses and constructions
- Word order: SOV default, emphasis by position, hyperbaton (W3; CLC 1–2)
- Coordination: et, -que, atque/ac, sed, aut, vel, neque/nec, et…et (W2; LL II, VII)
- Questions: -ne, nōnne, num; question words (W40; LL I–VII)
- Relative clauses (indicative); relative of characteristic (subj.) (W17, 38)
- Indirect statement: acc. + inf., tense of infinitive relative to main verb, sē in indirect statement (W25; LL XI, XXV; CLC 3rd/4th book)
- Ablative absolute (W24; LL XVI)
- Independent subjunctives: hortatory/jussive, deliberative, optative, potential (W28; LL XXIX–XXX)
- Purpose clauses ut/nē; relative purpose; ad + gerund(ive); supine (W28, 38–39; LL XXIX)
- Result clauses ut/ut nōn (W29; LL XXVIII)
- Sequence of tenses (W30)
- Indirect questions (W30; LL XXIX)
- Indirect commands / jussive noun clauses (W36; LL XXVII)
- Cum clauses: temporal, circumstantial, causal, concessive (W31; LL XXIX)
- Temporal clauses: dum, dōnec, postquam, ubi, simul ac, antequam/priusquam
- Causal clauses quod, quia, quoniam (ind./subj.)
- Conditionals: simple, future more vivid/less vivid, contrary-to-fact present/past, mixed (W33; LL XXX+)
- Proviso clauses dummodo (W32); concessive (quamquam, etsī, quamvīs)
- Fear clauses nē / ut (W40)
- Clauses of doubting/prevention (quīn, quōminus)
- Subordinate clauses in indirect discourse (subjunctive); oratio obliqua (later CLC Book 4)

## 9. Prosody and reading real texts
- Quantity rules, dactylic hexameter scansion, elegiac couplet; elision, caesura (LL XXXIV)
- Hendecasyllables (Catullus) basics
- Reading strategies: reading left-to-right, case-driven expectation (CLC "reading method")
- Graded authors path: Fabulae Faciles / Ørberg Roma Aeterna → Caesar, Nepos, Phaedrus, Martial, Catullus, Cicero letters, Ovid, Vergil

## 10. Vocabulary
- Core ~1,000 lemmas by frequency (Dickinson College Commentaries Latin Core Vocabulary, ~1,000 words; Diederich frequency lists)
- Principal parts for all verbs taught; genitive + gender for nouns
- Derivatives/English connections as memory hooks
- Spoken-life domains: greetings, family, body, food & meals, house, time & weather, numbers & money, city, school, feelings, travel

## 11. Conversational functions (spoken track; LL-style and modern spoken-Latin practice)
- Greet / take leave (salvē, valē, avē, bonum diem); names; how are you
- Please / thanks / sorry (quaesō, amābō tē, sīs; grātiās tibi agō; ignōsce)
- Asking & answering questions (quis, quid, ubi, quandō, cūr, quōmodo, quot)
- Classroom Latin (iterum dīc, quid significat…?, nōn intellegō, lentius quaesō)
- Describing people/things, likes/dislikes (mihi placet)
- Food, ordering, buying (quantī cōnstat?)
- Time, days, weather, telling time (hōra quota est?)
- Narrating the past (perfect vs imperfect)
- Plans and wishes (future, volō, utinam)
- Opinions and reporting speech (putō + acc./inf., dīcit sē…)
- Requests and polite commands (fac ut…, velim + subj.)
- Hypotheticals (sī…, quid facerēs sī…)

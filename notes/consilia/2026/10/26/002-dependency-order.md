# Dependency Order: prerequisite graph and book/chapter plan

## Prerequisite graph (A → B means A before B)

```
sounds & macrons → everything
nom/acc + 3rd-person present → other cases, persons
present (all persons) of 1st/2nd conj + sum → imperfect/future → perfect system
1st/2nd decl → 3rd decl → i-stems → 4th/5th decl
1st/2nd decl adjectives + agreement → 3rd decl adjectives → comparison → present participle declension
personal pronouns → reflexive sē/suus → indirect statement (sē)
relative pronoun → relative clauses → relative of characteristic (needs subjunctive)
present passive → perfect passive (needs PPP = 4th principal part) → deponents
perfect passive participle → participles generally → ablative absolute
present infinitive (complementary) → all 6 infinitives (needs perfect stem + passive + fut. participle) → indirect statement
future active participle → future infinitive, active periphrastic
gerundive form (fut. pass. participle) → gerund/gerundive uses → passive periphrastic + dative of agent
present subjunctive → imperfect → perfect/pluperfect → sequence of tenses → indirect question, cum clauses, conditionals, indirect command, fear, quīn/quōminus
indirect statement + subjunctive → subordinate clauses in oratio obliqua
quantity/syllables (from Book I) → prosody and scansion
```

Principle: **chunks before rules.** Formulaic phrases (mihi nōmen est, quid agis, grātiās tibi agō) are learned as spoken chunks early and *analyzed* when the grammar arrives. Each chapter: ~40–80 Q/A frames, a Law/Commandment, a spoken drill, homework, ~15–25 new words.

## Plan: 6 books, 72 chapters (12 per book)

Notation: **G** grammar · **V** vocabulary domain · **S** speaking goal

### Book I: Prīma Verba (First Words). Hear it, say it, make simple sentences
1. **Salvē!** G: salvē/valē ± -te as chunks (plural imperative law), sound values, macrons. V: greetings. S: greet, name, how are you. *(EXISTS; fits. See note below.)*
2. **Quid est?** G: est/sunt, nominative sg/pl 1st & 2nd decl, gender by ending, -ne / num / nōnne preview. V: classroom & food objects. S: "Quid est? Pōmum est."
3. **Magnus et Parvus** G: 1st/2nd decl adjectives in nominative, agreement in gender/number. V: sizes, colors, tastes. S: describe things.
4. **Marcus pōmum edit** G: accusative sg/pl; nom+acc+verb; 3rd-person present (-at/-et/-it). V: food, eating verbs. S: who eats what.
5. **Ego et Tū** G: all six persons present of 1st/2nd conj + sum; personal pronoun nominatives. V: daily actions. S: talk about yourself.
6. **Audī! Venī!** G: all 4 conjugations + io-verbs, present + imperative sg/pl (generalizes Ch1's -te law), dīc/dūc/fac/fer, nōlī(te) + inf. as chunk. V: commands. S: give/obey commands.
7. **Ubi est?** G: ablative with in/ex/ab/cum/sine; place where/from. V: house & town. S: where is X.
8. **Quō īs?** G: (*quō īs? / eō* used as a chunk; analyzed in Ch32) accusative with ad/in/per etc.; place to which; locative Rōmae/domī. V: places, motion. S: where are you going.
9. **Quīnte!** G: vocative (-e, -ī); possessives meus/tuus/noster/vester. V: family. S: address people, my/your.
10. **Liber Puerī** G: genitive (possession), the five-case 1st/2nd decl table assembled. V: belongings. S: whose is this.
11. **Dā mihi pānem** G: dative (indirect object), mihi/tibi analyzed → mihi nōmen est explained (dative of possession). V: giving, table. S: asking for things politely (quaesō, amābō tē).
12. **Recapitulātiō I** Story chapter + review; 1st/2nd decl full; present system so far. S: a 2-minute self-introduction.

### Book II: Rēs Cotīdiānae (Daily Things). More nouns, past and future
13. **Rēx et Mīles** G: 3rd decl consonant stems m/f. V: people/roles.
14. **Corpus et Nōmen** G: 3rd decl neuters. V: body.
15. **Ovēs et Marē** G: 3rd decl i-stems. V: animals, nature.
16. **Heri et Crās** G: imperfect & future active (1st/2nd conj), erat/erit. V: time words. S: what you were doing / will do.
17. **Legam, Audiam** G: future/imperfect of 3rd/4th/io; future in -am/-ēs vs -bō. S: plans.
18. **Possum!** G: possum, complementary infinitive, volō/nōlō present. V: abilities, wants. S: I can / want / don't want.
19. **Hic et Ille** G: hic, ille, iste, is, īdem. S: this one, that one (shopping).
20. **Fortis et Celer** G: 3rd decl adjectives; adjective agreement across declensions (bonus rēx, fēmina fortis). S: describe people.
21. **Sē et Suum** G: full declension of ego/tū/nōs/vōs (mē, tē, nōbīs, vōbīs, meī/nostrum…); reflexive sē/suus vs eius; ipse. S: "he washes himself" / "his own".
22. **Ūnus, Duo, Trēs** G: numerals, partitive genitive, ablative of time. V: money, time, calendar. S: telling time, prices (quantī?).
23. **Manus et Diēs** G: 4th & 5th decl; all five declensions summarized. V: days, things (rēs!).
24. **Recapitulātiō II** Review + longer story.

### Book III: Fābulae (Stories). Narrating the past, relatives, passive
25. **Vēnī, Vīdī, Vīcī** G: principal parts; perfect active (all conj.). S: what did you do?
26. **Erat et Fuit** G: perfect vs imperfect aspect; narration. S: tell a story.
27. **Iam Fēceram** G: pluperfect & future perfect. S: sequencing events.
28. **Quī, Quae, Quod** G: relative pronoun, relative clauses. S: the man who…
29. **Quis Venit?** G: interrogative pronoun vs adjective; indefinites aliquis, quīdam, quisque.
30. **Amor, Amāris** G: present-system passive (all conj.); ablative of agent vs means.
31. **Amātus Est** G: perfect passive system (needs 4th principal part).
32. **Ferō, Eō, Fīō** G: irregulars ferō, eō (+compounds), fīō; full volō/nōlō/mālō.
33. **Melior et Optimus** G: comparison of adjectives (regular, irregular), quam vs abl. of comparison.
34. **Celeriter!** G: adverbs, formation and comparison. S: how/how fast/better.
35. **Ablātīvus** G: ablative uses summary (manner, accompaniment, cause, degree of difference, respect, description). 
36. **Recapitulātiō III** Adapted myth (e.g., Daedalus).

### Book IV: Verba Agentia (Verbals). Participles and infinitives
37. **Laudātus** G: perfect passive participle as adjective & clause.
38. **Laudāns** G: present active participle (3rd decl adj declension).
39. **Laudātūrus** G: future active participle; active periphrastic. S: I am about to…
40. **Urbe Captā** G: ablative absolute.
41. **Sex Īnfīnītīvī** G: all six infinitives (pres/perf/fut × act/pass).
42. **Dīcit Sē Venīre** G: indirect statement, acc. + inf., tense relations, sē. S: report what someone said; putō + inf. opinions.
43. **Loquor** G: deponent & semi-deponent verbs; their participles & imperatives; ablative with ūtor etc.
44. **Mihi Placet** G: dative with special verbs, compounds, adjectives; impersonals (licet, placet, oportet); dative of reference/purpose.
45. **Domī Mīlitiaeque** G: place & time constructions consolidated; locative; extent of time/space.
46. **Meminī, Ōdī, Coepī** G: defective verbs; inquit; genitive with verbs & adjectives.
47. **Nōlī Timēre** G: negative commands fully; future imperative; prohibition styles.
48. **Recapitulātiō IV** Adapted Roman history narrative.

### Book V: Modus Subiūnctīvus (The Subjunctive). Wishes, purposes, reported thought
49. **Eāmus!** G: present subjunctive forms; hortatory/jussive/deliberative. S: let's…
50. **Utinam!** G: optative; potential; velim/vellem. S: polite requests, wishes. (*vellem* taught as a chunk here; its imperfect-subjunctive form is analyzed in Ch52.)
51. **Ut Discam** G: purpose clauses (ut/nē, relative purpose).
52. **Tam Fessus Ut…** G: imperfect subjunctive; result clauses.
53. **Rogō Quid Faciās** G: perfect/pluperfect subjunctive; indirect questions.
54. **Ōrdō Temporum** G: sequence of tenses.
55. **Cum Vēnisset** G: cum clauses (temporal, circumstantial, causal, concessive).
56. **Imperat Ut** G: indirect commands; verbs of asking/ordering/persuading.
57. **Sī…** G: conditionals (simple, future more/less vivid, contrary-to-fact). S: what would you do if…
58. **Timeō Nē** G: fear clauses; quīn, quōminus; doubting/prevention.
59. **Dum, Antequam, Dummodo** G: temporal clauses with ind./subj.; proviso; concessive (quamquam/quamvīs); relative clause of characteristic.
60. **Recapitulātiō V** Adapted Cicero letter / Nepos life.

### Book VI: Ad Auctōrēs (To the Authors). Gerunds, oratio obliqua, poetry, real texts
61. **Ars Legendī** G: gerund.
62. **Carthāgō Dēlenda Est** G: gerundive; passive periphrastic + dative of agent; ad + gerundive purpose.
63. **Mīrābile Dictū** G: supine -um/-ū.
64. **Ōrātiō Oblīqua** G: subordinate clauses in indirect discourse; virtual oratio obliqua.
65. **Ōrdō Verbōrum** G: word order, emphasis, hyperbaton, chiasmus; reading left-to-right.
66. **Quantitās** G: syllable quantity rules; accent recap.
67. **Arma Virumque** G: dactylic hexameter scansion; elision.
68. **Odī et Amō** G: elegiac couplet; hendecasyllable; Catullus.
69. **Caesar** Reading: De Bello Gallico excerpts with frames.
70. **Phaedrus et Mārtiālis** Reading: fables & epigrams.
71. **Cicerō et Ovidius** Reading: letters, Metamorphoses excerpts.
72. **Vergilius** Reading: Aeneid I opening; course capstone.

Spoken track runs through every chapter (S goals); Book VI drills are reading-aloud and Latin discussion of the text.

## Fit of existing Chapter 1
Fits as Book I Ch1. It uses forms ahead of their grammar (2nd-person agis, agitis, datives mihi/tibi, imperatives) but explicitly as **chunks**, which matches "chunks before rules"; Ch6 generalizes the -te law and Ch11 analyzes mihi/tibi. Small follow-ups: Ch1 should mention that *salvē* replies *salvē* and *avē* as variant (L3, optional). No accuracy problems found.

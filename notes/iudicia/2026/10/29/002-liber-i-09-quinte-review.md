## L1 (blocks; fixed)
1. **The Law of the Call said "singular *-us* words → *-e*" without limits.** *Domus* (taught in Ch8, an *-us* word from another family) would then wrongly become *dome* (its vocative is *domus*); the same goes for later 4th-decl and neuter *-us* words (*manus, tempus*). Now the Law and g-02 say "*-us* words of the *hortus* family," *domus* is listed under "same," and a new g-02 frame asks "Does *domus* become *dome*?" (plus error card `i09-e-dome`).

## L2 (fixed before publishing)
1. Law row "*-ius* → *-ī*" was unqualified; *-ius* adjectives call with *-ie* (*ēgregie*). Now "*-ius* (nouns and names)."
2. Lectiō used *sōlī* ("alone"), never taught and not in Vocābula. Now *Avia et ego hīc sumus* (*hīc*, Ch5).
3. Lectiō English for *Salvēte!* was "Hello to you both!" and "Hi, everybody!", breaking the "y'all" gloss convention. Both now "Hello/Hi, y'all!"
4. g-06 "family resemblance" frame paired *meus* with *mē*, but only *ego* (Ch5) and *mēcum* (Ch7 chunk) are known. Now cites Ch5 *ego, tū, nōs, vōs* and Ch7 *mēcum, tēcum*: *meus ~ mē(cum)*, *tuus ~ tū, tē(cum)*.
5. g-03 "Who said it?" said Suetonius *reports* the Greek words. He actually says Caesar died with a groan and that *some* reported "καὶ σύ, τέκνον" (Iūl. 82). Reworded to match.
6. Speak-it-aloud item 8 said "all seven parts"; the Lectiō has eight speakers (Iōannēs, Marcus, Cornēlia, Māter, Pater, Avus, Avia, Quīntus). Fixed.
7. Frame count: draft 80, at the top of the ~40–80 guide. Cut the redundant *Iūlī* frame (g-03; *Iūlius* still appears in the Cornēlia/Iūlia naming frame), "Quīntus is a son; Cornēlia is a daughter" (g-04), and "We run into our house" (g-07). Added the *domus* frame (L1). Now 78.

## Checks
- **Accuracy:** vocatives *Marce, amīce, Quīnte, Brūte, domine, discipule, ave* (*avus*, short a, short e) vs *avē* (long ē); *-ius → -ī*: *fīlī, Cornēlī* (stress on the long ē) — distinct from nom. pl. *fīliī*; *-a* nouns unchanged (short -a; contrast abl. -ā); *-er* nouns (*puer, magister*) and neuters (*mālum*) unchanged; plural vocative = nominative (*Salvēte, discipulī/puerī/amīcī; Venīte, fīliī/līberī*). *Iōannēs* unchanged (3rd decl in Latin, gen. *Iōannis*; voc. = nom.). *Mī* = masc. voc. sg. of *meus* (*mī fīlī, mī amīce, mī frāter*); fem. *mea fīlia* unchanged. Possessives: *meus/tuus* like *magnus*; *noster, nostra, nostrum* / *vester, vestra, vestrum* like *niger* (Ch3), short o/e. Agreement with the owned thing: *fīlius meus* (Iūlia), *māter mea* (Marcus), *nōmen meum* (neuter; *nōmen mihi est* kept as the idiom). Case forms: *hortum tuum, aviam meam, in culīnā nostrā* (both ā long), *cum amīcīs meīs, in stomachum tuum* (acc. after *in* "into"), *Meā culpā* (abl. of cause in the Confiteor; flagged "a use for later"; both ā long). *nostrī* appears only as masc. nom. pl. (*amīcī nostrī*), never as the genitive of *nōs*; *vestrī* not used. Family words and macrons: *fīlius, fīlia, avus, avia, līberī* (long ī; vs *librī*), *familia* (fa-MI-li-a), *dominus, domina* (< *domus*, correct derivation), *pater* (short a), *māter, frāter* (long ā), *soror*; *Quīntus* (long ī, praenomen "fifth"; Horace = Quīntus Horātius Flaccus), *Cornēlius*, *Iūlius*, *Brūtus*. Roman naming claim (a daughter takes the feminine of the father's nomen: Cornēlius → Cornēlia) is standard.
- **Third-declension words:** *pater, māter, frāter, soror, Iōannēs* are used **only** as subjects or vocatives, where the form equals the dictionary form; no *patrem, mātre, frātris* etc. anywhere (checked text, Lectiō, drill, homework, cards). The frames say their other endings wait for Book II (3rd decl = Ch13–15). Vocābula has a note saying the same.
- **Mottos:** *Et tū, Brūte?* (Shakespeare, attributed as Latin in an English play); *Salvēte, discipulī!* (Ch1 callback); *Tū quoque, mī fīlī?* (attributed as a later Latin version; g-03 gives Suetonius's Greek report); *Avē, ave!* (simple, the chapter's pun); *Mī frāter, mī frāter, mī frāter!* (Cicero, Ad Q. fr. 1.3.1, 58 BC from exile; verified against Purser's text, which is headed MARCVS QVINTO FRATRI SALVTEM: a real Marcus writing to a real Quīntus); *Pater noster, quī es in caelīs* (liturgical Lord's Prayer; Vulgate Mt 6:9 has *quī in caelīs es*; *quī* flagged as later, *in caelīs* explained as Ch7 abl. pl.); *Meā culpā* (Confiteor); *Ubi es, Quīnte?*; *Vōx audīta perit, littera scrīpta manet* (medieval proverb); *Verba volant…, Dīcendō discimus, Gutta cavat lapidem* as before. No clash with the auto Tabellae motto.
- **Logic:** Marcus has been talked about but never addressed in Ch1–8 (grep: no *Marce* anywhere; Ch8's *Marcus! Lectiō payoff: everyone yells *Marce!* (first time ever), the real culprit is Quīntus, *Et tū, Quīnte?*.
- **Ordering:** no genitive (Ch10), no *suus* / "his, her, their" (none in Latin or English frames; the Lectiō's "your son" uses *tuus*), no dative beyond the Ch1 chunk *mihi*, no 3rd-decl forms beyond nom./voc. *Quī* only in a glossed motto. *Meā culpā*'s ablative of cause flagged as later.

## L3 (logged)
- *Marcus* has a hidden long ā (*Mārcus*: Greek Μᾶρκος, inscriptions with apex). The book has written *Marcus* since Ch4 (like Wheelock/LLPSI) and the sound check says MAR-ke; stress is unaffected. Decide with John whether to adopt *Mārcus* book-wide (would touch Ch4–9 and cards).
- Optional *Ō* before vocatives (*Ō Marce!*) not mentioned; add in Ch12 recap or later.
- *Deus* (voc. *deus*) and adjectives in *-ius* (*-ie*) are exceptions for later.
- English "Come in, friend" for *Venī, amīce* is loose (literally "Come, friend"); fine for the scene.
- Romans often omit "my" when obvious (*Mātrem amō*); could note when 3rd decl arrives.
- Sound-check motto *Vōx audīta perit, littera scrīpta manet* is about writing, not sound; acceptable.
- *Mī* is also a poetic short form of *mihi*; not mentioned to avoid confusion.

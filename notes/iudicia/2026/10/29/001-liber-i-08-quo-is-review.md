## L1 (blocks; fixed)
1. g-01 glossed *ad* with "think 'address,' 'advance'." *Advance* is a false etymology (Old French *avancer* < Late Latin *abante*, i.e. *ab* + *ante*, not *ad*). Now "think 'advent,' a coming-to" (*ad* + *venīre*, and *veniō* is known from Ch6).

## L2 (fixed before publishing)
1. Frame count: first draft had 92 frames, above the ~40–80 guide (Ch7: 71). Cut or merged 13 redundant frames (duplicate drills like *Nōs ad forum ___*, *ad hortōs*, *in hortō sum*, *per viās*, *ad forum eunt*, *Rōmam eō, why no ad*, a sound-check line; merged the *-s* "you" note, the *in* + acc. "into" answer, the *Rōmae*-looks-plural note, and the *domus*-is-feminine/Ch23 note into neighboring answers). Also dropped the frame that analyzed *e-* vs *ī-* stems ("before a vowel…"): the plan says *eō* stays a chunk until Ch32, so the chant is presented without analysis.
2. g-08 motto frame said *ubīque* = *ubi* + *-que* "wherever you like." That gloss fits *ubīvīs*, and since Ch3 taught *-que* = "and," it invited a wrong parse. Now: this *-que* is not "and"; it makes "every-where"; note the long ī (vs short *ubi*).
3. Locative Law said "*-ae* for *-a* towns, *-ī* for *-us/-um* towns" without limits; plural town names (*Athēnae → Athēnīs*) break it. Added a caveat ("towns with plural names work differently; later"). Likewise the "which nouns skip the preposition" answer now says "plus a couple more, later" (small islands, *rūs*), so it isn't stated as exhaustive.
4. *Īte, missa est* was called "the last words of the old Latin Mass." In the Tridentine rite the blessing and Last Gospel follow it. Now "the dismissal at the end of the Latin Mass."
5. "Old English had all three: where, whence, whither": *whence/whither* are Middle/Early Modern English. Now "Older English."
6. Homework "Fix these" item: *In domum eō* is not wrong in itself (*in domum Caesaris*, into a particular house) and *In culīnam sum* needed its intended meaning. Added glosses ("I'm going home," "I'm in the kitchen") so each item has exactly one fix. The error card `i08-e-in-domum` carries the gloss too.
7. g-03 "People work too." was ambiguous (*labōrō* = work, Ch5). Now "*Ad* works with people too."

## Checks
- **Accuracy:** *eō, īs, it, īmus, ītis, eunt* (ī long in *īs, īmus, ītis*; *it* short; *eō* e short + ō long; *eunt* two syllables e-unt); imperative *ī, īte*; infinitive *īre*. *quō* (long ō). Prepositions + acc.: *ad* (to/toward; with a town = "to the vicinity of," A&G 428a), *in* (into/onto, motion), *per*, *circum* (acc. even at rest: *circum mēnsam sedent*), *trāns* (long ā). *usque* (short u). Place constructions: *domum / domī / domō* without preposition; *Rōmam / Rōmae / Rōmā*; *Capuam / Capuae / Capuā*; *Tarentum / Tarentī / Tarentō* (locative *Tarentī*, Livy). *Rōmae* = ROH-mye (ae = "eye," Ch1). Accusatives: *tabernam/tabernās, hortum, lūdum, thermās, Marcum, culīnam, mēnsam, templum, hortōs, stomachum, silvam, fenestram, viam/viās, māla*. Neuter acc. pl. *māla* = nom. pl. (*mālum*, long ā, vs *mala* "bad things," Ch1). *Domus* f., declension deferred to Ch23 (4th/5th decl in Book II). Macrons checked: *lūdus, templum, silva, fenestra, thermae* (short e), *domus/domī/domō, Rōma, trāns, circum, per, ad, usque, quō, vādis* (*vādō*, long ā), *ubīque* (long ī), *quī, ūnō, diē, aedificāta, omnēs, dūcunt, aspera, astra, missa*.
- **Mottos:** *Quō vādis?* (Vulgate John 13:36, *Domine, quō vādis?*); *Īte, missa est* (Mass dismissal; John's Catholic-school Latin, *missa* → "Mass"); *Ab ōvō usque ad māla* (Horace Sat. 1.3.6–7; Ch7's teaser now decoded); *In culīnā sedeō; in culīnam currō* (simple Latin, the chapter's core contrast); *Per aspera ad astra* (proverb, decoded in g-05); *Domī sum* (simple); *Omnēs viae Rōmam dūcunt* (medieval proverb; *omnēs* flagged for Book II); *Nusquam est quī ubīque est* (Seneca, Ep. 2.2, decoded in g-08); *Quō īs? Ad tabernam!*; *Rōma nōn ūnō diē aedificāta est* (medieval proverb); *Verba volant…, Dīcendō discimus, Gutta cavat lapidem* as before. No clash with the auto Tabellae motto.
- **Logic:** *ubi / unde* (Ch7) → missing "where to" → *quō*; *Quō īs? Eō ad hortum* → *hortum* recognized as Ch4's accusative → pattern induced (where/from = abl., where to = acc.) before any Law. *eō* chant built from Ch5 endings (only *eunt* is new-looking). *ad* + acc. drilled sg/pl; *ad* vs inside. *in culīnā* vs *in culīnam* contrast, then Law of the Switch; Ch7's *in hortōs sumus* error card paid off (*in hortōs currimus*). *per/circum/trāns*, with the honest caveat that only *in* switches (*circum* is acc. at rest). *domum/domī/domō* as chunks → locative named → cities on the same pattern, Law of Home and Rome table. Sorting drill with all three questions. Payoffs: *ad māla*, *in hortōs*, *ubīque* hides *ubi*, *Īte, missa est*, Lectiō punch line *ā cāseō usque ad cāseum*.
- **Ordering:** *eō* forms as a chant, analysis deferred to Ch32 (stated); no genitive (Lectiō says "in his stomach" in English only; no *Marcī*), no vocative of *-us* nouns (*Iōannēs, Iūlia, Cornēlia* unchanged forms; Marcus never addressed), no dative, no 3rd decl in running text (*omnēs* only in a glossed motto, flagged; *Athēnae* only named). *Domus* declension → Ch23; full place constructions (*rūs*, islands, plural towns, extent) → Ch45. *Rōmā* "from Rome" included because it fits the *domō* pattern and Ch7's *unde* (STATE allowed it "if it fits").

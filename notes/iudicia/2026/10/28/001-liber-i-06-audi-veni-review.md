## L1 (blocks; fixed)
1. g-08 claimed flatly that "Romans didn't say" *nōn* + imperative. Prohibitions with *nē* + imperative do occur (early Latin, poetry); the accurate claim is that ordinary prose uses *nōlī* + infinitive. Reworded: "Not the everyday Roman way. In ordinary prose they used *nōlī* plus the infinitive."
2. Law of the Little i said "No long vowels." False as stated (*legō* has a long ō; *scrībō*, *dīcō* have long stem vowels). Now "The family vowel is … never long."

## L2 (fixed before publishing)
1. g-02 called *ēs* ("you eat") the "short twin" of *edis*, right before contrasting it with short *es*. Confusing: *ēs* is long. Now "a shorter twin too: *ēs*, with a long ē."
2. g-04 *Quid facis?* → "Answer: dinner." answered with *Cēnam parō* and the claim that Romans only "prepared" dinner, dodging the target verb. *Cēnam facere* is good Latin. Now *Cēnam faciō.* with *Cēnam parō* as an alternative.
3. The Second Law of *-te* presented "-e for one" as a new rule, inconsistent with the Law of the Clipped Command (which already produces *lege* from *legere*). Rewritten: clipping still works for one; only the plural changes *e* → *i* (*-ite*).
4. "Penult" was used without definition (also in Ch5 g-09). Now defined at first use in g-01: "the next-to-last syllable."
5. "Imperative" was defined only inside a frame; added a "Grammar words, in plain English" box after the Clipped Command law (infinitive and 3rd/4th conjugation already had one).
6. Cards: error card id `i06-e-noli-timē` contained a macron; ids are ASCII. Now `i06-e-noli-time`.

## Checks
- **Accuracy:** 3rd conj *legō, legis, legit, legimus, legitis, legunt* (short i, u before -nt; stress LE-gi-mus); *edō, edis … edunt*, *bibō*, *emō*, *agō* (Ch1 *agitis* re-read as 3rd conj), *intellegitis*. 4th conj *audiō, audīs, audit, audīmus, audītis, audiunt* (ī shortened before final -t and before a vowel), *veniō, dormiō, sciō*. -iō verbs *capiō, capis, capit, capimus, capitis, capiunt*; *faciō*. Dictionary markers *-āre/-ēre/-ere/-īre*. Imperatives *amā/amāte, vidē/vidēte, audī/audīte, lege/legite, cape/capite, ede/edite, bibe/bibite, scrībe/scrībite, curre/currite, age/agite*; *dīc, dūc, fac, fer* with *dīcite, dūcite, facite, ferte*; *nōlī, nōlīte* + infinitive. *ēs* (long, "you eat", athematic form of *edō*) vs *es* (short, "you are"); *venī* (short e, imperative) vs *vēnī* (long ē, perfect). Macrons checked on *dīc, dūc, hūc, statim, nesciō, scīre, nōlīte, pōculum, lentius, quaesō*. Pronunciation: v = w (WE-ni-oh), c = k (FA-ki-oh, DEE-ki-te), sc = sk (SKEE-re); church habits flagged each time.
- **Mottos:** *Nōn vītae sed scholae discimus* (Seneca, Ep. 106.12, his original complaint; the school motto inverts it); *Repetītiō māter studiōrum* (proverb); *Audīmus et discimus* (simple Latin); *Fugit irreparābile tempus* (Virgil, G. 3.284); *Cavē canem* (Pompeii doorway mosaic, House of the Tragic Poet); *Carpe diem* (Horace, Odes 1.11.8); *Dīc, dūc, fac, fer* (classroom mnemonic); *Nōlī mē tangere* (John 20:17, Vulgate); *Vēnī, vīdī, vīcī* (Caesar after Zela, 47 BC; Suetonius, Iul. 37); *Festīnā lentē* (Augustus, Suetonius Aug. 25, said in Greek σπεῦδε βραδέως); *Sapere audē* (Horace, Ep. 1.2.40); *Verba volant…*, *Dīcendō discimus*, *Gutta cavat lapidem* as in prior chapters. No duplication of the auto Tabellae motto.
- **Logic:** 3rd-conj endings induced from *edō* (Ch4's *edit/edunt* + Ch5's six endings); dictionary short *-ere* vs long *-ēre* contrasted with macrons; 4th conj contrasted with 3rd via "you/we" forms; -iō verbs induced as a mix of both; imperative induced from Ch1 *salvē/valē* and Ch5's *Audī, vidē, tacē* (clip -re), plural via Ch1's -te law; *legite* induced from *legitis*; the four rebels as exceptions; *nōlī* + infinitive; obey-a-command → answer -ō / -mus; payoff *venī* vs *vēnī*.

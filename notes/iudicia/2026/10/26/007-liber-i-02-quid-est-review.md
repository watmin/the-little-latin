## Checks
- **Accuracy:** every form and macron checked: mālum/māla, mala, pōmum, ōvum/ōva, vīnum, pirum/pira, cāseus/cāseī, stilus/stilī, discipulus/-ī, discipula/-ae, liber/librī, līber, magister/magistrī, magistra/-ae, puer/puerī, puella/-ae, mēnsa/-ae, sella, tabula, aqua, ūva/-ae, agricola, nauta (m.), est/sunt, -ne, nōnne, num, ita, minimē, quis/quid. Mottos: *Ab ōvō usque ad māla* (Horace, Satires 1.3.6–7), *Ē plūribus ūnum*, *Suum cuique*, *Nōmina rērum*, *Quaerite et inveniētis* (Vulgate, Matthew 7:7; the plural imperative glossed "y'all"), *Fēstīnā lentē*, *Vīva vōx*, *Gutta cavat lapidem* (Ovid, Ex Ponto 4.10.5), *Ūsus est optimus magister* (proverb), *Ōre, nōn oculīs*. Stress: EST-ne (the stress falls before the enclitic), dis-KI-pu-lus, MĀ-la vs. MA-la.
- **Logic:** each rule is induced before it's named: -us→-ī from Ch1's *discipulī*; -a→-ae; -um→-a; then "number"; then the three families → "gender"; the -a ambiguity → Law of the Neuter Plural; the naming form → "case"/"nominative". Every grammar term gets a plain-English definition and its old-school name (noun = *nōmen*, enclitic, neuter = "neither").
- **Ordering:** only Ch1 material plus new nominatives. The vocative *Quīnte* appears once, flagged as a form to just hear (taught in Ch9). *Iōannēs* is used unchanged. Pānis is used as a known Ch1 word, without its 3rd-declension plural. No accusative, adjectives, or other persons.
- **Structure:** every Q|A table has a g-NN marker and every ## section has a motto; strict build passes; nav and the Liber I page are updated. Homework uses `- [ ]`. No "cover with your hand" wording.
- **Usefulness:** speaking: naming real objects with est/sunt; asking and answering yes/no questions. Reading: neuter-plural/feminine-singular disambiguation, the first real case concept, and Horace and Ovid in the mottos.

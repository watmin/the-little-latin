- Six new terms, each in plain words with the old-school name: **ablative of accompaniment** ("with whom"), **of manner** ("how"), **of cause** ("why"), **of degree of difference** ("by how much"; "measure of difference"), **of respect** ("in what respect"; "specification"), **of description** ("what kind"; "quality").
- No new endings: g-02 reviews Ch23's five ablatives (+ *marī*, *fortī*, *altiōre*, glued *mēcum/vōbīscum/quōcum*).
- Organizing idea: **Law of the Three Old Jobs** (ablative = old "from" + "with/by" + "in/at" cases; A&G §§398 ff. "ablative proper / instrumental / locative"); the earlier jobs sorted into it (agent and comparison under "from", price under "with/by", time when under "in/at"); cause flagged as straddling ("from fear").
- **Law of the Three Withs** separates company (*cum* + person), manner (*cum* + abstract noun; *cum* optional with an adjective, *silentiō* alone) and tool (plain, Ch30). **Law of the Six Questions** = sorting habit + table of all 13 jobs.
- Payoffs: Ch7's promise "picks up more jobs in later books", Ch7 *sine cūrā* → *cum cūrā*; Ch32 *auferō… ablātum* (title) and the Lectiō pun *Cāseus ablātus est! - Genitive of description (*vir magnae virtūtis*, *modicae fideī*, *puer decem annōrum*) shown as recognition only.
- *Fēstīnātiō* written *fēstīnātiōne* to match the book's *Fēstīnā* (still an open quantity question in STATE; dictionaries give *festīnō*).

## L1 (blocks; fixed)
1. **Unverified claim:** g-08 said "Caesar's war reports are full of *paulō post*." Checked *B.G.* 1–7 on The Latin Library: one occurrence (Book 4). → "*Paulō post* is a storyteller's friend: put it next to Chapter 26's *deinde* and *tandem*."
2. **Unverified claim:** g-07 called *Quā dē causā?* "Caesar's favorite 'why?'". In *B.G.* 1 it occurs once, and as a relative connective ("for this reason"), not a question. → "A handy 'why?'", with the relative use pointed to §9 (Caesar 1.1).
3. **Wrong history:** g-06 had Bias "fleeing his burning city with nothing in his hands." Cicero (*Paradoxa* 1.8) and Ch20 say his city fell and the others fled with their belongings while he took nothing. → retold to match Ch20; motto attribution aligned with Ch20's style ("Bias of Priene, via Cicero, *Paradoxes of the Stoics* 1.8").
4. **Invented canon:** blurb *Quīntus nātū minimus*, g-09 *Avus nātū maximus*, g-11 *Avus nātū maximus est*, homework *Avia nātū maior est quam Avus* asserted family ages the story never set (Cornēlia's age vs. Quīntus; Grandpa vs. Grandma). → *Quīntus nātū minor* (Ch9: little brother), *Avus nātū maior est quam Pater*; *nātū maximus* taught generically.
5. **Wrong grammar claim:** g-10 said "size" leans to the genitive of description, contradicting the chapter's own Suetonius *excelsā statūrā*. → "measurements, ages, and numbers" lean genitive (A&G §415: measure is genitive; physical traits ablative).

## L2 (fixed before publishing)
1. Lectiō motto and last line *Medice, cūrā tē ipsum* (Luke 4:23): *cūrā* = imperative of *cūrō*, a homograph of today's ablative *cūrā*, unflagged. → trap note in the Lectiō intro; *cūrō* in the glossed list.
2. Lectiō *Medicum quaerite!* glossed "Find the doctor" → "Look for the doctor, you two!" (*quaerō* = look for, Ch17).
3. Lectiō *Marcus | Paulō post… ecce medicus! Nōbīscum venit.* put narration in the mouth of a boy who is out fetching the doctor. → *Avia | Paulō post… Ecce! Medicus cum puerīs venit!*; g-13 company list updated (*cum puerīs*).
4. Lectiō *Salvēte!* glossed "Hello, all!" → "Hello, y'all!" (John's plural-you preference).
5. g-08 "Latin measures the gap with *paulō, multō*, not *paulum, multum*" was too absolute (*multum* + comparative occurs in poetry/late Latin). → "classical prose"; and the error deck avoids *multum maior* (uses *multus maior*).
6. g-09 *Marcus pede claudus est* "since Chapter 34's tumble": Ch34 hurt his **nose**. → "suppose it had been his foot."
7. g-01 motto gloss "Carry away, carried away" did not match the four keys. → "I carry away, to carry away, I carried away, carried away."
8. g-03 said today's jobs are "almost all" instrumental without noting that cause is filed under the "from" job by many grammars. → one sentence added. "True ablative" → "ablative proper" (A&G's term).
9. g-09 Helvetians line lacked its context (*quā dē causā* points back to the Belgians' fighting the Germans, which John read in Ch33). → tied to Ch33's *Hōrum omnium fortissimī sunt Belgae*.
10. g-01/blurb misquoted Ch7 ("pick up more jobs"); Ch7 says "it picks up more jobs in later books." → exact quote.

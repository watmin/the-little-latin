- Rule 1 is stated with "naming form ends in *-is* or *-ēs*" so *pater, patris* (two and two) is correctly excluded; *mīles, mīlitis* (unequal) excluded; *Iōannēs, Iōannis* jokingly "qualifies on paper." Rule 2 says "ends in *-s* or *-x*" so *pater* (*patr-is*, two consonants) is again excluded and *rēx/dux/pāx/vōx* (one consonant) fall out.
- Rebels: *canis → canum* (drilled); *mūs, mūris* (one consonant, yet *mūrium/mūrum*; recognition only). Optional acc. pl. *-īs* and abl. sg. *-ī* of *nāvis, ignis, avis, cīvis*: recognition only, with a look-alike warning against Book I's *-īs*.
- *Os, ossis* (Ch14's contrast word) now in Vocābula: i-stem by rule 2 (*ossium*) but not an *-e/-al/-ar* neuter, so *in osse, ossa*. Lectiō payoff *Os in ōre habet* (bone in his mouth).
- *Lītus, lītoris* added as a Law-of-the-Disguise payoff and a contrast to the Law of the Sea (*in lītore*, not *-ī*).
- *Pānis, pānis* (Ch1/Ch11) named as an equal-syllabled cousin; its gen. pl. (which wobbles) is not shown. *Mēns, mentis* pays off Ch14's *Mēns sāna*.
- Adjectives: only 1st/2nd-decl adjectives on i-stem nouns (*ovis alba, mare magnum et caeruleum, multae ovēs, canis parvus, Cīvis Rōmānus*), continuing Ch14's "same case, not same letters" precedent. No 3rd-decl adjectives; *omnis* only inside Caesar's quotation, glossed as "all" like *Omnēs viae*; *trēs* signposted to Ch22; *dīvīsa, dormienda* glossed in quotations.
- Story: *Ad mare*. Family day at the sea with the puppy Cāseus (*canis parvus*); sheep on the hill, Marcus *rēx piscium*, a *rāra avis* (seagull) steals the big cheese and flies *in nūbibus*; fire on the shore at night; Cāseus gets a bone instead; gag ends *in stomachō… avis!* (gen. = nom. for an equal-syllabled noun, made into a comprehension frame).

## L1 (blocks)
1. **Wrong grammar claim (absolute):** the Law of the Sea said "*in marī*, never *in mare*." Poets occasionally use ablative *mare* (e.g. Lucretius, Ovid). Now "not *in mare*; a poet bends this once in a while."

## L2 (fixed before publishing)
1. **Over length:** 82 frames, above the ~75-80 target. Merged the "One sheep or many?" title frame into the *Pāsce ovēs meās* frame, the *pars* frame into the Caesar frame, the *vulpēs* i-stem frame into the *Vulpēs pilum mūtat* frame, and cut the redundant "I see bodies / I see seas" frame: 78, then 79 with the Lectiō frame added in 6.
2. **Unanswerable question (Logic):** g-06 asked for *mare*'s gender with "Hint: Chapter 14," but nothing in Ch14 lets a learner induce that an *-e* noun is neuter. Gender now given in the question; the frame asks for "I see the sea" (Neuter Twins).
3. **Unclear table cell:** the i-stem table's nominative ending read "*-is, -ēs* or (varies)". Now "*-is, -ēs*, or short (*urbs*)".
4. **Look-alike trap unflagged:** the optional acc. pl. *-īs* (*ovīs, urbīs*) looks exactly like Book I's dative/ablative plural *-īs* (*puellīs, hortīs*). Added the warning; also "in poets and older prose" (not poets only).
5. **Skipped step in a cultural payoff:** *Cīvis Rōmānus sum* "should have saved him" did not say why. Now names Verres and adds "no Roman citizen could lawfully be flogged."
6. **Lectiō never used the chapter's key form:** no *-ium* anywhere in the story. Added Marcus's line *In marī ego rēx piscium sum!* (Ch13's king game, at sea) and a g-08 frame on *piscium*.

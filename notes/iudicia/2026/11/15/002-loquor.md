- Terms: **deponent** ("looks passive but means active"; *dēpōnō* "lay aside": usual explanation = laid aside the passive meaning, kept the look), **semi-deponent** ("half-deponent": active present system, deponent-style perfect system). Ch30's *videor* used as the contrast (true passive of *videō* vs no *loquō*).
- Three Laws: **Laid-Aside Voice** (passive endings, active meaning; first key in *-or*; five-family chant table *hortor, vereor, loquor, patior, mentior*), **Having-Done** (all deponent participles active; the perfect participle inside the third key = "having …ed"; ordinary *laudō* vs *sequor* table), **Five Ablative Verbs** (*ūtor, fruor, fungor, potior, vescor*; ablative of means per A&G §410; PUFFV given as "a memory word some teachers use"). Semi-deponents in an info table, not a Law.
- Three keys (*loquor, loquī, locūtus sum*); second key = Ch30 present passive infinitive form; all tenses (imperfect *loquēbātur*, future *loquar/hortābor*, *loqueris/loquēris* = Ch30 *legeris/legēris* trap, *locūtus eram/erō*); three active infinitives *loquī, locūtus esse, locūtūrus esse*; *Arbitror Avum mentīrī*, *pollicētur sē nōn mentītūrum esse*; imperatives *loquere/loquiminī* (singular looks like an active infinitive: trap), *hortāre, verēre, mentīre*; *nōlī loquī*; ablative absolutes *Avō loquente*, *Sōle oriente / ortō*; *Oriēns* = the East.

## L1 (blocks; fixed)
1. **Wrong case after *ūtor* in a motto (Vocābula):** "*Verba nova ūsus sum*": the chapter's own Law says *ūtor* takes the ablative. → "*Verbīs novīs ūsus sum*."

## L2 (fixed before publishing)
1. **Intro promised an untaught verb:** "speak, follow, **try**, suffer…": *cōnor* is not in the chapter. → "promise" (*polliceor*, taught in g-05).
2. **Etymology muddled (g-01):** "the old grammarians said these verbs had laid aside their active forms" (the usual textbook explanation is the passive *meaning*; and "no active forms" then contradicts the active-looking participles). → "the usual explanation is that these verbs have laid aside their passive meaning and kept only the passive look."
3. **Wrong cross-reference (g-01):** "exceptions in section 6": the active-looking future infinitive *locūtūrus esse* is in section 5. → "apart from a few active-looking forms in sections 5 and 6."
4. **Notation clash (g-06, Law of the Having-Done, g-07):** "fourth-key participle" for deponents right after section 3 taught that they have **three** keys. → "perfect participle (the one inside the third key, *secūtus sum*)"; g-06 frame 1 adds "from the third key, *locūtus sum*"; g-07 "the perfect participle".
5. **Wrong cross-reference (g-06 motto frame):** "a participle used as people (Chapter 38)": the "participle as people" frame is Ch39's *moritūrī*. → "like Chapter 39's *moritūrī*."
6. **Unverified claim (Law of the Five Ablative Verbs):** "some old books say PUFFV". → "A memory word some teachers use: PUFFV." The ablative-of-means explanation in g-11 reworded to match A&G §410 ("Grammar books file it under Chapter 30's ablative of means").
7. **Awkward, half-joking prompt (g-08):** "'Don't be afraid…' well, 'Fear!'" → "'Urge him!' 'Be wary!' And, from Grandpa's mischief, 'Lie!'" with *Hortāre eum!*
8. **Confusing question (g-14):** "Is anyone being followed by Grandpa here? Yes, the cheese!" (mixes active and passive in the very frame meant to separate them). → "Does *sequor* mean Grandpa is being followed? **No.** … 'I'm following,' not 'I'm being followed.'"
9. **Doubtful idiom (Lectiō):** Grandpa's *Hodiē vērum locūtus sum* (*vērum dīcere* is the taught idiom; *vērum loquī* singular not verified). → *Hodiē nōn mentītus sum!* "Today I didn't lie!" (also lands the *mentior* thread).
10. **Accuracy (g-11):** Cicero's *abūtēre* explained with Ch30's warning, which was about **poets**; Cicero is prose. → "*-re* can stand for *-ris*, and not only in poets."

L3 fixed while there: Lectiō English "I was here talking" → "I've been here talking" (*locūtus sum*); *moritūrus* note "not *mortuūrus*" → "it isn't built from *mortuus*"; unused *admīror* removed from the glossed list (the *Nīl admīrārī* frame was cut pre-draft for length).

## L1 (blocks; fixed)
1. **Misattributed motto (§4):** *Factum fierī īnfectum nōn potest* was labeled "proverb, after Terence, *Phormio* 1034". The line is Plautus, *Aulularia* 741: *factum est illud: fierī īnfectum nōn potest* (checked). → motto now quotes Plautus exactly; the g-04 frame rewritten to contrast *factum est* (two pieces) with *īnfectum* (a describer with *fierī*).
2. **Missing macron:** §9 blurb began *Infantem* (long ī). → *Īnfantem*.
3. **Wrong grammar claim (g-11):** *Hoc dictum est bonum* was offered as an unambiguous one-piece "this saying is good", but it reads just as well as two pieces ("this has been said (as) good"). → replaced with *Amō hoc dictum* (object of *amō*, unambiguous), plus the rule "if *est* could pair with the participle, read it as two pieces". Choose card rewritten to match.

## L2 (fixed before publishing)
1. **Livy history (g-08):** said Brennus "said" *Vae victīs* while "the Romans weighed out gold". Livy has an unnamed *insolens Gallus*, the Romans complaining about false weights, and the words "heard". → rewritten with the complaint, "a Gaul (later writers say their chief, Brennus)", and "the Romans heard".
2. ***Rēs gestae* claims (§8 motto, g-08):** called the "title" and said "carved in bronze outside his tomb". The Ancyra heading actually begins *Rērum gestārum dīvī Augustī…*; Suetonius has bronze tablets set before the Mausoleum. → "the usual name of Augustus's own account", "to be cut on bronze tablets in front of his tomb (a stone copy survives in Ankara)".
3. **Lectiō untaught construction:** John's *Ō cāseum ab omnibus laudātum!* used the accusative of exclamation, never taught. → *Cāseus ab omnibus laudātus…* (lead-in to the *in stomachō* refrain).
4. **g-13 inaccuracy:** called *Rēs gestae* "a participle phrase as a noun" (*rēs* is the noun; *gestae* describes it). → "*gestae* describes *rēs*, 'things carried out', so 'deeds'".
5. **Lectiō line without a participle:** Mom's list *trēs librī lēctī, multa capita, multī cāseī servātī* left *capita* bare in a list meant to model headline participles. → *multa capita lēcta*.
6. **Law of the Grown-up Key** plural line omitted the neuter genitive *-ōrum*. → "*-ōrum, -ārum, -ōrum · -īs* (all genders)".
7. **Notation:** "Ch28's first line of the *Aeneid*" broke the "Chapter NN" style. → "Chapter 28's".
8. ***Satur*** used in the Lectiō but glossed only in the end list. → added to the Lectiō intro.
9. **Caesar snip unflagged (g-07):** the quoted clause follows a consular-date ablative absolute (*M. Messalā… cōnsulibus*); silently cutting it could confuse a reader checking the text. → "Caesar's sentence opens with the year, which we skip."
10. **Repeated motto:** Horace's *Laudātur ab hīs, culpātur ab illīs* stood as the §1 motto, inside g-05, and (adapted) as the Lectiō motto. → §1 motto now Ch31's headline *Cāseus ēsus!* "Cheese eaten!"

## L1 (fixed)
1. Lectiō: *Lūdum faciāmus* for "let's play a game" is not idiomatic (*lūdum facere* is not "play a game"). → *Lūdāmus*.
2. §15 said the Ecclesiastes passage was "the opening lines … unchanged," but 3:3b and 3:5–6 are omitted. → says exactly which verses are given and which left out; homework item adjusted.
3. §6 Caesar 4.23.1 was called "the same night as Chapter 59's ships at anchor" (4.23.4 is the next day, waiting till the ninth hour). → "the start of the crossing that ends with Chapter 59's ships waiting at anchor."

## L3 (logged)
- Ovid AA 1.1 printed *Sī quis* (Latin Library has *Siquis*); same words.
- Motto *Edimus ad vīvendum…* attributed loosely to "an old saying of Socrates" (Latin versions use *ut* clauses: Rhet. Her. 4.39).
- Dative gerund only mentioned (rare); *inter / ob* + gerund not mentioned.
- *Gutta cavat lapidem* later ending: origin left as "someone later."
- Mark 4:9 *Quī habet aurēs audiendī, audiat* skipped: the Nova Vulgata (used in earlier chapters) reads *Quī habet aurēs, audiat*.
- Gerund + object after *ad* (*ad irrīdendum eum*) is shown as real Nepos; Ch62 should explain why Romans usually swap to the gerundive there.
- Lectiō 28 lines; the reading game could return in Ch62 (*Carthāgō dēlenda est* = Cato's cheese?).

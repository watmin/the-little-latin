## L1 (blocks; fixed)
1. **Wrong source claim (g-10 motto):** "a later proverb; Cicero says it first, *Philippics* 12.5." Cicero's sentence (*Cuiusvīs hominis est errāre…*) is a version of an older commonplace (Greek and Latin), not the first, and not the proverb's wording. → "(a later proverb; the thought is in Cicero, *Philippics* 12.5)"; the frame keeps "The famous short form came much later."

## L2 (fixed before publishing)
1. **Continuity (Lectiō):** Grandpa's *cāseum vīcīnī ēdisse feror* ("a neighbor's cheese") contradicted Ch26, where his boyhood theft was his mother's whole cheese from the kitchen. → *Mē puerō, cāseum tōtum ēdisse feror…*; English reordered ("I'm said to have eaten a whole cheese when I was a boy…").
2. **Logic (Lectiō):** John hid the cheese in the cupboard (no box mentioned), but the morning line said *capsa vacua est*. → *Māne armārium vacuum est!* / "the cupboard is empty."
3. **Keep-out (g-09):** quoted *Ō nāta mēcum cōnsule Mānliō* (Odes 3.21.1); *nāta* is the participle of deponent *nāscor* (Ch43; Ch40 dropped *Dominō cooperante* for the same reason). → told in English, keeping only *cōnsule Mānliō* (Ch40 year-naming; 65 BC verified, *Mānlius* long *ā* per Wiktionary).
4. **Untaught reference (g-03):** "Like the short *amāstī* for *amāvistī*": *amāstī* was never taught (open Ch25 L3). → "The *-vi-* or *-ve-* simply drops out."
5. **Term not defined (g-06):** the sixth form was never given its grammar name. → "Grammar books call *laudātum īrī* the **future passive infinitive**… 'the "to be about to be ___ed" form.'"
6. **Ambiguous *quam* (g-09, Aeneid 1.15):** John has three *quam*s (than, how, relative). → gloss "*Quam*: Chapter 28's relative, 'which,' pointing back to Carthage."
7. **Incomplete list (g-02):** "Which have a passive infinitive? *Ferrī*" contradicted section 6's *īrī*. → "and *īre* has a rare one (section 6)."
8. **Wrong cross-reference (g-10):** "Chapter 32's motto, seen again in Chapters 33 and 35": *Errāre hūmānum est* is first a motto in Ch27 (Ch33 has the longer *corrigere optimum* version). → "The fix-it motto of Chapters 27, 32, and 35."
9. **Unclear grammar wording (g-11):** "Chapter 18's complementary infinitive keeps the subject in the nominative" (the subject is nominative anyway; the point is the predicate adjective). → "With a complementary infinitive (Chapter 18), a describing word agrees with the main subject, in the nominative."
10. **Clumsy explanation (g-04):** "agrees with whatever 'has been' done to." → "agrees with whoever or whatever the action was done to."
11. **Wrong citation (g-03):** "Even the wild verbs are tame in the perfect (Chapter 25)": that point was made in Ch32 g-01. → "(Chapter 32: the perfect system is always regular)."
12. **Motto attribution (g-11):** "North Carolina's state motto, after Sallust": the state traditionally cites Cicero, *On Friendship* 98, which has the same words. → "(the words are in Sallust and Cicero)"; Cicero's *nōn tam multī … esse quam vidērī volunt* added to the NC frame (verified, *Amic.* 98).
13. **Consistency (g-06 motto):** *Rāra avis* attribution style differed from Ch15 (*Rāra avis in terrīs*, Juvenal, *Satires* 6.165). → matched.
14. **Glossed list (Vocābula):** listed *Sallustius* (never in the text) and lacked *tam … quam*, *corrēxī*. → fixed.
15. **Cards:** choose card `iv41-c-esus` had the distractor *Cāseus ēsse vidētur* (*ēsse* is a real archaic "to eat," untaught); `iv41-c-fore` had *forem* (subjunctive, keep-out). → *Cāseus edī vidētur*; *esse*.

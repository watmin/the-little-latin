Decisions recorded:
- One new term, **ablative absolute** ("the loose-standing ablative"; *absolūtus* "set loose", English *absolve*); the old-school name is the same everywhere, so no second synonym; English "nominative absolute" named for contrast (*Weather permitting*).
- Three Laws: **Untied Pair** (noun + participle both ablative, agreeing, loose; perfect = before, present = same time with *-e*), **Unshared Noun** (the noun has no other job; else the participle agrees in that case, Ch37–38), **Missing "Being"** (no participle of *sum*: *Cicerōne cōnsule, mē puerō, Avō invītō, Caesare duce*). Many Englishes reprinted as "absolute edition" (when/after/while, because, although + *tamen*, with, "having ___ed").
- "Having bought" payoff: Ch37's trap (no active perfect participle) answered by *Cāseō emptō, Pater domum vēnit* = "Having bought the cheese, Dad came home."
- Future participle in the ablative absolute: one line, "rare, mostly later writers; recognize only."
- Year naming by consuls as the cultural hook (Ch37's skipped Orgetorix date paid off).
- *Deō volente* labeled "a later Latin phrase" (no ancient source claimed), paired with the verified Vulgate James 4:15 *Sī Dominus voluerit… faciēmus hoc aut illud* (Ch27 future perfect). *Cēterīs paribus* and *sēde vacante* labeled later usage. *Ab urbe conditā* used as the preposition look-alike.
- Storyline: *diē octāvā*, the day after Ch39. Grandma bought a replacement cheese (*Cāseō hesternō ā custōde ēsō, alium ēmī*); Grandpa banned from dinner (*Avō absente cēnābimus · Mē invītō?! · Tē invītō*); kids build a city of chairs and pillows to guard the cheese; John is consul (*Mē cōnsule, urbs tūta erit*), Cornelia leads the Gauls (*Mē duce*), Grandpa joins the Gauls (*Cāseō captō, partem habēbō*), Dad *Mē puerō… Cantābō, vōs adiuvāns* → *Nōlī, Pater!*; *Signō datō… Avō clāmante, sellae cadunt*; *Patre cantante, pugnāre nōn possum*; *Urbe captā*, box empty, Grandpa accused but John's alibi *Avum pugnantem semper vidēbam* (Unshared Noun in action); *nōbīs omnibus pugnantibus*, *Portā apertā, canis in urbem vēnit*; *In stomachō… Cāseī*; Grandpa *Mē puerō, canēs cāseum nōn edēbant!* / Grandma *Tē puerō, Ave, tū cāseum omnium edēbās!*; John *Crās, Deō volente…*; Dad *Cāseō āmissō, cantābō!* → *Nōlī, Pater!*

## L2 (fixed before publishing)
1. **Edition mismatch (g-10):** Matthew 14:23 was quoted from the Nova Vulgata (*dīmissīs turbīs*) while the Clementine has *Et dīmissā turbā, ascendit in montem sōlus orāre*; Matthew 26:20 likewise differs (*cum duodecim discipulīs suīs* in the Clementine). → 14:23 quoted as *dīmissā turbā… * with "(newer editions: *dīmissīs turbīs*)"; 26:20 cut to *discumbēbat cum duodecim…* with "some editions add *discipulīs suīs*"; capitalized *Duodecim* lowered and *duodecim* glossed as *duo* + *decem* (12 is not in Ch22's numbers).
2. **Wrong sequence claim (g-09):** "Before Chapter 39's grain-burning came the packing": in *B.G.* the grain burning is 1.5.3 and *Omnibus rēbus… comparātīs* is 1.6, after it; and the "Goodbye, Helvetians" answer said they were "packed in Chapter 39's line." → both reworded (grain burned in 1.5.3; packing done in 1.6; war closed in 1.30.1).
3. **Muddled explanation (g-08):** "Two ablative absolutes' worth of names" for *M. Messallā, M. Pīsōne cōnsulibus*. → "One ablative absolute (two names + *cōnsulibus*), then a PPP in the nominative (*inductus*), because it belongs to the subject."
4. **Inaccurate gloss (g-08):** Horace's *hoc* in *Odes* 3.14.27 is a snub at a girl's door, not "a quarrel", and "when Horace was 23" is off (born Dec. 65 BC; he turned 23 at the very end of 42). → "he'd let a snub pass these days… *Odes* 3.14.25–28", "in his early twenties."
5. **Untaught words in a blurb (g-04):** *Cum? Quod? Quamquam?* named a subjunctive conjunction (keep-out) and two untaught words. → *Duo verba Latīna, multa Anglica.*
6. **Present vs perfect unflagged (g-10):** *Venit Iēsus* is present (Greek historic present), but the gloss said "came" with no note; John has known *venit/vēnit* since Ch6. → note added.
7. **Joke that didn't land (g-07):** *Patre absente, cantāre nōn possumus* ("we can't sing… secretly relieved") was self-contradictory. → *Patre absente, nēmō cantat* ("everyone is secretly relieved").
8. **Broken italics (intro):** `*… promised it: *urbe captā*, …*` nested asterisks. → closed and reopened italics around the Latin, as in Ch39's blurb.
9. **Admonition title escaping:** `"The Law of the Missing \"Being\""` risked a broken title. → ‘Being’ with typographic quotes (renders correctly).
10. **Odd fix-it scene (g-13):** "With the box open, the dog went in" (into a box). → *canis cāseum invēnit*.
11. **Pronunciation respellings (g-15):** *captā* and *factō* were respelled KAHP/FAHK (long *a*); both have short *a*. → KAP-tah, FAK-toh.
12. **Small accuracy hedges:** *Absolūtus* called "the fourth key" (it's the participle; key is *absolūtum*) → fixed; *Deō volente* "later Christian phrase" → "later Latin phrase"; *A.U.C.* "Romans sometimes counted" → "Later historians counted"; Caesar "opens chapter after chapter" → "often opens a chapter… (*Quibus rēbus cognitīs* is another)"; Suetonius "jokers dated papers" → "some city wits, signing papers for fun."

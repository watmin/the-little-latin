## L1 (blocks)
1. **Wrong grammar claim on a real quotation (Accuracy):** the Polyphemus frame said *mōnstrum horrendum, īnfōrme, ingēns* are all "subject." In *Aeneid* 3.655-658 they stand in apposition to *pastōrem Polyphēmum*, the object of *vidēmus*. Now "in the same case as *mōnstrum*", with a note that in Vergil they're objects and that neuter subject and object look alike.

## L2 (fixed before publishing)
1. **Inaccurate history:** "Wilfred Owen, in the trenches of 1917": he wrote the poem in hospital (Craiglockhart). Now "a soldier-poet of the First World War".
2. **Misstated contrast:** "*rēge* and *fortis* will part ways": the contrast is *rēge* vs ***fortī***. Now "their 'with' forms part ways: *cum rēge fortī*."
3. **Citation precision:** the §3 motto's word order (*Omnia mea mēcum portō*) is the proverb's, not Cicero's (*omnia mēcum portō mea*). The attribution now says so. The frame's "fleeing with nothing in his hands" now follows Cicero: everyone fled with belongings, Bias took nothing.
4. **Untaught word in the Lectiō (Ordering):** Grandma's *Puerō celerī aut puellae celerī* used *aut*, never taught. Rewritten as *Ita, sī vincet! · Et puerō celerī? · Et puerō celerī! Omnēs parēs estis.*
5. **Wrong sense:** *Omnibus pār sum* was glossed "I'm fair to everyone"; it means "I'm a match for anyone." Replaced by *Omnēs parēs estis* ("You're all equal"); the frame now teaches the plural *parēs*.
6. **Untaught construction:** Marcus's *Quem cāseum?* uses *quem* as "which?", an interrogative adjective never taught (Ch4 had only *quem?* "whom?"). Now *Cāseus? Canis an cāseus ācer?*, and Grandma answers *Cāseus ācer!*
7. **Duplicate motto:** the Lectiō motto repeated §6's *Fortēs fortūna adiuvat*. Now Cicero's *Fortūna caeca est* (*On Friendship* 54), which fits Grandpa's luck.
8. **Wrong cross-reference:** *adiuvō* = *ad* + "Chapter 17's *iuvō*"; *iuvō* first appeared in Ch16's Lectiō motto *Forsan et haec… iuvābit*. Fixed.
9. **Missing sources:** Pliny the Elder's *Fortēs fortūna iuvat* now cites his nephew's *Letters* 6.16; Seneca's *vītam brevem esse, longam artem* now cites *On the Shortness of Life* 1.2.
10. **Tense step not flagged:** *sī vincet* ("if she wins") uses a future after *sī*, while Ch18 had only *sī* + present. The Lectiō intro now says it's literally "if she will win."

Decisions recorded:
- Terms: ***cum* clause** ("a when / since / although clause"); **temporal *cum*** ("the plain 'when'", fact mood, answers "at what time?"); **circumstantial *cum*** ("the story *cum*", also narrative/historical *cum*); **causal *cum*** ("*cum* = since"); **concessive *cum*** ("*cum* = although", signpost *tamen*). Early spelling *quom*, cousin of *quī* (A&G §544).
- Indicative: present/future/future perfect (*Cum vēneris, cēnābimus*; Ch27 Catullus 5 and Ch47 Luke 23:42 paid); perfect indicative dates the time (*BG* 6.12.1 *Cum Caesar in Galliam vēnit*; A&G §545 n.1); "whenever" = perfect + present / pluperfect + imperfect (A&G §548; Verres 2.5.27 *cum rosam vīderat*); *cum prīmum* + perfect (A&G §543 group; taught as a chunk, *postquam/ubi/simul atque* left for Ch59). Cicero *Cat.* 1.21 *cum tacent, clāmant* taught as plain "when" (it is often classed explicative *cum*; not named).
- Story *cum*: imperfect = same time, pluperfect = earlier, always past team (A&G §546); "Not doubt: it paints the scene." Learner's rule: tell stories with the subjunctive *cum*. Fills Ch39's missing "having eaten" (A&G §544). Story *cum* after historic present always secondary (A&G §485e note; *BG* 1.7.1 *cum id nūntiātum esset … mātūrat*, *BC* 1.34 *Quō cum vēnisset, cognōscit*).
- Causal/concessive: subjunctive, any tense by the matching rule (A&G §549): *Quae cum ita sint* (*Cat.* 1.10, present = now team), Pompey *BC* 3.86 *cum tantum equitātū valeāmus*, *BG* 7.62.4 *cum prīmī ōrdinēs … concidissent, tamen …*, Socrates (*Tusc.* 1.71) *cum facile posset ēdūcī ē custōdiā, nōluit* (concessive by sense, no *tamen*). Early Latin indicative noted (A&G §549 n.3).
- Three ways to set a scene: participle (Ch38) / ablative absolute (Ch40) / *cum* clause; Luke 2:42–43 shows both tools.
- Story: day 23 (*Diē vīcēsimā tertiā*): Grandma goes to the forum (*Cum redierō, cēnābimus*; *nēmō cāseum tangat*), Grandpa volunteers as guard (*Cum ego hīc sim, cāseus tūtus est*) → Grandma's Juvenal echo *Cum tū cāseum custōdiās, quis tē custōdiet?*; Grandpa stares silently (*cum tacēret, tamen oculī eius clāmābant*; John quotes *cum tacent, clāmant*), "sleeps" with one eye open; a mouse climbs onto the table; Grandpa jumps up, the dog chases it off (*Cum mūrem fugāverīs, tibi grātiās agō*); Grandpa, though very hungry, doesn't eat, but his finger is in the cheese ("only checking"); Grandma finds the cheese whole, sees the finger, laughs, gives Grandpa the first piece (*Cum tam bonus custōs fuerīs*); Grandpa *Quae cum ita sint, cēnēmus!*; Grandma's Verres joke *Cum cāseum vīdistī, cēnam incipere arbitrāris!*; after dinner a small piece of the big cheese remains, Grandpa watching it. **New canon: day 23 the family ate most of the big cheese at dinner (Grandpa got the first piece); a mouse exists in the house; a small piece of the big cheese is left on the table.**

## L1 (blocks; fixed)
1. **Wrong macron:** *Cuius rēgiō, eius religiō* (motto and g-04 frame, Vocābula gloss list). *Regiō, regiōnis* has a short e (from *regō*), unlike *rēx*. → *regiō* everywhere, with a note "short e, from *regō*."
2. **Correct Latin presented as an error:** g-09 fix-it (and homework, error card) *puer cui liber hic est* for "the boy whose book this is." With Ch11's dative of possession, *cui liber est* is good Latin ("who has this book"), so the "fix" taught a false rule. → Replaced with *puer quem librum legō* → *puer cuius librum legō* ("the boy whose book I'm reading"); card `iii28-e-puer-quem` matches.

## L2 (fixed before publishing)
1. g-03 Vergil frame said the verb *vēnit* "waits two lines"; it is in line 2 (*Ītaliam, fātō profugus, Lāvīniaque vēnit*). → "waits until the next line."
2. g-01 said *vir* goes like *puer* "(Chapter 9)"; *puer, puerī* was set up in Ch10. → "like *puer, puerī* (Chapter 10)."
3. g-01 "Latin uses *quī* for people, animals, and things alike" contradicted g-02 (*quae/quod*). → "Latin has one word for people, animals, and things alike. It changes only to match its noun, as you'll see next."
4. g-05 Matthew 3:17 gloss "*mihi*: here, 'to myself'" was confusing/misleading. → "*Mihi complacuī*: 'I am well pleased,' a set phrase built on a perfect."
5. g-05 "the houses in which we live" (the family has one *vīlla*). → "the roads on which we walk," *Viae in quibus ambulāmus* (choose card updated).
6. g-06 called *Age quod agis* "the homework motto from Chapter 4 on"; in Ch4 it's a mid-chapter motto. → "Chapter 4's motto (and many a homework motto since)."
7. g-08 Phaedrus answer gave a garbled literal ("Those things are not always (the things) which they seem"). → Natural English + "*Ea… quae*: 'those things… which'."
8. Lectiō *Omnēs | Cāseus!* (blaming the dog) read as nominative "Cheese!" and clashed with the real cheese. → *Omnēs | Canis!* ("The dog!"), which Cornēlia's *Nōn canis!* answers.
9. g-10 "Latin can put a little word between the antecedent and the relative": the word is the verb *venit*. → "a word or two, here *venit*."
10. g-10 "since Chapter 16 the kids have been begging *Nōlī, Pater!*"; that first appears in Ch18 (Ch16 has *Ēheu!*). → "since Chapter 18."
11. g-11 *cui* "KOOY" disagreed with Ch19/Ch21 (HOO-ik one beat; KOO-ee-kweh). → "KOO-ee squeezed into one beat (like *huic*)."
12. Homework glue pair *Puella cantat. Puellae vōx dulcis est.* had two right answers (*cuius* or dative-of-possession *cui*). → *Marcus cantat. Marcī vōx dulcis nōn est.* (only *cuius*).
13. Homework used untaught *rīdent*. → *Līberī currunt.*

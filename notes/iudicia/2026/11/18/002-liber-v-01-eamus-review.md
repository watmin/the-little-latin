Decisions recorded:
- Terms: **mood** ("the verb's attitude"), **indicative** ("the 'fact' mood"; Ch25's old-school "perfect active indicative" recalled), **imperative** ("the 'command' mood"), **subjunctive** ("the 'maybe / let's / should' mood"; *modus subiūnctīvus* "the joined-under mood," also "conjunctive"), **hortatory** ("the 'let's' subjunctive"; Ch43 *hortor*), **jussive** ("the 'let him / let them' subjunctive"; *iubeō*; A&G §439 n.2: same construction as the hortatory), **deliberative** ("the 'what am I to do?' subjunctive"; *dēlīberō*).
- Forms: first family *a* → *e* (*amem*, short *e* before final *-m, -t, -nt*); all others the "I" form *-ō* → *-am* (*habeam, dūcam, capiam, audiam*); *-im* rebels *sim, possim, velim (vel-), nōlim, mālim*; regular *eam, feram, fīam* (long *ī* in *fīat, fīant*); *dem, dēs* as first family (Ch11 paid); passive/deponent with the Ch30 endings (*laudētur, dūcātur, lauder, dūcar, loquāmur, sequāmur, laetēmur, moriāmur*). Look-alikes: *dūcam/audiam/capiam/faciam* = future "I" too; *dūcēs* vs *dūcās*; *habēs* vs *amēs*; *eat* = "let him go."
- Uses: hortatory (we, *nē*), jussive (he/they, *nē*; contrasted with Ch47's *-tō* of laws), deliberative (I/we in a question, **nōn**: Terence *Nōn eam?*; A&G §444). Second-person present subjunctive (*Nē timeās*, Horace *sapiās*) labeled poetry/comedy/Bible (A&G §439a, §450); talk still uses *nōlī* + infinitive. Prayer lines (*adveniat, fīat, sanctificētur*) translated "let…" with a note that a prayer's "let" is really a wish, named in Ch50.
- Payoffs: Ch31/32 *Fīat lūx / Fīat voluntās tua*, Ch11 *Dō ut dēs* and Ch4 *ut vīvās … ut edās* (forms only; *ut*: Ch51), Ch17/39 Horace *sit* and Ch46 Catullus 85 *faciam* (forms only; indirect question: Ch53), Ch47 *Nē timeās*, Ch44 *Ecl.* 10.69 *cēdāmus Amōrī* ("the verb form waits for Liber V"), Ch6 *carpe diem* neighbors (*sapiās, vīna liquēs*), Ch37/47 shepherds (Luke 2:15).

## L1 (blocks; fixed)
1. **Wrong pronunciation claim (g-07):** *eat* "rhymes with 'ay-at'": the *e* is short ("eh"), so the hint taught a long vowel. → "Two syllables: E-at." (matches g-17).
2. **Teaching contradicted by the Lectiō:** Grandma's *Dormiās, Ave* used a second-person present subjunctive as an everyday command, the very usage g-11 says Romans avoided in talk (A&G §439a: only indefinite "you," early Latin, poetry), and g-16 explained it as "soft." → *Dormī, Ave*; the g-16 question removed.

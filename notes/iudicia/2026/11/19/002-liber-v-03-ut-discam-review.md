Decisions recorded:
- Terms: **purpose clause** ("the 'so that / in order to' clause"; old-school "final clause," *fīnis*), **relative purpose clause** ("a *quī*-clause that tells a goal"). A&G §529–531 (purpose with *ut/nē*; relative purpose after sending/choosing; *nē quis*; *ut nōn* not classical for purpose).
- Why subjunctive: the goal isn't a fact yet (Ch49 "maybe" mood).
- Main verbs kept to present, future, imperatives, and one perfect "have" (John 10:10 *vēnī*); a past-story goal deferred to Ch52 without naming sequence.
- *nē quis / nē quid* via Ch29's "ali- drops after sī, nisi and a few little words"; *ut nēmō* and *ut nōn* flagged as Ch52's job (result) without naming it.
- Vulgate *ut nōn* (Matt 7:1, 26:41) labeled Bible usage; classical *nē* taught for speaking (Ch47's cut Matt 7:1 now paid).
- Contrast with Ch39 future participle of purpose (*lūsūrī*, *ēsūrus*), after verbs of going only; *ut* + indicative "as" (*ut dīxī, ut vidēs*) as a look-alike.
- Story: day 19; Marcus and Quintus sent to the forum *ut novum cāseum emant* (Quintus *quī tēcum eat*), Cornelia stays home *ut discat*; Grandpa's kiss-for-cheese *Dō ut dēs* gets bread (*ut vīvās, nōn cāseum, ut edās*), proverb split between Grandpa and Grandma; the old cheese shut in the cupboard *nē quis eum tangat*; merchant *Gustā prīmum, ut sciās*; *Nōn venit nisi ut fūrētur* (John 10:10 echo) about the dog; *Edāmus, ut fortēs sīmus*; Grandpa gives the dog cheese and gets a lick: *Dō ut dēs!*; Grandma: *Utinam canis quoque Latīnē discat…* **New canon: a new big cheese bought at the forum on day 19; the old small one is in the cupboard; the dog got a small piece from Grandpa.**

## L1 (blocks; fixed)
1. **Self-contradiction in a grammar claim (Law of the Goal):** "Today every main verb is 'now,' 'later,' or a command," but g-05 teaches John 10:10 *ego vēnī ut vītam habeant* (perfect). → "…a command, or 'have done' (John's *vēnī*, 'I have come'); a goal in a past story needs a new form: Chapter 52."
2. **Wrong grammar claim (g-02):** "It starts with *ut* … Always" contradicts *nē* (Section 7) and the relative *quī* (Section 10). → "*ut* ('so that'; Section 7 adds its 'not' twin) and its verb is subjunctive, always."
3. **Wrong pronunciation (g-17):** *ut* respelled "OOT" (long u) while the note said short. → "ut (short *u*, as in 'put') … Don't stretch *ut* into 'oot'."

## L2 (fixed before publishing)
1. **Garbled explanation (g-10):** "(*ut is* is close to *quī*)" → "It works like *ut is cāseum emat*, 'so that he may buy cheese': *quī* stands in for *ut is*."
2. **Unsupported example (g-10):** *dūcō* listed as a sending verb with no example. Removed (*mittō, ēligō* kept).
3. **Mistranslation (g-09):** Matt 26:41 *ut nōn intrētis in tentātiōnem* rendered "don't fall into" → "don't enter into."
4. **Illogical example (g-13):** *Canem tangō ut videās* ("I touch the dog so that you may see") → *Lucernam accendō ut videās* ("I light the lamp so that you can see"), reusing g-06 *lucerna/accendō*.
5. **Misquoted proverb in the Lectiō:** Grandpa's *Ēsse oportet ut vīvam…* bent the real line; → *ut vīvās* ("so that one may live"), Grandma finishes *…nōn vīvere ut edās!*
6. **Overclaim (g-03, Law, g-12):** "Latin never uses a plain infinitive for a goal" (poets occasionally do). → "Latin prose never…"; Law "never a Latin infinitive in prose"; g-12 "No plain infinitive for a goal."

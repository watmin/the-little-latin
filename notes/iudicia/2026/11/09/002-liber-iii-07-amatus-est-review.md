Decisions recorded:
- New terms, each in plain words: **participle** ("a verb-adjective"; *participium*, "taking part," because it shares in verb and adjective); **perfect passive participle**, called in this book "the fourth-key adjective" (full participle work → Ch37); **perfect / pluperfect / future perfect passive** ("was/has been …ed," "had been …ed," "will have been …ed").
- Laws: **Two Pieces** (fourth-key adjective agrees with the subject like *magnus*; *sum* tells person and time; pieces can split or swap); **One Word and Two** (present/imperfect/future passive one word, Ch30; perfect/pluperfect/future perfect two words; *sum/eram/erō* = looking back from now/then/later; so *amātus sum* ≠ "I am loved," *amātus erō* ≠ "I will be loved").
- Central trap drilled four times (g-03, g-09, g-11, Lectiō ending): *amātus sum* = "I was loved," vs Ch30 *amor* = "I am loved." The Lectiō ends on it: Grandpa *Ōlim amātus sum…* → John *Nōn "amātus es," Ave: amāris!*
- Happening vs done: *clauditur / clausa est*, *editur / ēsus est*, *pōnitur / positus est*; English "is …ed" for a finished state (*Cōnsummātum est*, *Gallia dīvīsa est*, *Capsa aperta est*).
- Payoffs: Ch25 fourth key and motto *Factum est*; Ch30's deferred "the cheese was eaten" and promise *Crās cāseus ā mē emētur* (→ *Cāseus ā mē emptus est!*); Ch30 *videor* (*vīsus est* = "seemed"); Ch30 Law of the Doer and the Tool; Ch15 Caesar *Gallia est omnis dīvīsa*; Ch19 *Fīlius meus dīlēctus* (fourth-key adjective of *dīligō*); Ch8 *Īte, missa est* (hedged: participle vs late noun); Ch16 *In prīncipiō erat Verbum* → John 1:14; Ch27 *Sērō! … iam ēderat* → *iam ēsus erat*, *Sī … ēderis* → *Sī cāseus ēsus erit*; Ch28 *Fuit cāseus*; Ch14 dog named Cāseus (*Cāseus ā Cāseō ēsus est*).
- New words: *pōnō, claudō, aperiō, mittō* (four keys), *inveniō*'s fourth key; glossed: *dīvidō, cantus, iaciō, ālea, lūx, fīat, carō, nihil, cōnsummō, dīligō, errātum, corrigō*.

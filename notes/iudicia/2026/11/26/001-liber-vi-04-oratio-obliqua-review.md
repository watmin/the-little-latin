## L1 (fixed)
1. §2 (g-02) Socrates frame said "Cicero adds that it's 'more convincing than true.'" Cicero's words are *illud est probābilius, neque tamen vērum* ("more plausible, but not true"): the gloss misreported the source. → "Cicero calls the saying 'more plausible, but still not true.'"
2. §14 Auctor intro said Caesar caught one Helvetian district "crossing the river Arar." BG 1.12.2 says he attacked the part *quae nōndum flūmen trānsierat* (still on the near bank; three-quarters had crossed), which is also what Divico's *cum iī quī flūmen trānsissent suīs auxilium ferre nōn possent* refers to. → "still waiting to cross the river Arar … ; the rest had already crossed."
3. §6 (g-06) first frame had the seller say *Nōlī cāseum in viā gustāre!*, but the Lectiō (and the card) have *Nōlī cāseum tōtum ūnō diē edere!*; the frame also said "the seller's last words," so the two contradicted each other. → frame rewritten to the Lectiō's words (*nē cāseum tōtum ūnō diē ederem*), with "(section 13)".

## L2 (fixed)
1. §5 (g-05) gloss "*num*: surely not? Chapter 9": *num* was taught in Chapter 2. → "Chapter 2."
2. §5 Ariovistus frame: "the verb of saying (*respondit*, 1.44.1) is pages back" overstates it (seven sentences). → "several sentences back."
3. §12 trap 4 called *Dīxit cāseum, quem attulerit, …* simply wrong, while §3's fine print (A&G §585b) says Caesar and Livy slip to the now team in long reports. → answer adds "(Caesar sometimes slips back to the now team in a long report, as section 3's fine print says. In your own Latin, follow the verb of saying.)"; error card says "(your own Latin)".
4. §8 (g-08) *For Archias* 20 called Plotius "a poet he liked"; Cicero only says Marius valued L. Plotius' talent to celebrate his deeds (his identity as poet or rhetorician is uncertain). → "a writer he favored, Lucius Plotius."
5. §6 Caesar 1.7.5: the text prints *ad Īd. Aprīl.*; the frame silently expanded it. → gloss adds "which Caesar writes *Īd. Aprīl.*"
6. §3 Liscus quote printed "[Liscus] prōpōnit", which hid a cut (*Tum dēmum Liscus ōrātiōne Caesaris adductus quod anteā tacuerat prōpōnit*). → "*Liscus … prōpōnit:*".
7. §2 Marcus "phones from school (well, sends a slave)": anachronistic joke that muddies the frame. → "Marcus sends word home from school."
8. "How to read this chapter" told the reader to look back for a verb of saying, which fails for the hidden report (no verb of saying by definition). → adds "If there isn't, ask whose reason or words it is: maybe it's someone else's (section 7)."
9. §14 Divico's comeback answer said "(He means Cassius's army.)", which is vague; the commentators (Harkness, via DCC) say it alludes to the hostages Cassius's survivors gave in 107 BC. → "(He means 107 BC: Cassius's beaten survivors had to hand over hostages.)"
10. §3 motto *Dīxit sē Rōmam itūrum ut cōnsulem vidēret* was attributed "(Allen and Greenough §585)" as if a quotation; it's the grammar's own made-up sentence. → "(grammar-book example, Allen and Greenough §585)."

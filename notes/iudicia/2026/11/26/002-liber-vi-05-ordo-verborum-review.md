## L1 (fixed)
1. §6 (g-06) and the passing gloss printed Quintilian's *incomptum / incomptus* without the macron: *incōmptus* (from *cōmō, cōmptus*). → *incōmptum, incōmptus* everywhere.
2. §3 (g-03) said Allen and Greenough treat the last place as "saved for last." A&G §597b says the opposite of a spotlight: "The more important word is never placed last for emphasis"; a last word is an afterthought. → "Grammars disagree. Gildersleeve and Lodge (§672) count the beginning and the end… Allen and Greenough (§597) say the first place is the brightest, and a word put last is really an afterthought"; the Law box adds "grammars disagree on whether that's a second spotlight."
3. §15 (g-15) said *silvās* is "accusative subject of *resonāre*, Chapter 42's accusative + infinitive after *doceō*." *Doceō* + acc. + inf. ("teach someone to do") isn't Ch42's reported statement. → "*doceō* takes the one taught in the accusative and what he's taught to do in the infinitive."
4. §15 (g-15) last frame called *Ō Avia, dea nōbīs haec ōtia fēcit* "Grandma's parody"; in the Lectiō it's Grandpa's line (16). → "Grandpa's parody in the Lectiō (line 16)… what did he change?"
5. Lectiō line 22 had Grandpa say Plato wrote "his words" many ways on wax tablets; Quintilian 8.6.64 says it was the four opening words of the *Republic*. → *Etiam Platō prīma quattuor verba librī suī multīs modīs in cērīs scrīpsit* ("the first four words of his book").

## L2 (fixed)
1. §1 (g-01) said *patulae* is "genitive singular" outright, though it could be dative or nominative plural until *fāgī* arrives (which §9 then teaches). → "'of' or 'to' one, or a plural subject … *patulae* fits as 'of' too."
2. §2 (g-02) Caesar 1.10.3 context said he "leaves his army in Gaul"; 1.10.3 puts Labienus in charge of the fortification and Caesar goes to raise legions. → "puts his officer Labienus in charge of the wall he has built, and goes for more troops"; *contendit* labeled a historic present (Ch54).
3. §3 (g-03) had Grandpa say *Ēdit Avus cāseum!* about himself in the third person. → Quintus says it.
4. §4 (g-04) and the Law said *igitur* "almost never" goes first; A&G §599b: "igitur usually second" (Sallust and others do put it first). → "usually isn't / usually doesn't."

Decisions recorded:
- Terms (plain + old-school): **fear clause** = "the fear-that clause" (old-school *clause of fearing*, "verbs of fearing"); **prevention clause** = "the stop-it clause" (old-school *clause of hindering/preventing*).
- Fear clause taught as the wish pushed inside (A&G §564 note): *Nē veniat!* → *timeō nē veniat*; *Utinam veniat!* → *timeō ut veniat* ("that…not"). Tenses by Ch54 matching rule (present = now/soon, perfect = already happened; imperfect/pluperfect on the past team).
- Paid: Ch52 Law of the Two Nots (ut after fear ≠ purpose), Ch56 *nē quis*, Ch43 *vereor*/*veritus*, Ch47 *timēre* + infinitive, Ch38 *Timeō Danaōs* (accusative), Ch39 *Quīn et* (a different *quīn*), Ch53 tucked-in question (*dubitō num*), Ch57's Cato and the philosopher joke.
- Kept out: *dubitō an* (idiomatically "I rather think"; would mislead), relative clause with *quīn* (= *quī nōn*, *nēmō est quīn*) which is Ch59, *quīn* as "why not?" in main clauses, fears with *-ūrus sit*, *vereor ut* + *possim* subtleties beyond the one Cicero line, *nōn dubito* in indirect discourse beyond BG 1.3.6 (read as a quoted line).

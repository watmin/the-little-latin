# Caput XXIX: Quis Venit?

*Liber III, Fābulae · Chapter 29*

*In which someone is coming and nobody can say who. Since Chapter 1 you have asked* Quis est? *and* Quid est? *Chapter 28 gave you the relative* quī, quae, quod *and warned you that question words share its forms. Today the question words get their own chapter: how to ask "who?", "what?", "whose?", "to whom?" and "which?" Then come their three cousins, for when you don't know the answer, or don't want to say:* aliquis *(someone),* quīdam *(a certain someone),* quisque *(each one). Three old chunks finally crack open: Chapter 1's* Quod nōmen tibi est? *Chapter 21's* Suum cuique, *and Chapter 26's* in quādam cīvitāte. *Speaking goal: ask who, what, whose, to whom, which, and talk about "someone," "something," "a certain," "each."*

!!! abstract "In this chapter"
    - **Saying:** *Quis venit?* · *Quid vīs?* · *Cuius est?* · *Cui cāseum dās?* · *Quod nōmen tibi est?* · *Aliquis cāseum ēdit!* · *Vir quīdam hīc est.* · *Suum cuique.*
    - **Grammar words, in plain English:** interrogative (a question word); interrogative pronoun (a question word that stands alone); interrogative adjective (a question word that sits on a noun); indefinite (a word that doesn't point at any one particular person or thing)
    - **Forms:** *quis, quid, cuius, cui, quem, quō* · plural *quī, quae, quae…* · *quī, quae, quod* with a noun · *aliquis, aliquid* · *quīdam, quaedam, quiddam* · *quisque, quidque*
    - **Laws:** the Law of the Question Words · the Law of Alone and Company · the Law of the Cousins
    - **Words:** *rogō, rogāre, rogāvī, rogātum*; *nēmō, nihil*; *faber, fabrī*
    - **Reading:** *Quis venit?* (a visitor, a lost purse, and a cheese that goes missing)
    - **Speaking:** ask "who? what? whose? to whom? which?" and say "someone, something, a certain…, each one"

!!! tip "How to read this chapter"
    Same as always. Say your answer **out loud**, then tap the wax tablet to check. Keep Chapter 28 open in your head: today's question words are built from the same forms as *quī, quae, quod*, and you will mostly be telling look-alikes apart.

---

## 1. *Quis? Quid?*

:: Quis custōdiet ipsōs custōdēs? || Who will guard the guards themselves? (Juvenal, *Satires* 6.347–348)

Quis venit? Quid est? Quem vidēs?

{#g-01}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 2: *Quis est? Quid est?* Which asks about a person, which about a thing? Now the title: *Quis venit?* (*Veniō*: Chapter 6.) | ***Quis*** = "who?" (a person) · ***quid*** = "what?" (a thing). *Quis venit?* = "Who is coming?" |
| Plain English: a word that asks a question is **interrogative**. Latin *interrogō*, "I ask" (*inter-* + *rogō*, ask). *Quis* stands in for a noun you don't know yet, so it is an **interrogative pronoun**, a question word that stands alone. In *Quis cāseum ēdit?*, what does *quis* stand for? | The unknown eater: *Canis cāseum ēdit*, *Avus cāseum ēdit*. *Quis* is the **subject**, nominative. |
| *Quid canis edit?* (*Edit*: eats.) Which job does *quid* do? And *Quid est in mēnsā?* | Object in the first: "**What** does the dog eat?" Subject in the second: "**What** is on the table?" Same form, *quid*: Chapter 4's Neuter Twins again. |
| For a person as object, Chapter 4's word. *Quem Marcus videt?* | "**Whom** does Marcus see?" *Quem*: accusative. Answer: *Cornēliam videt.* |
| Is there another "who" for a woman? *Quis venit?* … *Avia venit.* | No need: ***quis*** and ***quem*** work for a man or a woman. (Grammar books list a feminine form, but ordinary Latin leans on these two.) |
| Pilate to Jesus: *Quid est vēritās?* (*Vēritās*: truth.) | "**What** is truth?" (John 18:38, Vulgate). Neuter *quid* + *est*, just like Chapter 2. |
| The motto: *Quis custōdiet ipsōs custōdēs?* (*Custōs, custōdis*: guard, Chapter 13. *Custōdiō*: guard, an *audiō*-type verb.) Find the future, and the intensive from Chapter 21. | ***Custōdiet***: "will guard" (like Chapter 17's *audiet*). ***Ipsōs***: "the guards themselves," agreeing with *custōdēs*. "Who will guard the guards **themselves**?" |

## 2. Whose? To whom?

:: Cui bonō? || Who stood to gain? (Cicero, *For Roscius of Ameria* 84, quoting the judge Cassius)

Cuius est? Cui dās? Quōcum ambulās?

{#g-02}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 10: *Cuius est sacculus?* What does *cuius* ask? | "**Whose** is the purse?" The owner case, genitive. One form for a man, a woman, or a thing: the same *cuius* you met in Chapter 28. |
| Chapter 19 promised this word: *cui* is "to whom?" (*huic*, "to this," has the same rhyme). "To whom do you give the cheese?" | ***Cui cāseum dās?*** *Cui*: the receiver, dative (Chapter 11), one beat, KOO-ee. Answer: *Avō cāseum dō.* |
| Cassius, the judge Romans trusted most, asked of every crime: *Cui bonō?* (*Bonō*: good, a benefit.) Guess it. | "To whom (was it) for a benefit?" = "**Who gained** by it?" A question every detective still asks. |
| Chapter 7's *cum* glued on again, as in Chapter 28's *quōcum*. "With whom do you walk?" | ***Quōcum ambulās?*** (also *cum quō ambulās?*). Answer: *Cum Avō ambulō.* *Quō*: ablative, the *cum* case. |
| Chant the singular question word in case order: subject, of, to, object, by/with. Then the neuter. | ***Quis, cuius, cui, quem, quō*** · ***quid, cuius, cui, quid, quō.*** Compare Chapter 28's *quī, cuius, cui, quem, quō / quod*: only *quis* and *quid* are new shapes. |
| Marcus gives Quīntus Grandpa's cheese: *Marcus Quīntō cāseum Avī dat.* Ask about each noun. | ***Quis*** cāseum Quīntō dat? · ***Quid*** Marcus Quīntō dat? · ***Cui*** Marcus cāseum dat? · ***Cuius*** cāseum Marcus Quīntō dat? Answers: *Marcus · cāseum · Quīntō · Avī.* |

## 3. Plurals: *Quī? Quōs?*

:: Quōs ego—! || Whom I—! (Neptune, furious at the winds and breaking off mid-threat; Vergil, *Aeneid* 1.135)

Quī sunt? Quōs vidēs? Quibus cāseum dās?

{#g-03}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Neptune finds the winds wrecking his sea and sputters *Quōs ego—!* (He never finishes.) Case of *quōs*? | Accusative plural: "**Whom** I—!" The winds are the objects of the threat he doesn't speak. The ending is the one in Chapter 28's *puerī quōs videō*. |
| Plural "who": three boys come, then three girls. *Quī sunt? Quae sunt?* | ***Quī sunt?*** "Who are they?" (boys) · ***Quae sunt?*** (girls). And *Quae sunt?* can also ask "What are those?" about neuter things; the company decides, as in Chapter 28. |
| "Whom do you see?" asked about several. | ***Quōs vidēs?*** (boys) · ***Quās vidēs?*** (girls) · ***Quae vidēs?*** (things). |
| "Whose are these books?" (*Hī librī*: Chapter 19.) Which plural "of"? | ***Quōrum sunt hī librī?*** Answer: *Puerōrum sunt.* *Quōrum / quārum*: the endings of Chapter 28. |
| "To whom (several) do you give the cheese?" "With whom (several) do you walk?" | ***Quibus cāseum dās?*** *Līberīs.* · ***Quibuscum ambulās?*** *Cum amīcīs.* *Quibus* does double duty, Chapter 13's Law. |
| *Quis legit?* or *Quī legunt?* One answer is *Marcus legit*, the other *Marcus et Quīntus legunt*. Which is which? | *Quis legit?* → *Marcus legit* (one reader). *Quī legunt?* → *Marcus et Quīntus legunt* (several). The verb's number gives it away. |

!!! success "The Law of the Question Words"
    The singular question word has only **two new shapes**: ***quis*** (who?) and ***quid*** (what?).
    Everything else is borrowed from the relative: *cuius, cui, quem, quō* in the singular, and the whole plural *quī, quae, quae · quōrum, quārum · quibus · quōs, quās, quae*.
    *Quis cāseum ēdit? **Quem** vidēs? **Cui** dās? **Quōrum** sunt?*

## 4. *Quī vir? Quod nōmen?*

:: Quod nōmen tibi est? || What is your name? (Chapter 1)

Quī vir? Quae puella? Quod mālum?

{#g-04}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 1 gave you two ways to ask a name: *Quid est nōmen tibi?* and *Quod nōmen tibi est?* Why is the second *quod*? | *Quod* sits on a noun: *nōmen* (neuter singular). "**What** name?" It agrees with *nōmen* like any adjective (Chapter 3). |
| Plain English: a question word that sits on a noun is an **interrogative adjective** ("which? what?"). One that stands alone is an interrogative pronoun. Sort: *Quis venit?* · *Quī vir venit?* | *Quis venit?*: **pronoun**, alone ("who?"). *Quī vir venit?*: **adjective**, sitting on *vir* ("which man?"). |
| The adjective forms are Chapter 28's *quī, quae, quod*. "Which girl is singing?" | ***Quae puella cantat?*** Feminine noun, feminine *quae*. |
| "What do you want?" and "Which apple do you want?" | ***Quid vīs?*** (alone: pronoun) · ***Quod mālum vīs?*** (on *mālum*: adjective). |
| "Which book are you reading?" "In which garden are you sitting?" | ***Quem librum legis?*** (masc. accusative, like *librum*) · ***In quō hortō sedēs?*** (ablative after *in*). |
| What is the whole difference between the two kinds? | **Alone**, the singular subject is ***quis / quid***. **With a noun** it is ***quī / quae / quod***, an ordinary adjective that matches its noun's gender, number, and case. |
| Chapter 22's partitive genitive at work in the everyday greeting *Quid novī?* (*Novus*: new.) | "**What of new?**" = "What's new?" Like *pars cāseī*: *quid* + a genitive. And *Quid agis?* ("What are you doing?") is "How are you?" (*Agō*: Chapter 4's *Age quod agis*). |
| Ask about an animal on the path: *Quod animal est?* Answer it. | "**What kind of animal** is it?" *Quod* sits on *animal* (neuter, Chapter 15). Answer: *Canis est.* *Quid est?* alone would just be "What is it?" |

!!! success "The Law of Alone and Company"
    **Alone** → ***quis*** (who?) and ***quid*** (what?).
    **With a noun** → ***quī, quae, quod*** (which? what?), agreeing with that noun.
    In the other cases (*cuius, cui, quem, quō*, the plurals) pronoun and adjective share their forms; the adjective just matches its noun (*quam, quā* for a feminine noun).

## 5. Asking, or pointing back?

:: Vidē quis rogat, vidē quid rogat. || Look who is asking, look what is asked. (teaching line; *rogat*: asks)

Quī vir? Vir quī… Quis est? Est vir quī…

{#g-05}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Quī vir cantat?* and *Vir quī cantat.* Both have *quī*. What's the difference? | The first **asks**: "Which man is singing?" The second **points back**: "the man who sings." The noun usually comes **right after** an asking *quī* and **before** a relative *quī*. |
| *Quem librum legis?* and *Liber quem legis.* | "**Which book** are you reading?" vs "the book **that** you are reading." |
| Chapter 28's riddle game, in one breath. *Quis est? Est vir quī in lectō stertit.* | Ask with *quis*, answer with a relative: "Who is it? It's the man who snores in bed." The question word and the relative live side by side. |
| *Quōcum ambulās?* and *Quō īs?* Both have *quō*. | *Quōcum ambulās?*: "With whom do you walk?" (a person; *cum* is glued on). *Quō īs?*: Chapter 8's "Where are you going?" (a place). |

## 6. Someone: *aliquis*

:: Ex Āfricā semper aliquid novī. || Out of Africa, always something new. (a Roman saying; Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* 8.42, quotes a Greek proverb)

Aliquis venit. Aliquid vidēs. Nēmō venit.

{#g-06}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Someone is at the door and nobody knows who. You can't say a name, just "someone." Plain English: a word that doesn't point at any particular person or thing is **indefinite** (Latin *indefīnītus*, "not marked off"). The Latin for "someone" is *aliquis*. Build it: *ali-* ("some") + which question word? | ***Ali- + quis.*** And "something" is ***aliquid***. |
| So: "Someone ate the cheese!" and "Something is on the table." | ***Aliquis cāseum ēdit!*** · ***Aliquid in mēnsā est.*** |
| It declines like *quis*, with *ali-* in front. "I gave the cheese to someone." "I see someone." "I walk with someone." | ***Alicui cāseum dedī.*** · ***Aliquem videō.*** · ***Cum aliquō ambulō.*** (Also *alicuius*, "someone's.") |
| The company rule again. "Some apple," "some girl," "some boy." (Guess: *ali-* in front of which adjective forms?) | ***Aliquod mālum*** · ***aliqua puella*** · ***aliquī puer.*** (The Law of Alone and Company again.) |
| The Roman saying *Ex Āfricā semper aliquid novī.* What Chapter 22 trick is *novī*? | The partitive genitive: "something **of new**" = "something new." (*Āfricā*: ablative after *ex*, Chapter 7.) |
| The opposites: *nēmō* ("no one," Chapter 18's *Nēmō potest*) and *nihil* ("nothing"). Grandpa shakes the empty box: "Who's in it? What's in it?" | *Quis in capsā est? **Nēmō!** · Quid in capsā est? **Nihil!*** Pairs: *aliquis / nēmō* · *aliquid / nihil.* |
| One twist. After *sī* ("if") the *ali-* drops off: *sī quis* = "if anyone," *sī quid* = "if anything." Translate with Chapter 27's habit of *sī* + future perfect: *Sī quis cāseum ēderit, Avia clāmābit.* | "**If anyone** eats the cheese, Grandma will shout." The English present, the Latin future perfect. (The same drop happens after *nisi*, "unless," and a few other little words.) |

## 7. A certain: *quīdam*

:: Homō quīdam habuit duōs fīliōs. || A certain man had two sons. (Luke 15:11, Vulgate; the story of the Prodigal Son)

Quīdam canis… vir quīdam… cuidam puerō…

{#g-07}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 26's Apuleius: *Erant in quādam cīvitāte rēx et rēgīna.* Chapter 26 said *quādam* would wait until Chapter 29. Guess it. | "There were a king and a queen **in a certain city**." *Quādam*: feminine ablative singular (after *in*, place where). The word is ***quīdam, quaedam, quoddam***, "a certain." |
| It is *quī* with *-dam* on the end, and it declines like *quī*. Luke's *Homō quīdam habuit duōs fīliōs.* (*Habuit*: Chapter 25. *Duōs*: Chapter 22.) | "A **certain** man had two sons." Neuter alone is *quiddam* ("a certain thing"); with a noun, *quoddam*. |
| *Aliquis* and *quīdam* are both "somebody." Cornēlia has a particular man in mind, Dad singing on the road, but doesn't name him: *Quīdam vir in viā cantat.* Why *quīdam* and not *aliquis*? | **Aliquis** = someone, any one at all (who knows who). **Quīdam** = a certain one: a particular person, just not named. "A certain man sings on the road…" |
| "I see a certain girl." "I see a certain man." Why *quandam* and *quendam*, not *quamdam*? | ***Puellam quandam videō. Quendam virum videō.*** The *m* melts into *n* before *d*, like Chapter 19's *eum-dem → eundem*. Plurals: *quōrundam, quārundam.* |
| "I gave the cheese to a certain boy." "Of a certain boy." | ***Cuidam puerō cāseum dedī.*** · ***cuiusdam puerī.*** *Cui + dam, cuius + dam.* |
| "Certain boys are singing." And the everyday opener: "Some people say…" | ***Quīdam puerī cantant.*** · ***Quīdam dīcunt…*** Plural *quīdam, quaedam, quaedam.* |
| "A certain dog ate a certain cheese." Say it, with both *quīdam* and *quendam*. | ***Quīdam canis cāseum quendam ēdit.*** The sentence Grandpa will hope you believe. |

## 8. Each one: *quisque*

:: Faber est suae quisque fortūnae. || Each person is the maker of his own fortune. (attributed to Appius Claudius Caecus; quoted in a letter once credited to Sallust)

Suum cuique. Quisque suam partem habet.

{#g-08}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 21's motto *Suum cuique* has a chunk you took on trust. Now crack it. *Cuique* = *cui* + *-que*. Chapter 3's *-que* meant "and." Does it here? | No. ***Quisque*** = *quis* + *-que* and is **one word**: "each one." *Cuique* = "to each." *Suum cuique* = "to each his own." |
| The forms: *quisque* ("each one"), *cuiusque* ("each one's"), *cuique* ("to each"), *quemque*, *quōque*; neuter ***quidque***. "Each one eats his own cheese." (*Suum*: Chapter 21.) | ***Quisque cāseum suum edit.*** *Quisque* is singular, so the verb is singular: "each one eats." |
| Same idea, feminine thing: "Each one has his own piece." Why *suam*? | ***Quisque suam partem habet.*** *Pars* is feminine, so *suam* (Chapter 21's *suus, sua, suum*). |
| "Each one loves cheese" and "all love cheese." Which verb is singular? | ***Quisque cāseum amat*** (each, singular) · ***Omnēs cāseum amant*** (all, plural). |
| The Roman saying *Faber est suae quisque fortūnae.* (*Faber, fabrī*: maker, craftsman.) Where does *quisque* sit, and why *suae*? | *Quisque* usually isn't the first word; it leans on another (*suae … quisque*). *Suae fortūnae*: genitive, "of his own fortune." "Each man is the **maker of his own fortune**." |
| *Avus cāseum cuiusque ēdit.* What did Grandpa eat? | "**Each one's** cheese": *cuiusque* is "of each." |

!!! success "The Law of the Cousins"
    *Aliquis, quīdam* and *quisque* are the question word with a **tag**: ***ali-**quis* (someone) · ***quī**dam* (a certain) · ***quis**que* (each).
    They decline like the word they were built from, so you already know their endings.

    | | m. | f. | n. |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | **who? what?** (alone) | *quis* | *quis* | *quid* |
    | **which?** (with a noun) | *quī* | *quae* | *quod* |
    | **someone, something** (alone) | *aliquis* | *aliquis* | *aliquid* |
    | **some** (with a noun) | *aliquī* | *aliqua* | *aliquod* |
    | **a certain** | *quīdam* | *quaedam* | *quiddam / quoddam* |
    | **each** (alone) | *quisque* | *quisque* | *quidque* |
    | **each** (with a noun) | *quisque* | *quaeque* | *quodque* |

    For *cuius, cui, quem, quō* and all the plurals, take the question word's form and add the tag. For *quīdam*, *m* turns to *n* before the *-dam*.

## 9. Fix it

:: Quī errat, discit. || Whoever slips up, learns. (Chapter 28's teaching line)

Quis an quī? Aliquis an quīdam? Cuique suum.

{#g-09}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Who came?": *Quem vēnit?* | ***Quis vēnit?*** "Who" is the subject: nominative *quis*. |
| "Which apple do you want?": *Quid mālum vīs?* | ***Quod mālum vīs?*** On a noun → *quod*. |
| "Which girl is singing?": *Quī puella cantat?* | ***Quae puella cantat?*** Feminine noun → *quae*. |
| "To whom do you give the cheese?": *Quis cāseum dās?* | ***Cui cāseum dās?*** The receiver → dative *cui*. |
| "Someone ate the cheese.": *Aliquem cāseum ēdit.* | ***Aliquis cāseum ēdit.*** The eater is the subject. |
| "Each one eats his own cheese.": *Quisque cāseum suum edunt.* | ***Quisque cāseum suum edit.*** *Quisque* is singular. |
| "I gave the cheese to a certain boy.": *Quīdam puerō cāseum dedī.* | ***Cuidam puerō cāseum dedī.*** The receiver is dative: *cuidam*. |

## 10. Lectiō: *Quis venit?*

:: Quī quaerit, invenit. || Whoever seeks, finds. (Matthew 7:8, Vulgate; Chapter 17's *Quaerite et inveniētis*)

Aliquis venit. Quis est? Quid portat?

Read each Latin line aloud. Guess the English, then tap to check. For the reverse drill, tap **Tege Latīna** and rebuild each line from the English. As you read, label every question word (pronoun or adjective) and every indefinite.

::: dialogue lectio
Avus | Quis venit? Aliquem audiō! || Grandpa | Who's coming? I hear someone!
Avia | Nēmō venit, Ave. Tū nihil audīs! || Grandma | Nobody's coming, Grandpa. You hear nothing!
Cornēlia | Ecce! Vir quīdam hīc est! || Cornēlia | Look! A certain man is here!
Quīntus | Quis est? Quid portat? || Quīntus | Who is it? What's he carrying?
Marcus | Aliquid in manū habet. Quid est? || Marcus | He has something in his hand. What is it?
Pater | Quis es? Quid vīs? || Dad | Who are you? What do you want?
Tabernārius | Salvēte! Tabernārius sum. Sacculum in tabernā invēnī. Cuius est? || Shopkeeper | Hello, y'all! I'm the shopkeeper. I found a little purse in the shop. Whose is it?
Avus | Meus est! || Grandpa | It's mine!
Avia | Nōn tuus est, Ave! Tū hodiē domī mānsistī. || Grandma | It's not yours, Grandpa! You stayed home today.
Tabernārius | Quī puer hodiē in tabernā erat? || Shopkeeper | Which boy was in the shop today?
Iōannēs | Ego! Sacculus meus est! || John | Me! It's my purse!
Tabernārius | Quot dēnāriī in sacculō sunt? || Shopkeeper | How many denarii are in the purse?
Iōannēs | Quattuor! Et aliquid dulce: crustulum! || John | Four! And something sweet: a cookie!
Tabernārius | Ita! Sacculus tuus est. || Shopkeeper | Yes! The purse is yours.
Iōannēs | Grātiās tibi agō! Cui crustulum dō? Tibi, tabernārī! || John | Thank you! To whom shall I give the cookie? To you, shopkeeper!
Tabernārius | Grātiās! Prō crustulō, ecce cāseus! Quisque partem habet! || Shopkeeper | Thanks! In exchange for the cookie, here's a cheese! Everyone gets a piece!
Avia | Suum cuique! Quisque suam partem habet. || Grandma | To each his own! Each one has his own piece.
Cāseus | Bau! || Cheese | Woof!
Marcus | Ubi est pars mea? || Marcus | Where's my piece?
Quīntus | Et mea? || Quīntus | And mine?
Cornēlia | Aliquis partēs nostrās ēdit! Quis? || Cornēlia | Someone ate our pieces! Who?
Avus | Nōn ego! Quīdam canis… || Grandpa | Not me! A certain dog…
Avia | Quid in manū tuā est, Ave? || Grandma | What's in your hand, Grandpa?
Avus | Aliquid… aliquid parvum… || Grandpa | Something… something small…
Omnēs | In stomachō… || Everyone | In the stomach of…
Avus | …Avī. || Grandpa | …Grandpa.
Avia | Suum cuique? Tū cāseum cuiusque ēdistī! || Grandma | To each his own? You ate everyone's cheese!
Cāseus | Bau! || Cheese | Woof!
:::

{#g-10}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Quis venit? Aliquem audiō!* Which half asks, which states, and what job does *aliquem* do? | *Quis venit?* asks "Who is coming?" *Aliquem audiō* states "I hear someone": *aliquem* is the object of *audiō*. Grandma answers *Nēmō*, *nihil*. |
| *Vir quīdam hīc est.* Why *quīdam* and not *aliquis*? | Cornēlia sees a particular man but doesn't name him: "a certain man." (*Quīdam* is nominative, masculine, agreeing with *vir*.) |
| *Quis es? Quid vīs?* Dad asks two things. Which is a person, which a thing? | *Quis es?* "Who are you?" (person). *Quid vīs?* "What do you want?" (thing). Both stand alone, so both are pronouns. |
| *Cuius est?* and *Quī puer hodiē in tabernā erat?* Which is the pronoun, which the adjective? | *Cuius est?*: alone, a pronoun ("whose is it?"; Chapter 10's chunk). *Quī puer*: *quī* sits on *puer*, an adjective ("which boy?"). |
| *Cui crustulum dō? Tibi, tabernārī!* Why *cui*, and why *tabernārī*? | *Cui*: receiver, dative ("to whom?"). *Tabernārī*: vocative of *tabernārius*, ending in *-ī* like Chapter 9's *fīlī*. |
| *Iōannēs: …aliquid dulce: crustulum!* What is *aliquid dulce*? | "Something sweet": *aliquid* (neuter) with Chapter 20's neuter *dulce*. |
| *Quisque partem habet* and *Suum cuique!* Translate both. | "Each one has a piece" · "To each his own." Chapter 21's chunk, now fully see-through: *cuique* is "to each." |
| *Tū cāseum cuiusque ēdistī!* What did Grandpa do? | He ate **each person's** cheese: *cuiusque*, genitive of *quisque*. The dog (who barks twice) is innocent for once. |

## Vocābula: Words of this chapter

:: Quid novī? Verba nova! || What's new? New words! (teaching line)

New forms:

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| *quis, quid* | who? what? (alone) |
| *cuius · cui · quem · quō* | whose? · to whom? · whom? · by/with whom? (same as the relative) |
| *quī, quae, quod* | which? what? (with a noun: interrogative adjective) |
| *aliquis, aliquid* | someone, something |
| *aliquī, aliqua, aliquod* | some (with a noun) |
| *quīdam, quaedam, quiddam / quoddam* | a certain (one, thing) |
| *quendam, quandam; cuiusdam, cuidam; quōrundam* | a certain (object, of, to; plural "of") |
| *quisque, quidque / quodque; cuique, cuiusque* | each one; to each, of each |

New words:

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| *rogō, rogāre, rogāvī, rogātum* | ask |
| *nēmō* | no one, nobody |
| *nihil* | nothing |
| *faber, fabrī* (m.) | maker, craftsman |
| *Quid novī?* | What's new? |
| *Quid agis?* | How are you? (What are you doing?) |
| *interrogative* | a question word |
| *interrogative pronoun* | a question word standing alone (*quis, quid*) |
| *interrogative adjective* | a question word sitting on a noun (*quī vir? quod nōmen?*) |
| *indefinite* | a word that doesn't point at any one particular person or thing (*aliquis, quīdam*) |

Glossed in passing (no card): *interrogō* I ask, *vēritās* truth, *custōdiō* guard, *custōs* guard, *novus* new, *Āfrica* Africa, *cīvitās* city, *nisi* unless, *tabernārius* shopkeeper, *dēnārius* denarius, *invēnī* I found.

## Speak it aloud

:: Quis dīcit? Tū dīcis! || Who is speaking? You are! (teaching line)

1. Chant the singular question word: *quis, cuius, cui, quem, quō* · *quid, cuius, cui, quid, quō*. Then the plural: *quī, quae, quae · quōrum, quārum · quibus · quōs, quās, quae*.
2. Say the three Laws in your own words: Question Words (two new shapes), Alone and Company (*quis/quid* vs *quī/quae/quod*), Cousins (a tag on the question word).
3. Ask five questions about one sentence: *Avus Quīntō cāseum dat.* (*Quis? Quid? Cui?* …)
4. Ask a question with a noun: *Quod mālum? Quae puella? Quī vir? Quem librum?*
5. Say "someone / nobody / something / nothing," then "a certain man / each one / if anyone."
6. The real lines: *Quis custōdiet ipsōs custōdēs?* · *Cui bonō?* · *Quōs ego—!* · *Homō quīdam habuit duōs fīliōs.* · *Faber est suae quisque fortūnae.* · *Suum cuique.*
7. Read the *Lectiō* aloud, playing every part (the dog too). Then hide the Latin and rebuild it.

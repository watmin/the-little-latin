# Caput XXV: Vēnī, Vīdī, Vīcī

*Liber III, Fābulae · Chapter 25*

*In which Caesar's three words finally open. Chapter 6 taught you to hear the macron that turns* Venī! *into* vēnī; *Chapter 16 promised a finished-fact past beside the imperfect's film; Liber II kept saying* heri *with*-bam*. *Today you get the dictionary's four keys, the perfect stem, one chant that fits every family, and the irregulars you already half-know (*fuī, īvī/iī, tulī, dedī*). Speaking goal: what did you do?*

!!! abstract "In this chapter"
    - **Saying:** *Vēnī, vīdī, vīcī.* · *Quid fēcistī?* · *Heri Rōmam iī.* · *Cāseum ēmī.* · *Fuī.* · *Dedī tibi.*
    - **Grammar words, in plain English:** principal parts (the four dictionary keys of a verb); perfect (the finished-fact past — "I …ed / I have …ed"); perfect stem (the stem the perfect is built on); present stem (the stem you've used since Book I)
    - **Forms:** perfect active of all conjugations: *-ī, -istī, -it, -imus, -istis, -ērunt*; regular patterns (*amāvī, habuī, audīvī*) and wild stems (*lēgī, cēpī, fēcī, dīxī, vēnī, vīdī, vīcī*); irregulars *fuī, iī/īvī, tulī, dedī, potuī*
    - **Laws:** the Law of the Four Keys; the Law of the Finished Fact
    - **Words:** verbs listed with four principal parts; *perfectum*; *heri* stories retold as snapshots
    - **Reading:** *Quid fēcistī?* (the morning after the feast — what everyone did)
    - **Speaking:** say what you did yesterday in the perfect

## 1. Caesar's door

:: Vēnī, vīdī, vīcī. || I came, I saw, I conquered. (Julius Caesar, 47 BC; Suetonius, *Julius* 37)

Vēnī. Vīdī. Vīcī. Quid fēcistī?

{#g-01}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 6: *Venī!* vs Caesar's *vēnī*. What's the difference? | Command *venī* has a short e: "Come!" Caesar's ***vēnī*** has a long ē: "**I came.**" One macron flips the clock. |
| Say all three of Caesar's words, classical. | WAY-nee, WEE-dee, WEE-kee. *Vēnī, vīdī, vīcī*: I came, I saw, I conquered. |
| Chapter 16 called the imperfect a film still running. What did it promise for one finished act? | The **perfect**: a snapshot. "I sang (once, done)," not "I was singing." Today that tense arrives. |
| Plain English: the **perfect** is the finished-fact past. Old-school name? | Perfect tense / perfect active indicative. Latin *perfectum* = "completed, finished." English often: "I came" or "I have come." |
| *Quid fēcistī?* Guess from *faciō* and the new endings you'll learn. | "What did you do?" / "What have you done?" The speaking goal of this chapter. |
| *Vēnī* is "I came." Who is the "I"? | Caesar — first person singular. The ending ***-ī*** will mean "I" in the perfect, every family. |
| So Liber III opens how? | With finished facts: what happened, what you **did**, not only what was going on. |

## 2. Four keys

:: Nōsce tē. || Know yourself. (after Cicero, *Tusculans* 1.52; Chapter 21)

Amō, amāre, amāvī, amātum. Quattuor clāvēs.

{#g-02}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 5 / 6: how has this book listed a verb so far? | Two forms: "I do it" and "to do it." *Amō, amāre.* *Habeō, habēre.* *Legō, legere.* |
| Real dictionaries list **four**. Look: *amō, amāre, amāvī, amātum.* Name them in order. | 1) *amō* — I love (present). 2) *amāre* — to love (infinitive). 3) ***amāvī*** — I loved / I have loved (perfect). 4) *amātum* — the fourth key (you'll need it for passives later; learn it now as the dictionary's last form). |
| Plain English: **principal parts** are the four dictionary keys of a verb. Old-school? | "Principal parts" (from *prīncipālis*, chief). Memorize the four; they unlock every tense. |
| Why four, not two? | Key 1 + 2 build the **present stem** (everything you've conjugated so far: present, imperfect, future). Key 3 builds the **perfect stem** (today). Key 4 waits for Book III's passives (Chapter 31). |
| *Habeō, habēre, habuī, habitum.* Find the perfect "I." | ***Habuī***: "I had / I have had." Not *habēvī* — second conjugation often uses *-uī*. |
| *Audiō, audīre, audīvī, audītum.* Perfect "I"? | ***Audīvī***: "I heard / I have heard." Fourth conjugation likes *-īvī*. |
| *Legō, legere, lēgī, lēctum.* Is the perfect predictable from *legere*? | Not really. ***Lēgī*** (long ē): "I read / I have read." Third conjugation often has a wild perfect stem — that's why the dictionary prints it. |
| So the rule for learning verbs from now on? | Learn **all four keys**. Two was enough for the present system; four are enough for the whole verb. |

!!! tip "Principal parts"
    The **principal parts** (four keys) are the dictionary's four forms of a verb: present "I," infinitive, perfect "I," and the fourth form (*-tum / -sum*). Example: *amō, amāre, amāvī, amātum*. From today, Vocābula lists verbs this way.

!!! success "The Law of the Four Keys"
    Every full Latin verb has **four principal parts**. Keys 1–2 give the **present stem** (present, imperfect, future — Books I–II). Key 3 gives the **perfect stem** (perfect active — today). Key 4 is banked for the perfect passive system later. **Learn the four; don't invent the third.**

## 3. Two stems

:: Ab ōvō. || From the egg (from the very beginning). (after Horace, *Ars Poetica* 147)

Amā- / amāv-. Leg- / lēg-. Cap- / cēp-.

{#g-03}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| From *amāre*, strip *-re*. What's left? | ***Amā-***: the **present stem**. *Amō, amābam, amābō* all ride on it. |
| From *amāvī*, strip the person ending *-ī*. What's left? | ***Amāv-***: the **perfect stem**. Every perfect form of *amō* starts here. |
| Plain English: the **perfect stem** is the stem the perfect is built on. How do you find it? | Take principal part 3, drop *-ī*: *amāvī → amāv-*, *habuī → habu-*, *lēgī → lēg-*, *vēnī → vēn-*. |
| *Videō, vidēre, vīdī, vīsum.* Present stem vs perfect stem? | Present ***vidē-*** (*videō, vidēbam, vidēbō*). Perfect ***vīd-*** (*vīdī…*). Long ī in the perfect — Caesar's *vīdī*. |
| *Veniō, venīre, vēnī, ventum.* Same split? | Present ***venī-*** (short e: *veniō, veniēbam*). Perfect ***vēn-*** (long ē: *vēnī*). Chapter 6's macron, explained. |
| *Capiō, capere, cēpī, captum.* | Present ***capi-*** / *cap-*. Perfect ***cēp-*** (long ē). *Cēpī*: I took / I have taken. |
| *Faciō, facere, fēcī, factum.* | Perfect ***fēc-***: *fēcī*, I did / I made. So *Quid fēcistī?* is this stem + "you" ending. |
| Can you build *amāvī* from the present stem alone? | **No.** *Amā- + ī* is not how Latin works. You need the perfect stem from key 3. Some verbs rhyme with the present (*amāvī*), many don't (*lēgī, fēcī, tulī*). |

## 4. The finished-fact chant

:: Factum est. || It is done. / It has been done. (recognition; passive sense waits for Chapter 31)

Amāvī, amāvistī, amāvit. Amāvimus, amāvistis, amāvērunt.

{#g-04}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Warm up: present of *amō*, all six. | *Amō, amās, amat, amāmus, amātis, amant.* |
| Perfect: *amāvī, amāvistī, amāvit, amāvimus, amāvistis, amāvērunt.* What is glued to *amāv-* each time? | The perfect person endings: ***-ī, -istī, -it, -imus, -istis, -ērunt***. |
| Chant the six endings alone. | *-ī, -istī, -it, -imus, -istis, -ērunt.* "I, you, he/she/it, we, y'all, they." |
| Map them to English. | *Amāvī* I loved / have loved · *amāvistī* you loved · *amāvit* he/she loved · *amāvimus* we loved · *amāvistis* y'all loved · *amāvērunt* they loved. |
| Which ending is new compared with the present's *-ō, -s, -t…*? | Almost all of them. Only a family resemblance: *-t* still "he/she," *-mus/-tis* still we/y'all — but the shapes are perfect-only. Learn this chant once; it fits **every** conjugation. |
| Long vowels: *amāvī, amāvistī, amāvit, amāvimus, amāvistis, amāvērunt* — where are the long marks on the endings? | ***-ī*** long; ***-istī*** ends long ī; ***-ērunt*** has long ē. The middle *-it* is short i. |
| "I carried the cheese." (*Portō, portāre, portāvī, portātum*) | ***Cāseum portāvī.*** |
| "Y'all loved the dog." | ***Canem amāvistis.*** |
| "They worked yesterday." (*Labōrō*) | ***Heri labōrāvērunt.*** |

!!! success "The Law of the Finished Fact"
    Build the perfect active from the **perfect stem** (principal part 3 minus *-ī*) + one set of endings for every family:

    | | ending | *amō* |
    |---|---|---|
    | I | *-ī* | *amāvī* |
    | you | *-istī* | *amāvistī* |
    | he, she, it | *-it* | *amāvit* |
    | we | *-imus* | *amāvimus* |
    | y'all | *-istis* | *amāvistis* |
    | they | *-ērunt* | *amāvērunt* |

    Meaning: a **finished fact** — "I …ed" / "I have …ed." The imperfect (*amābam*) is still the film; the perfect (*amāvī*) is the snapshot. Which past to pick when both fit is Chapter 26's job — today, learn the snapshot.

## 5. Every family

:: Audīvī, vīdī, lēgī. || I heard, I saw, I read. (built for this chapter)

Habuī. Lēgī. Audīvī. Cēpī. Dīxī.

{#g-05}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| First conjugation pattern: many *-āre* verbs form perfect *-āvī*. Chant *portō* perfect, all six. | *Portāvī, portāvistī, portāvit, portāvimus, portāvistis, portāvērunt.* |
| Second conjugation: *habeō → habuī*. Chant all six. | *Habuī, habuistī, habuit, habuimus, habuistis, habuērunt.* |
| *Videō → vīdī* (not *viduī*). "I saw, you saw, he saw." | *Vīdī, vīdistī, vīdit.* |
| Third conjugation: trust the dictionary. *Legō → lēgī.* All six? | *Lēgī, lēgistī, lēgit, lēgimus, lēgistis, lēgērunt.* |
| *Dīcō, dīcere, dīxī, dictum.* "I said." "They said." | *Dīxī. Dīxērunt.* (*Dīx-* from *dīc-* + *s* sound.) |
| *Scrībō, scrībere, scrīpsī, scrīptum.* "We wrote." | *Scrīpsimus.* |
| Fourth: *audiō → audīvī.* All six. | *Audīvī, audīvistī, audīvit, audīvimus, audīvistis, audīvērunt.* |
| *-iō* verbs: *capiō → cēpī*, *faciō → fēcī*. "I took the cheese." "What did Marcus do?" | *Cāseum cēpī. Quid Marcus fēcit?* |
| *Vincō, vincere, vīcī, victum.* Caesar's third word, fully declined in "I / you / he." | *Vīcī, vīcistī, vīcit.* |
| Success check: same endings on *amāv-, habu-, lēg-, audīv-, cēp-*. | Yes. **One chant, every family.** Only the stem changes. |

!!! success "Patterns (not promises)"
    | Family | Often… | Example | Watch for wild cards |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | 1st (*-āre*) | *-āvī* | *amāvī, portāvī* | a few irregulars (*dō → dedī*) |
    | 2nd (*-ēre*) | *-uī* or *-ēvī* | *habuī*; *vīdī* is wild | learn key 3 |
    | 3rd (*-ere*) | many shapes | *lēgī, dīxī, scrīpsī, vīcī* | always check the dictionary |
    | 4th (*-īre*) | *-īvī* | *audīvī, vēnī* is wild | *veniō → vēnī* |
    | *-iō* | stem change | *cēpī, fēcī* | like third, wild |

## 6. Early irregulars

:: Fuī quod es, eris quod sum. || I was what you are; you will be what I am. (tombstone formula; recognition)

Sum, esse, fuī. Eō, īre, iī. Ferō, ferre, tulī. Dō, dare, dedī.

{#g-06}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Sum, esse, fuī, futūrus.* Perfect "I was / I have been," all six. | *Fuī, fuistī, fuit, fuimus, fuistis, fuērunt.* Same endings on *fu-*. |
| Chapter 16's *eram* vs today's *fuī*? | *Eram* = imperfect "I was (being / in a state)." *Fuī* = perfect "I was / I have been" as a finished fact. Full contrast: Chapter 26. For now, know both forms exist. |
| *Eō, īre, iī* (or *īvī*), *itum.* Both *iī* and *īvī* are real. This book prefers the short set commonly printed: *iī, īstī, iit, iimus, īstis, iērunt.* | "I went, you went…" *Īvī, īvistī…* is also fine when you see it. |
| "Yesterday we went to Rome." | ***Heri Rōmam iimus.*** (Or *īvimus*.) |
| *Ferō, ferre, tulī, lātum.* Perfect stem? | ***Tul-*** — nothing like *fer-*. *Tulī, tulistī, tulit, tulimus, tulistis, tulērunt.* "I carried / brought." |
| Chapter 6's command *fer!* now has a perfect twin. "The bird carried off the cheese." | ***Avis cāseum tulit.*** (Chapter 15's gull, finished.) |
| *Dō, dare, dedī, datum.* Short *da-* in the present; perfect ***ded-***. All six? | *Dedī, dedistī, dedit, dedimus, dedistis, dedērunt.* |
| Chapter 11's *dabō* was future. "I gave you the cheese." | ***Cāseum tibi dedī.*** |
| *Possum, posse, potuī.* Chapter 18 teased this. "I couldn't" as a finished fact? | ***Nōn potuī.*** "I wasn't able (and that's the fact)." Imperfect *nōn poteram* = the long scene of not being able. |
| Caesar again, with dictionary tags: | *Veniō, venīre, vēnī, ventum* · *Videō, vidēre, vīdī, vīsum* · *Vincō, vincere, vīcī, victum.* |

## 7. Yesterday, finished

:: Heri vīdī; hodiē sciō. || Yesterday I saw; today I know. (teaching line; *sciō* recognition)

Heri natābam. Heri natāvī. Quid interest?

{#g-07}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 16: *Heri in marī natābam.* Film or snapshot? | Film: "I was swimming / kept swimming." The imperfect. |
| Same day, finished: "Yesterday I swam (once / done)." (*Natō, natāre, natāvī, natātum*) | ***Heri natāvī.*** Snapshot. |
| *Heri cāseum portābam* vs *Heri cāseum portāvī.* | *Portābam*: I was carrying it (scene). *Portāvī*: I carried it (done — maybe it's already in Grandpa's stomach). |
| Liber II payoffs — retell as perfects: "We bought a cheese." (*Emō, emere, ēmī, emptum*) | ***Cāseum ēmimus.*** |
| "I brought Grandpa a big cheese." (Chapter 16's promise, now done) | ***Cāseum magnum Avō portāvī.*** (Or *tulī*.) |
| "The dog won." (*Vincō*) | ***Canis vīcit.*** |
| "What did y'all do?" "What did he do?" | ***Quid fēcistis? Quid fēcit?*** |
| Careful: do *amābam* and *amāvī* mean the same thing? | Not exactly. Both are past, but **aspect** differs: ongoing/repeated vs finished. Chapter 26 (*Erat et Fuit*) trains you to choose. Today, prefer the perfect when you mean "I did X (fact)." |
| Fence for later: *amāveram* (pluperfect), *amāverō* (future perfect)? | Not today — Chapter 27. Passive perfect (*amātus sum*): Chapter 31. |

!!! warning "Tease — film vs snapshot"
    Same yesterday can wear either tense. *Heri Avus dormiēbat* (scene: he was sleeping). *Heri Avus dormīvit* (fact: he slept). Liber III Caput II will make you choose on purpose. Today: **form the snapshot correctly.**

## 8. Lectiō: *Quid fēcistī?*

:: Quid fēcistī, Iōannēs? || What did you do, John?

Māne est. Familia domī est. Heri cāseum ēdērunt. Hodiē… quid fēcērunt? Audī perfecta!

Read each Latin line aloud. Guess the English, then tap to check. For the reverse drill, tap **Tege Latīna** and rebuild each line from the English. New perfects do the work; a few glosses sit in the comprehension questions.

::: dialogue lectio
Avia | Salvēte, līberī! Quid heri fēcistis? || Grandma | Morning, kids! What did y'all do yesterday?
Marcus | Ego ad tabernam iī! || Marcus | I went to the shop!
Quīntus | Et ego quoque iī. Cāseum ēmimus! || Quīntus | And I went too. We bought cheese!
Cornēlia | Ego canem dūxī. Cāseus vēnit mēcum. || Cornēlia | I led the dog. Cheese came with me.
Cāseus | Bau! || Cheese | Woof!
Iōannēs | Ego Rōmam iī et cāseum magnum vīdī. Ēmī eum. || John | I went to Rome and saw a big cheese. I bought it.
Avus | Et mihi eum dedistī? || Grandpa | And you gave it to me?
Iōannēs | Dedī tibi, Ave! Sed… || John | I gave it to you, Grandpa! But…
Avia | Sed quid? || Grandma | But what?
Marcus | Canis cāseum cēpit! || Marcus | The dog took the cheese!
Quīntus | Et tulit eum in hortum! || Quīntus | And carried it into the garden!

Pater | Ego labōrāvī. Nihil vīdī. || Father | I worked. I saw nothing.
Māter | Ego librum lēgī. Tū, Pater, cantāvistī! || Mother | I read a book. You, Dad, sang!
Līberī | Ēheu! Audīvimus! || The kids | Oh no! We heard!
Avus | Ego cāseum ēdī! Fuī fēlīx! || Grandpa | I ate the cheese! I was happy!
Avia | Tū ēdistī? An canis ēdit? || Grandma | You ate it? Or did the dog eat it?

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Quid heri fēcistis?* Person and tense? | Perfect, "y'all": *fēc-istis*. Grandma wants finished facts. |
| *Ego ad tabernam iī.* Which irregular? | *Eō → iī*: "I went." |
| *Cāseum ēmimus.* Stem and ending? | Perfect stem *ēm-* (*emō, emere, ēmī*) + *-imus*: "we bought." |
| *Canis cāseum cēpit! · tulit eum* | *Cēpit* from *capiō*; *tulit* from *ferō*. Two wild perfects, one crime. |
| *Ego labōrāvī. · librum lēgī. · cantāvistī!* | Regular *-āvī* (*labōrāvī, cantāvistī*) beside wild *lēgī*. |
| *Ego cāseum ēdī! Fuī fēlīx!* | *Ēdī* from *edō, edere, ēdī, ēsum* ("I ate"); *fuī* from *sum*. Grandpa's finished happiness. |
| *Vēnī, vīdī… Canis vīcit!* | Caesar's door, paid off as a joke: John came and saw; the **dog** conquered. |
| *Ambō ēdimus!* (*Ambō*: both — gloss.) | Perfect "we ate": Grandpa and Cāseus share the stomach gag. |
| Untaught / glossed? | *Dūxī* from *dūcō, dūcere, dūxī, ductum* (Ch6 *dūc*); *lātrāre* infinitive after *audīvī*; *nihil* nothing; *ambō* both; *partem* object of *ēdī*. |
| Aspect tease in the Lectiō? | All verbs are perfect snapshots of Liber II adventures — the film versions lived in Chapters 16–24. |

## Speak it aloud

:: Dīcendō discimus. || We learn by speaking.

1. Chant the perfect endings alone: *-ī, -istī, -it, -imus, -istis, -ērunt*.
2. Chant *amō* perfect, all six; then *habeō* (*habuī…*); then *audiō* (*audīvī…*).
3. Wild stems, fast: *lēgī, dīxī, scrīpsī, cēpī, fēcī, vēnī, vīdī, vīcī*.
4. Irregulars: *fuī…fuērunt* · *iī…iērunt* · *tulī…tulērunt* · *dedī…dedērunt* · *potuī*.
5. Caesar: *Vēnī, vīdī, vīcī.* Then *Quid fēcistī? Quid fēcistis?*
6. Snapshot sentences: *Heri Rōmam iī. Cāseum ēmī. Avō dedī. Canis vīcit. Fuī fēlīx.*
7. Read the *Lectiō* aloud, playing every part (Cheese too). Then hide the Latin and rebuild it.

# Caput XXIII: Manus et Diēs

*Liber II, Rēs Cotīdiānae · Chapter 23*

*In which the last two noun families open. Chapter 14 made you point with a* digitus *because* manus *was waiting; Chapter 1's* Nūlla diēs sine līneā *and Chapter 22's* Carpe diem *kept* diēs *as a chunk. Today the hand gets every job, the day and the thing (*rēs!*) get every job, and all five families sit side by side at last. Then the family argues about whose hands held the cheese — and on which day it vanished.*

!!! abstract "In this chapter"
    - **Saying:** *Manus manum lavat.* · *Carpe diem.* · *Nūlla diēs sine līneā.* · *Rēs pūblica.* · *In manū.* · *Diē tertiā.* · *Quid rēī est?*
    - **Grammar words, in plain English:** fourth declension (the fourth family — the long-ū family); fifth declension (the fifth family — the long-ē family); declension (a family of noun endings — you now have all five)
    - **Forms:** *manus, manūs* f.; *cornū, cornūs* n. (light); *diēs, diēī* m.; *rēs, reī* f.; five-family overview table
    - **Laws:** the Law of the Long ū; the Law of the Day; the Law of Five Families
    - **Words:** *manus, cornū, genū, exercitus, senātus, diēs, rēs, fidēs, spēs, pūblicus, merīdiēs*
    - **Reading:** *Manūs et rēs* (whose hands, which day, what thing — and where the cheese went)
    - **Speaking:** decline *manus* and *diēs*; say *in manū*, *diē tertiā*, *rēs magna*

!!! tip "How to read this chapter"
    Same as always. Say your answer **out loud**, then tap the wax tablet to check. Keep Chapter 14's body words, Chapter 8's *domum / domī / domō*, and Chapter 22's *Carpe diem* within reach. The trick today is the **long vowel** in the "of" form: *-ūs* for the fourth family, *-ēī / -eī* for the fifth.

## 1. *Manus* — the hand that waited

:: Manus manum lavat. || One hand washes the other. (Petronius, *Satyricon* 45; Chapters 4 and 21)

Ecce manus. Ecce manus Marcī. Manum videō. In manū cāseus est.

{#g-01}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 14 left the hand for later: *Manus* belongs to a fourth family. Point at your hand and name it. | ***Manus***, f.: hand. MA-nus (short a). Feminine — even though it ends in *-us* like *hortus*. |
| Chapter 4 / 21's motto: *Manus manum lavat.* Which *manus* is the subject? Which the object? | Subject ***manus*** (naming form). Object ***manum*** ("the other hand"). One hand washes another hand — not *sē*. |
| "Marcus's hand." Guess the "of" form. Does it rhyme with *hortī*? | ***Manūs*** Marcī. Long **ū**: ma-NOOS. Not *manī*. The fourth family's "of" ending is ***-ūs***, not *-ī*. |
| Take off *-ūs* from *manūs*. What's the stem? | ***Manu-***. The fourth family is the **u-stem** family — the long-ū family. |
| Grammar name? | ***Fourth declension***: the fourth family of nouns. Plain English: the long-ū family. Old-school: "fourth declension" / "u-stems." Most members end in *-us* and are masculine; a famous few (*manus, domus*) are feminine. |
| "I see a hand." "In the hand" / "by hand." | Object: ***manum***. Ablative: ***in manū*** / ***manū*** (long ū). Dative ***manuī*** (ma-nu-ee: three syllables). *In manū cāseus est.* |
| Chant singular *manus*: subject, of, to/for, object, by/with/from. | *Manus, manūs, manuī, manum, manū.* Circle the long *ū* in *manūs* and *manū*. |
| The motto again: *Manus manum lavat.* Why not *Manus sē lavat*? | Because one hand washes **another** hand (*manum*), not itself. Chapter 21's *sē* would mean the hand washes itself. |

!!! tip "Fourth declension"
    The **fourth declension** (fourth family) is the long-ū family. Dictionary pair: *manus, manūs*. Genitive singular ends in ***-ūs*** (long). Most *-us* nouns here are masculine (*senātus, exercitus*); *manus* and *domus* are feminine. Neuters end in *-ū* (*cornū, genū*) — §3.

## 2. Many hands — and the Law of the Long ū

:: Multae manūs onus levant. || Many hands lighten a load. (teaching proverb; cf. the English saying)

Duae manūs. Manūs puerōrum. Cum manibus. Manibus cāseum dō.

{#g-02}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Plural subject: "hands." Guess from the genitive's long *ū*. | ***Manūs.*** Same spelling as the genitive singular! Company tells them apart: *manūs Marcī* (one boy's hand — "of") vs *manūs puerōrum* (boys' hands — subject plural). |
| Object plural? | Also ***manūs.*** Subject = object in the plural (like third-family *-ēs*). *Manūs videō* — "I see hands." |
| "Of hands." | ***Manuum.*** *-uum* (two u's): ma-nu-um. |
| "To/for hands" and "with/from hands." | ***Manibus*** for both (Double Duty, like third-family *-ibus*). *Manibus cāseum dō. Cum manibus labōrō.* |
| "Two hands." Gender of *manus*? | *Duae manūs* — feminine, so *duae* (Chapter 22). |
| "In Grandpa's hands." | *In manibus Avī.* |
| Full singular + plural of *manus*: | See the success box below. |
| Trap: *hortus, hortī* (2nd) vs *manus, manūs* (4th). Both end *-us* in the naming form. How do you know the family? | The **dictionary genitive**: *-ī* → second family; ***-ūs*** → fourth. Learn the pair. |
| The teaching line *Multae manūs onus levant.* (*Onus, oneris*, n.: load; *levant*: they lift.) | "Many hands lighten a load." *Multae* agrees with feminine *manūs*. |

!!! success "*Manus, manūs* (f.) — fourth family"
    | | singular | plural |
    |---|---|---|
    | **subject** | *manus* | *manūs* |
    | **of** | *manūs* | *manuum* |
    | **to/for** | *manuī* | *manibus* |
    | **object** | *manum* | *manūs* |
    | **by/with/from** | *manū* | *manibus* |

!!! warning "Law of the Long ū"
    In the **fourth family**, watch the long **ū**:
    - Genitive singular ***-ūs*** (and nominative/accusative plural ***-ūs***) — same letters; sort by company.
    - Ablative singular ***-ū***.
    - Dative singular ***-uī*** (or sometimes bare *-ū*).
    - Genitive plural ***-uum***; dative/ablative plural ***-ibus***.
    Naming form *-us* looks like the second family — trust the genitive pair, not the naming form alone.

## 4. *Diēs* — seize the day, decline the day

:: Carpe diem. || Seize the day. (Horace, *Odes* 1.11.8; Chapters 6 and 22)

Nūlla diēs sine līneā. Diēs longus. Diem carpō. Diē tertiā.

{#g-04}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 22 left *diēs / diem* as chunks. Open them. Naming form? | ***Diēs***, "day." DEE-ays. Fifth family. |
| "Of the day" / "to the day." | ***Diēī*** (dee-AY-ee: three syllables; long *ē* before *-ī*). Same form for genitive and dative singular. |
| Object? Ablative? | Object ***diem*** (short e — you already used it in *Carpe diem*). Ablative ***diē*** (long ē): "on the day…" |
| Chant singular *diēs*. | *Diēs, diēī, diēī, diem, diē.* |
| Plural: subjects/objects ***diēs***; of ***diērum***; to/with ***diēbus***. | *Multī diēs. Diērum. Diēbus.* |
| Gender: *diēs* is usually **masculine** (*diēs longus*). Sometimes **feminine** for an appointed / set day (*cōnstitūtā diē*, "on the set day"). Plural is masculine. | Learn *diēs* as m. by default; watch for set-day phrases in f. |
| Chapter 1 / 3: *Nūlla diēs sine līneā.* Why *nūlla* if *diēs* is usually m.? | Here *diēs* is treated as feminine ("no day…") — a set / abstract day. Pliny of the painter Apelles: no day without a line of work. Both genders show up in real Latin. |
| Chapter 22's Law of When: *hōrā tertiā*. Now: "on the third day." | ***Diē tertiā.*** Ablative of time — *diē* + ordinal. (*Tertiā* feminine if *diē* is felt as f. in the set-day sense; *diē tertiō* also occurs. Recognition: bare ablative = when.) |
| *Merīdiēs*, m.: midday, noon. Same family. | *Merīdiē* — "at noon" (ablative of time). |
| Grammar name? | ***Fifth declension***: the fifth family. Plain English: the long-ē family. Old-school: "fifth declension" / "ē-stems." Dictionary pair ends in ***-ēī*** or ***-eī***. |

!!! tip "Fifth declension"
    The **fifth declension** (fifth family) is the long-ē family. Almost all are feminine (*rēs, fidēs, spēs*). The big exception: ***diēs*** (usually m.) and *merīdiēs* (m.). Only *diēs* and *rēs* are declined in full through the plural; most others are singular-heavy.

!!! success "*Diēs, diēī* (m.) — fifth family"
    | | singular | plural |
    |---|---|---|
    | **subject** | *diēs* | *diēs* |
    | **of** | *diēī* | *diērum* |
    | **to/for** | *diēī* | *diēbus* |
    | **object** | *diem* | *diēs* |
    | **by/with/from** | *diē* | *diēbus* |

## 5. *Rēs* — the thing itself

:: Rēs pūblica. || The public thing (the commonwealth / republic).

Quid rēī est? Rēs magna. Rem videō. Dē rē. Rērum.

{#g-05}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Rēs, reī*, f.: thing, matter, affair, business. The most useful fifth-family noun after *diēs*. | Naming ***rēs***. Genitive/dative ***reī*** (short e — *reī*, not *rēī*). Object ***rem***. Ablative ***rē***. |
| Chant singular *rēs*. | *Rēs, reī, reī, rem, rē.* |
| Plural? | *Rēs, rērum, rēbus, rēs, rēbus.* Of things: ***rērum***. With things / about affairs: ***rēbus*** / *dē rēbus*. |
| "A great thing." "I see the thing." "About the matter." | *Rēs magna. Rem videō. Dē rē.* |
| *Quid rēī est?* Literally "what of a thing is it?" | "What's the matter?" / "What's going on?" Partitive flavor: what (kind) of thing? |
| The motto *Rēs pūblica.* (*Pūblicus, pūblica, pūblicum*: public.) | "The public thing" → the commonwealth, the republic. English *republic* is this phrase worn smooth. *Rēs* stays feminine: *rēs pūblica magna*. |
| *Fidēs, fideī*, f.: faith, trust, loyalty (mostly singular). *Spēs, speī*, f.: hope (singular-heavy). | Recognition: *bonā fidē* "in good faith"; *spē* "in hope." Same fifth-family endings. |
| Why did Book II's subtitle say *Rēs Cotīdiānae*? | "Daily things / everyday affairs." *Rēs* + *cotīdiānus* (daily). You've been studying *rēs* since the cover — today the word opens. |
| Side by side: *diēī* (long ē) vs *reī* (short e). | After a vowel, the genitive is *-ēī* (*diēī*); after a consonant, classical Latin shortens: *reī, fideī, speī*. Say what you see in the dictionary. |

!!! warning "Law of the Day"
    In the **fifth family**:
    - Nominative singular ***-ēs***; genitive/dative singular ***-ēī*** or ***-eī***; accusative ***-em***; ablative ***-ē***.
    - Plural: ***-ēs, -ērum, -ēbus*** (nom./acc., gen., dat./abl.).
    - Only ***diēs*** and ***rēs*** run the full plural in everyday use.
    - Gender: almost all **feminine**; *diēs* usually **masculine** (sometimes f. for a set day).

!!! success "*Rēs, reī* (f.) — fifth family"
    | | singular | plural |
    |---|---|---|
    | **subject** | *rēs* | *rēs* |
    | **of** | *reī* | *rērum* |
    | **to/for** | *reī* | *rēbus* |
    | **object** | *rem* | *rēs* |
    | **by/with/from** | *rē* | *rēbus* |

## 6. All five families — the Law of Five Families

:: Quīnque familiae, ūna lingua. || Five families, one language. (teaching line)

Puella · hortus · rēx · manus · diēs. Five naming forms; five "of" forms.

{#g-06}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Family 1 — first declension: *puella*. Genitive? | *-ae*: *puellae*. (Chapter 10.) |
| Family 2 — second: *hortus / mālum*. Genitive? | *-ī*: *hortī, mālī*. |
| Family 3 — third: *rēx / nōmen / ovis*. Genitive? | *-is*: *rēgis, nōminis, ovis*. (Chapters 13–15.) |
| Family 4 — fourth: *manus*. Genitive? | ***-ūs***: *manūs*. |
| Family 5 — fifth: *diēs / rēs*. Genitive? | ***-ēī / -eī***: *diēī, reī*. |
| How does the dictionary tell you the family? | Nominative + genitive ending: *-ae* 1st, *-ī* 2nd, *-is* 3rd, *-ūs* 4th, *-ēī/-eī* 5th. **Always learn the pair.** |
| Ablative singular quick map: | 1st *-ā*; 2nd *-ō*; 3rd *-e* (i-stem often *-ī*); 4th ***-ū***; 5th ***-ē***. |
| Neuter Twins still hold in every family that has neuters: naming = object (sg.), plural *-a / -ia / -ua*. | 2nd *mālum*; 3rd *nōmen / mare*; 4th *cornū → cornua*. (5th has no everyday neuters.) |
| Look-alike drill: *manūs* vs *manus*; *diēs* sg. vs pl.; *reī* vs *rē*. | Long *ū* marks 4th "of"/plural; *diēs* same letters sg./pl. — use the verb/adjective; *reī* = of/to the thing, *rē* = by/about the thing. |

!!! success "Law of Five Families"
    Latin nouns live in **five families** (five declensions). The genitive singular is the family badge:

    | family | badge (gen. sg.) | example | abl. sg. |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | 1st | *-ae* | *puella, puellae* | *-ā* |
    | 2nd | *-ī* | *hortus, hortī* · *mālum, mālī* | *-ō* |
    | 3rd | *-is* | *rēx, rēgis* · *nōmen, nōminis* · *ovis, ovis* | *-e* / *-ī* |
    | 4th | *-ūs* | *manus, manūs* · *cornū, cornūs* | *-ū* |
    | 5th | *-ēī / -eī* | *diēs, diēī* · *rēs, reī* | *-ē* |

    Learn every noun as a **pair** + gender. The naming form alone will lie to you (*manus* looks like *hortus*; *diēs* looks like a 3rd-decl *rēx*-cousin).

## 7. Mix and catch

:: Errandō discitur. || By erring, one learns. (teaching line)

Manus an hortus? Diēs an rēs? Fix the traps.

{#g-07}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Fix: *Manī cāseum habeō.* (I have the cheese in my hand.) | ***Manū*** *cāseum habeō* — ablative *-ū*, not 2nd-decl *-ī*. |
| Fix: *Diēī longus est.* | ***Diēs*** *longus est* — subject is *diēs*, not the genitive. Or *Diēs longus est.* |
| Fix: *Cum manūs labōrō.* | *Cum **manibus** labōrō* — ablative plural *-ibus*. |
| Fix: *Rem pūblicum* (as nominative phrase). | ***Rēs pūblica*** — both feminine nominative. Accusative would be *rem pūblicam*. |
| Fix: *In diem tertium veniō* meaning "on the third day." | ***Diē tertiā*** (or *diē tertiō*) — ablative of time, not *in* + accusative. (*In diem* can mean "until / for the day" in other senses — not "on.") |
| Fix: *Manum manūs lavat* (trying to say the motto). | ***Manus manum** lavat* — subject *manus*, object *manum*. |

## 8. Lectiō: *Manūs et rēs*

:: Quae rēs? Cuius manūs? Quō diē? || What thing? Whose hands? On which day?

Hodiē. Familia domī est. Cāseī heri… ubi sunt?

::: dialogue lectio
Avia | Ubi sunt cāseī? Heri quattuor cāseōs emēbāmus! || Grandma | Where are the cheeses? Yesterday we were buying four!
Avus | In manibus meīs erant… || Grandpa | They were in my hands…
Avia | In manibus tuīs? An in stomachō tuō? || Grandma | In your hands? Or in your stomach?
Marcus | Ego manūs lavō. Cāseum nōn habeō! || Marcus | I'm washing my hands. I don't have cheese!
Quīntus | Ego quoque manūs lavō… || Quīntus | I'm washing my hands too…
Cornēlia | Sed ōs tuum olet, Quīnte! || Cornēlia | But your mouth smells, Quīntus!
Quīntus | Ēheu! || Quīntus | Oh no!
Iōannēs | Quālem rem quaerimus? Cāseum an rem pūblicam? || John | What sort of thing are we looking for? Cheese or the republic?
Avia | Rem magnam — cāseum magnum! Diē hesternō eum emēbāmus. || Grandma | A big thing — a big cheese! Yesterday we were buying it.
Pater | Ego diē hodiērnō labōrō. Manūs meae sunt… || Father | I'm working today. My hands are…
Māter | …in librīs tuīs, nōn in cāseō! || Mother | …in your books, not in the cheese!
Avus | Audīte! Rēs est gravis. Quis cāseum in manū habet? || Grandpa | Listen, y'all! The matter is serious. Who has the cheese in hand?
Cāseus | Bau! Bau! || Cheese (the dog) | Woof! Woof!
Marcus | Canis! In ōre canis est! || Marcus | The dog! It's in the dog's mouth!
Avia | Ō canis! Manūs tuās… id est, pedēs tuōs… || Grandma | O dog! Your hands… that is, your paws…
Iōannēs | Diē hodiērnō canis vincit. Carpe diem, Cāsee! || John | Today the dog wins. Seize the day, Cheese!
Avus | Carpe cāseum, nōn diem! || Grandpa | Seize the cheese, not the day!
Omnēs | In stomachō… || Everyone | In the stomach of…
Avus | …canis! Nōn Avī! Ēheu! Manūs meae vacuae sunt! || Grandpa | …the dog! Not Grandpa's! Oh no! My hands are empty!
Avia | Rēs pūblica cāseī… canī est! || Grandma | The cheese republic… belongs to the dog!
:::

{#g-08}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *In manibus meīs / tuīs* — which family, which case? | Fourth family, ablative plural *manibus* + possessive. |
| *Ego manūs lavō* — subject or object *manūs*? | Object plural: "I wash (my) hands." (Compare Chapter 21 *Ego mē lavō*.) |
| *Quālem rem quaerimus? · Rem magnam* | Fifth family object *rem*; *quālis* from Chapter 20. |
| *Diē hesternō / diē hodiērnō* | Ablative of time: "yesterday / today" as day-when. (*Hesternus / hodiērnus*: of yesterday / of today — gloss.) |
| *Quis cāseum in manū habet?* | Ablative singular *manū* — Law of the Long ū. |
| *Rēs est gravis* · *Rēs pūblica cāseī* | *Rēs* as "matter" and as the start of *rēs pūblica* (joke: the cheese commonwealth). |
| *Carpe diem, Cāsee! · Carpe cāseum!* | Horace paid off (*Cāsee*: vocative, Chapter 9); Grandpa prefers the edible object. |
| *Manūs meae vacuae sunt!* | Nominative plural *manūs* + *meae* (feminine agreement). |
| End: cheese in the **dog's** stomach — not Grandpa's. | Continuity twist: Cāseus the dog wins the cheese again (Chapter 20 race energy). |
| Untaught? | *Hesternus / hodiērnus* glossed in the Q\|A; *vacuus* "empty" glossable from context; *gravis* Chapter 20; *quālis* Chapter 20; *olet* Chapter 22. |

## Speak it aloud

:: Dīcendō discimus. || We learn by speaking.

1. Chant *manus* singular and plural from the success box; circle every long *ū*.
2. Chant *diēs* and *rēs* singular and plural.
3. Five-family badges out loud: *puellae, hortī, rēgis, manūs, diēī*.
4. Phrases: *in manū · in manibus · diē tertiā · merīdiē · quid rēī est? · rēs pūblica · dē rē*.
5. Old lines, now fully cracked: *Manus manum lavat · Carpe diem · Nūlla diēs sine līneā · Rēs pūblica*.
6. Look-alikes: *manus/manūs · diēs/diēī · reī/rē · manuī/manū*.
7. Read the *Lectiō* aloud, playing every part. Then hide the Latin and rebuild it.

# Caput XVI: Heri et Crās

*Liber II, Rēs Cotīdiānae · Chapter 16*

*In which your verbs finally leave the present. Every verb so far has happened now. Today you learn to say what was going on yesterday and what will happen tomorrow, with one tiny sign tucked into the middle of the verb. Then old chunks crack open (*amābō tē*, *dabō tibi*, *Hodiē mihi, crās tibi*), and the family, the morning after the sea, remembers the thieving gull and plans a new cheese.*

!!! abstract "In this chapter"
    - **Saying:** *Heri in lītore erāmus.* · *Crās domī manēbimus.* · *Amābō tē!* · *Dabō tibi.* · *In prīncipiō erat Verbum.*
    - **Grammar words, in plain English:** tense (the time of the verb), present (now), imperfect (was …ing, used to), future (will), tense sign
    - **Forms:** *amābam… amābant*, *habēbam… habēbant*; *amābō… amābunt*, *habēbō… habēbunt*; *eram… erant*, *erō… erunt*; *ībam, ībō*
    - **Laws:** the Law of the Time Signs, the Law of the Clock
    - **Words:** time words: yesterday, today, tomorrow, now, then, soon, always, often, never, already, in the morning, in the evening
    - **Reading:** *Heri et crās* (the morning after the sea)
    - **Speaking:** say what you were doing yesterday and what you'll do tomorrow

!!! tip "How to read this chapter"
    Same as always. Say your answer **out loud**, then tap the wax tablet to check. This chapter is about verbs, so keep the Chapter 5 chant handy: *amō, amās, amat, amāmus, amātis, amant*. Every new form is that chant with a little something added.

## 1. *Tempus*

:: Tempus fugit. || Time flies. (after Vergil, *Georgics* 3.284)

Hodiē cantō. Heri cantābam. Crās cantābō.

{#g-01}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 5: *Cantō*, "I sing." When is that happening? | Now (or as a habit: "I sing in the shower"). Every verb in this book so far has been about now. |
| Grammarians have a name for the "when" of a verb. | **Tense**: the time of the verb. It comes, through French, from Latin *tempus*, time (Chapter 14). Nothing to do with feeling tense. |
| The "now" tense is the **present**. Read the blurb: *Hodiē cantō. Heri cantābam. Crās cantābō.* You know *hodiē* (Chapter 5) and *crās* (Chapter 11's tombstone). Guess *heri*. | "Today I sing. **Yesterday** I was singing. Tomorrow I will sing." *Heri* (HE-ri) is yesterday. |
| Line up *cantō, cantābam, cantābō*. What stays the same? What's new? | *Cantā-* stays (the *cantā-* of *cantāre*). New: ***-bam*** for "was singing," ***-bō*** for "will sing." A little sign in the middle tells the time. |
| English uses helper words: "**was** singing," "**will** sing." What does Latin do? | Glues the time onto the verb: one word. The same way *cantō* already glues "I" onto "sing." |
| The "will" tense is the **future**. Now an old chunk from Chapter 11: *amābō tē*. Split *amābō*. | *Amā-* (love) + ***-bō***: "I will love." *Amābō tē*: "I will love you," the Roman "pretty please." Chapter 11 promised this. It's no longer just a chunk. |
| Chapter 11's motto: *Dā mihi factum, dabō tibi iūs.* Find the future. | ***Dabō***, "I will give": *da-* (short a, as in *dare*) + *-bō*. "Give me the facts, and I'll give you the law." |
| The "was …ing" tense is the **imperfect**. Why that name? | Latin *imperfectum*, "not finished" (*perfectum* = done). It shows an action **still going on** ("was singing") or **happening again and again** ("used to sing"). |

## 2. *Cantābam*

:: Et quod temptābam scrībere, versus erat. || And whatever I tried to write came out as verse. (Ovid, *Tristia* 4.10.26)

Amābam, amābās, amābat. Amābāmus, amābātis, amābant.

{#g-02}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Warm up with Chapter 5: all six persons of *amō* in the present. | *Amō, amās, amat, amāmus, amātis, amant.* I love, you love, he/she loves, we love, y'all love, they love. |
| Now the imperfect: *amābam, amābās, amābat, amābāmus, amābātis, amābant.* What's glued into every form? | ***-bā-*** (sometimes *-ba-*): *amā + ba + m*, *amā + bā + s*… That's the **tense sign** of the imperfect, the mark that says "was …ing." |
| Look at the very ends: *-m, -s, -t, -mus, -tis, -nt*. Which do you know? | All but one: *-s* you, *-t* he/she/it, *-mus* we, *-tis* y'all, *-nt* they, the same as the present. Only "I" is new: ***-m*** instead of *-ō*. |
| Where have you already seen "I" end in *-m*? | ***Sum***, "I am" (Chapter 5, "old and stubborn"). It's an old "I" ending. *Amābam*, I was loving. |
| Long or short: the *a* of *-ba-* in *amābam, amābās, amābat, amābāmus, amābātis, amābant*? | Short, long, short, long, long, short. Chapter 5's habit (*amās* but *amat, amant*): a vowel shrinks before final *-t* and *-nt*, and before final *-m* too. |
| *Marcus cantābat.* Give three Englishes. | "Marcus was singing." "Marcus used to sing." "Marcus kept singing." Pick the one the story needs. |
| "We were singing." "Y'all were singing." | *Cantābāmus. Cantābātis.* |
| Can *cantābam* mean a plain "I sang": once, finished, done? | Usually not. For one finished act Latin has another past tense, the **perfect** (a later chapter). The imperfect is a film still running; the perfect is a snapshot. Until then, yesterday's stories are told as what *was going on*. |
| *Heri in hortō ambulābam.* | "Yesterday I was walking in the garden." (*Ambulō*, Chapter 5.) |
| "The kids used to watch the sheep." (Chapter 15's *ovēs*) | *Līberī ovēs spectābant.* |
| The motto: *Et quod temptābam scrībere, versus erat.* (*Temptō, temptāre*: try; *quod*: what; *versus*: verse.) Find the imperfect. | ***Temptābam***, "I was trying," and kept trying. Ovid's father told him poetry doesn't pay, so he tried to write prose, but whatever he tried came out as verse. (*Erat*, "was," is in section 6.) |

## 3. *Habēbam*

:: Docēbat eōs. || He was teaching them. (Matthew 5:2, just before the Beatitudes)

Habēbam, habēbās, habēbat. Sedēbam, vidēbam, manēbam.

{#g-03}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Habeō, habēre* (Chapter 5): which family? What's its stem? | The second conjugation, the *ē*-verbs. Stem *habē-*, from *habēre*. |
| Guess "I was having, I used to have." | ***Habēbam***: *habē + ba + m*. Same sign, same "I." |
| Now all six. | *Habēbam, habēbās, habēbat, habēbāmus, habēbātis, habēbant.* |
| "We were sitting on the shore." (Chapter 15's *lītus*; *sedeō*, Chapter 5) | *In lītore sedēbāmus.* |
| "Grandma was watching; Grandpa was staying home." | *Avia spectābat; Avus domī manēbat.* |
| "The sheep were afraid." "The dog was barking." | *Ovēs timēbant. Canis lātrābat.* |
| The motto, *Docēbat eōs.* (*Eōs*, them: Chapter 19.) Which verb, and why the imperfect? | *Doceō*, teach (Chapter 5): "he was teaching them." Jesus on the mountain: a long sermon, in progress. The Beatitudes follow. |
| *Amābat* and *habēbat*: how do you tell the families apart? | The vowel before the sign: *amā-ba-t*, *habē-ba-t*. Exactly as in *amāre* and *habēre*. The sign itself is the same. |
| *Dō, dare* is first conjugation with a short a. "I was giving, you were giving…" | *Dabam, dabās, dabat, dabāmus, dabātis, dabant.* Short *da-* every time. |

## 4. *Cantābō*

:: Dabit deus hīs quoque fīnem. || God will put an end to these too. (Vergil, *Aeneid* 1.199)

Amābō, amābis, amābit. Amābimus, amābitis, amābunt.

{#g-04}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Section 1 cracked *amābō*. Now the whole future: *amābō, amābis, amābit, amābimus, amābitis, amābunt.* What vowel follows the *b* each time? | *ō, i, i, i, i, u*. Chant it: **bō, bi, bi, bi, bi, bu**. |
| So which sign means "will"? | ***-bi-***. It hides twice: "I" swallows it into ***-bō*** (like *amō*), and before *-nt* it turns into ***-bu-***: *amābunt*. |
| "You will sing." "Y'all will sing." | *Cantābis. Cantābitis.* |
| *Amābās* or *amābis*? | *Amābās*, you were loving (*ā*, the past); *amābis*, you will love (*i*, the future). One vowel tells the time. |
| "Tomorrow we will work." (*Labōrō*, Chapter 5) | *Crās labōrābimus.* |
| "Will Marcus sing?" (Chapter 2's *-ne*) | *Cantābitne Marcus?* |
| In Chapter 12 you promised Grandpa: *Cāseum magnum tibi portō, ave!* The present did the job then. Say it properly now. | ***Cāseum magnum tibi portābō, ave!*** "I'll bring you a big cheese, Grandpa!" *Portā + bō.* |
| "I will give you cheese." | *Cāseum tibi dabō.* Short a, like the motto's *dabō tibi iūs*. |
| The motto, *Dabit deus hīs quoque fīnem.* (*Deus*, god; *hīs*, to these: Chapter 19; *fīnis, fīnis*, an end.) | ***Dabit***, "will give." Aeneas, shipwrecked, cheers his men: "God will put an end to these too." Chapter 11's *dō* in the future. |

## 5. *Habēbō*

:: Semper honōs nōmenque tuum laudēsque manēbunt. || Your honor, your name, and your praises will remain forever. (Vergil, *Aeneid* 1.609)

Habēbō, habēbis, habēbit. Habēbimus, habēbitis, habēbunt.

{#g-05}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "I will have," all six. | *Habēbō, habēbis, habēbit, habēbimus, habēbitis, habēbunt.* *Habē-* + bō, bi, bi, bi, bi, bu. |
| "We will stay home." (Chapter 8's *domī*) | *Domī manēbimus.* |
| The motto. Find the verb, and say why *tuum* fits *nōmen*. | ***Manēbunt***, "will remain" (*maneō*, Chapter 5). *Nōmen* is neuter (Chapter 14), so *tuum*. Aeneas thanks Dido. *Semper* (Chapter 5): always. |
| From the Beatitudes, Matthew 5:8: *Beātī mundō corde, quoniam ipsī Deum vidēbunt.* Find the future. | ***Vidēbunt***, "they will see": "Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God." (The rest is glossed: *beātī*, blessed; *mundō corde*, with a clean heart; *quoniam*, because; *ipsī*, they themselves.) |
| *Vidēs, vidēbās, vidēbis.* | You see · you were seeing · you will see. No sign = now; *-bā-* = was; *-bi-* = will. |
| A teacher, to a noisy class: "Y'all will be quiet!" (*Taceō*) | *Tacēbitis!* |
| "The dog will be afraid of the bird." | *Canis avem timēbit.* (*Timeō* takes an object: he fears the bird.) |
| Careful. Can you put *-bō* on Chapter 6's *legō*: "*legābō*"? | **No.** This *-bō* future belongs to the *-āre* and *-ēre* families only (and *eō*, coming in section 6). *Legō, audiō, capiō* build their futures another way: Chapter 17. |

!!! success "The Law of the Time Signs"
    Between the stem and the person ending, a small **tense sign** tells the time.

    - No sign: **present**, now. *Amā-s*, you love.
    - ***-bā-***: **imperfect**, was …ing, used to. *Amā-bā-s*, you were loving. "I" is ***-m***: *amābam*.
    - ***-bi-***: **future**, will. *Amā-bi-s*, you will love. "I" is ***-bō***, "they" is ***-bunt***: bō, bi, bi, bi, bi, bu.

    The person endings are the old ones: *-s, -t, -mus, -tis, -nt*. For now the signs go on **first and second conjugation** verbs (*amāre, habēre*).

## 6. *Eram et erō*

:: In prīncipiō erat Verbum. || In the beginning was the Word. (the Gospel of John 1:1)

Eram, erās, erat. Erō, eris, erit.

{#g-06}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Sum*, all six, present. | *Sum, es, est, sumus, estis, sunt.* |
| "Was": *eram, erās, erat, erāmus, erātis, erant.* Compare *amābam, amābās…* | The same endings, *-m, -s, -t, -mus, -tis, -nt*, with the past *a*: *er-a-m*. No *b*: *sum* is a rebel, but a tidy one. |
| "Will be": *erō, eris, erit, erimus, eritis, erunt.* Compare *amābō*. | The same tune: ***ō, i, i, i, i, u***. *Er-ō, er-i-s, … er-u-nt*. |
| The motto, *In prīncipiō erat Verbum.* (*Prīncipium*, beginning; *verbum*, word.) | "In the beginning was the Word." The first line of your Gospel, Iōannēs. *Erat*: it was, and went on being. |
| Horace, an aging poet, begs the goddess of love to leave him alone: *Nōn sum quālis eram.* (*Quālis*: the sort of man that.) | "I'm not the man I used to be." *Sum* now, *eram* then: two tenses in four words. |
| *Erat* or *erit*? *Eris* or *es*? *Erunt* or *sunt*? | *Erat* was, *erit* will be (*a* past, *i* future, like *-bā-, -bi-*). *Es* you are, *eris* you will be. *Sunt* they are, *erunt* they will be. |
| "Yesterday we were on the shore. Tomorrow we'll be at home." | *Heri in lītore erāmus. Crās domī erimus.* |
| Chapter 8's *eō*, "I go." Its stem is *ī-*. Guess "I was going" and "I will go." | ***Ībam*** and ***ībō***: *ī + bam*, *ī + bō*, just like *amā-*. Then *ībās, ībat, ībāmus, ībātis, ībant* and *ībis, ībit, ībimus, ībitis, ībunt*. |
| Horace opens a satire: *Ībam forte Viā Sacrā.* (*Forte*: by chance; *Via Sacra*: the Sacred Way, Rome's main street.) | "I happened to be walking down the Sacred Way." The imperfect sets the scene… and then a terrible bore grabs his hand. |
| "Tomorrow we'll go to Rome." (Chapter 8: no *ad* with city names) | *Crās Rōmam ībimus.* |

!!! success "Three times, three verbs"
    | | present (now) | imperfect (was …ing, used to) | future (will) |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | I | *amō · habeō · sum* | *amābam · habēbam · eram* | *amābō · habēbō · erō* |
    | you | *amās · habēs · es* | *amābās · habēbās · erās* | *amābis · habēbis · eris* |
    | he, she, it | *amat · habet · est* | *amābat · habēbat · erat* | *amābit · habēbit · erit* |
    | we | *amāmus · habēmus · sumus* | *amābāmus · habēbāmus · erāmus* | *amābimus · habēbimus · erimus* |
    | y'all | *amātis · habētis · estis* | *amābātis · habēbātis · erātis* | *amābitis · habēbitis · eritis* |
    | they | *amant · habent · sunt* | *amābant · habēbant · erant* | *amābunt · habēbunt · erunt* |

    *Eō* rides along: *ībam…* like *amābam*, *ībō…* like *amābō*.

## 7. *Heri, hodiē, crās*

:: Semper ego audītor tantum? || Am I always to be just a listener? (Juvenal, *Satires* 1.1)

Heri, hodiē, crās. Nunc, tum, mox. Semper, saepe, numquam. Māne, vesperī.

{#g-07}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Heri, hodiē, crās.* Which tense likes each? | *Heri*, yesterday: the past (for now, the imperfect). *Hodiē*, today: the present. *Crās*, tomorrow: the future. |
| *Nunc, tum, mox*: now (Chapter 4), then, soon. "Now I'm working; soon I'll sing." | *Nunc labōrō; mox cantābō.* *Tum* is "then, at that time" (or "next"): *Tum avis in lītore erat*, "Then there was a bird on the shore." |
| *Semper, saepe* (Chapter 5), and ***numquam***, never (*ne* + *umquam*, not ever). "Grandpa never used to swim." | *Avus numquam natābat.* |
| ***Iam***: already, by now (YAM). "It was already night." Then *nōn iam*: "I'm no longer afraid." | *Iam nox erat.* (Chapter 15's *nox*.) *Nōn iam timeō*: "no longer." |
| ***Māne***, in the morning; ***vesperī***, in the evening (WES-pe-ree; think vespers). "In the morning we'll work; in the evening we'll sing." | *Māne labōrābimus; vesperī cantābimus.* |
| Chapters 11 and 12: *Hodiē mihi, crās tibi.* There's no verb. Fill in the right one for each day. | One way to fill it in: *Hodiē mihi **est**, crās tibi **erit***, "Today it's my turn, tomorrow it will be yours." The tombstone (and Grandpa) left the verbs out; now you can hear them. |
| The motto, *Semper ego audītor tantum?* (*Audītor*, listener, from *audiō*; *tantum*, only.) | "Am I always to be just a listener?" Juvenal is fed up with bad poets reading aloud; his next words ask, "Will I never pay them back?" (with *numquam*). So he writes his own satires. |
| Martial teases a friend who always says he'll start living "*crās*… *semper crās*." The poet's answer: *hodiē* is *iam* too late; the wise man started living *heri*. Say the joke in English. | "Tomorrow, always tomorrow? Even today is already too late. The wise one got started yesterday." (*Epigrams* 5.58.) |
| *Crās cantābam.* What's wrong? | The clocks disagree: *crās* is tomorrow, *-bam* is was. Say ***Crās cantābō***. Or ***Heri cantābam***. |

!!! success "The Law of the Clock"
    **The verb carries the clock.** No sign is now, *-bā-* is was, *-bi-* is will (*eram* was, *erō* will be). A time word (*heri, hodiē, crās, mox, tum…*) sets the scene, and the verb's clock must match it. If there's no time word, the verb alone still tells you when.

## 8. Lectiō: *Heri et crās*

:: Forsan et haec ōlim meminisse iuvābit. || Maybe someday it will be a pleasure to remember even this. (Vergil, *Aeneid* 1.203)

Māne est. Familia domī est. Heri in lītore erant. Et crās?

Read each Latin line aloud. Guess the English, then tap to check. For the reverse drill, tap **Tege Latīna** and rebuild each line from the English. Only one new word: *ēheu*, "oh no, alas." Everything else is old words with new clocks.

::: dialogue lectio
Māter | Salvēte, līberī! Ecce pānis, lac, et mel. || Mother | Morning, kids! Here's bread, milk, and honey.
Avus | Heri in lītore erāmus… hodiē domī sumus. || Grandpa | Yesterday we were on the shore… today we're at home.
Marcus | Heri in marī natābam! Rēx piscium eram! || Marcus | Yesterday I was swimming in the sea! I was the king of the fish!
Quīntus | Et ego piscēs spectābam. Multī piscēs erant! || Quīntus | And I was watching the fish. There were lots of fish!
Cornēlia | Cāseus quoque natābat. Nāsus niger in marī erat! || Cornēlia | Cheese was swimming too. There was a black nose in the sea!
Avia | Ego in lītore sedēbam et ovēs in monte spectābam. || Grandma | I was sitting on the shore and watching the sheep on the hill.
Avus | Et ego puer saepe in marī natābam. || Grandpa | And when I was a boy, I used to swim in the sea a lot.
Marcus | Tū, Ave? Tū natābās? || Marcus | You, Grandpa? You used to swim?
Avus | Semper natābam! Et numquam timēbam. || Grandpa | I was always swimming! And I was never afraid.
Quīntus | Sed tum avis magna et alba in lītore erat… || Quīntus | But then there was a big white bird on the shore…
Cornēlia | Et avis cāseum nostrum portābat… in nūbēs! || Cornēlia | And the bird was carrying off our cheese… up into the clouds!
Cāseus | Bau! Bau! || Cheese | Woof! Woof!
Iōannēs | Ita, Cāseus lātrābat. Sed avis nōn timēbat. || John | Yes, Cheese was barking. But the bird wasn't scared.
Avia | Rāra avis erat: avis fūr! || Grandma | It was a rare bird: a thief of a bird!
Pater | Bene, bene. Sed quid crās? || Father | All right, all right. But what about tomorrow?
Marcus | Crās ad mare ībimus! || Marcus | Tomorrow we'll go to the sea!
Māter | Minimē. Crās domī manēbimus. Pater labōrābit. || Mother | Not a chance. Tomorrow we'll stay home. Dad will be working.
Pater | Ita. Māne labōrābō… et vesperī cantābō! || Father | Right. In the morning I'll work… and in the evening I'll sing!
Līberī | Ēheu! Pater cantābit! || The kids | Oh, no! Dad's going to sing!
Cornēlia | Ego crās Cāseum docēbō. Mox Cāseus pedem dabit! || Cornēlia | Tomorrow I'll train Cheese. Soon Cheese will give his paw!
Quīntus | Et cāseum novum habēbimus? || Quīntus | And will we have a new cheese?
Iōannēs | Ita! Crās Rōmam ībō et cāseum magnum portābō. || John | Yes! Tomorrow I'll go to Rome and bring back a big cheese.
Avus | Iōannēs, amābō tē… cāseum mihi dabis? || Grandpa | John, pretty please… will you give the cheese to me?
Iōannēs | Dabō tibi, Ave! Sed pars cāseī līberīs erit. || John | I'll give it to you, Grandpa! But the kids will get a share.
Avus | Hodiē mihi, crās tibi… || Grandpa | Today it's mine, tomorrow yours…
Avia | Et ubi erit cāseus? In capsā? || Grandma | And where will the cheese be? In the box?
Quīntus | Spectāte! In hortō est avis! Avis magna et alba! || Quīntus | Look! There's a bird in the garden! A big white bird!
Cāseus | Bau! Bau! Bau! || Cheese | Woof! Woof! Woof!
Avus | Cavē avem, Iōannēs! || Grandpa | Watch out for the bird, John!
Iōannēs | Nōn timeō. Crās cāseus noster erit ubi semper erit: in stomachō… || John | I'm not afraid. Tomorrow our cheese will be where it always will be: in the stomach of…
Omnēs | …Avī! || Everyone | …Grandpa!
:::

{#g-08}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Heri in marī natābam! Rēx piscium eram!* Tense and person of each verb? | Both imperfect, "I": *natā-ba-m*, I was swimming; *eram*, I was. The *-m* is "I." |
| *Multī piscēs erant.* Why *erant*, not *erat*? | *Piscēs* is plural: "there **were** many fish." The verb matches its subject in number, in every tense. |
| *Et ego puer saepe in marī natābam.* Which English fits *natābam* here, and what does *puer* do? | "**Used to** swim": *saepe* plus the imperfect makes a habit. *Puer* says what he was then: "(as) a boy." |
| *Avis cāseum portābat… in nūbēs!* Why *nūbēs*, not *nūbibus*? | Chapter 8's Law of the Switch: *in* + object form is "into," motion. *In nūbibus* (Chapter 15) is "in the clouds," already there. |
| *Crās domī manēbimus. Pater labōrābit.* Persons? | *Manēbimus*, we will stay; *labōrābit*, he will work. The *-bi-* sign both times. |
| *Iōannēs, amābō tē… cāseum mihi dabis?* How many futures does Grandpa use? | Two: *amābō* (I will love = pretty please) and *dabis* (will you give?). Short *da-* again. |
| *Sed pars cāseī līberīs erit.* Word for word? | "But a part of the cheese will be to the kids": Chapter 11's *Liber mihi est* ("I have a book"), now in the future. The kids will get a share. |
| *In stomachō… Avī!* Whose stomach? | *Avī* is the "of" form of *avus*: **Grandpa's**. The bird's would be *avis*. As always, the cheese ends up in someone's stomach. |

## 9. Sound check

:: Nunc aut numquam. || Now or never. (proverb)

Audī et dīc.

{#g-09}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *amābam · amābāmus · amābātis*? | a-MAH-bam · a-mah-BAH-mus · a-mah-BAH-tis. A long second-to-last syllable takes the stress (emphasis). |
| *amābimus · amābitis*? | a-MAH-bi-mus · a-MAH-bi-tis. The *bi* is short, so the stress steps back to *mā*. |
| *habēbō · habēbunt · habēbāmus*? | ha-BAY-boh · ha-BAY-bunt · ha-bay-BAH-mus. |
| *eram · erāmus · erimus · erunt*? | E-ram · e-RAH-mus · E-ri-mus · E-runt. Short e every time. |
| *heri · hodiē · numquam · iam · vesperī*? | HE-ri · HO-di-ay · NUM-kwam · YAM · WES-pe-ree. Classical *v* is "w"; church Latin says VES-pe-ree. The *i* of *iam* is a "y." |
| *ībam · ībō · amābō tē*? | EE-bam · EE-boh · a-MAH-boh tay. |

## Speak it aloud

:: Dīcendō discimus. || We learn by speaking.

1. Chant *amō* three ways, all six persons: present, imperfect, future. Then *habeō*, then *sum*.
2. Future, fast: **bō, bi, bi, bi, bi, bu** with *cantā-, labōrā-, vidē-, manē-*.
3. Flip the clock: *cantō → cantābam → cantābō · habet → habēbat → habēbit · sumus → erāmus → erimus · īs → ībās → ībis*.
4. Yesterday, out loud: *Heri in hortō ambulābam. Heri cāseum habēbāmus. Heri avis in lītore erat.*
5. Tomorrow, out loud: *Crās labōrābō. Crās domī manēbimus. Crās Rōmam ībō. Cāseum tibi dabō.*
6. Old chunks, cracked open: *Amābō tē! · Dabō tibi iūs · Hodiē mihi est, crās tibi erit · In prīncipiō erat Verbum.*
7. Read the *Lectiō* aloud, playing every part (Cheese too). Then hide the Latin and rebuild it.

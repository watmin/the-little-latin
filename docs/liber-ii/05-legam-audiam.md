# Caput XVII: Legam, Audiam

*Liber II, Rēs Cotīdiānae · Chapter 17*

*In which the other two verb families get their yesterdays and tomorrows. Chapter 16 fenced off *legābō* with a "No." Today you learn why: *legō, audiō,* and *capiō* keep the old past sign, but they build the future a whole new way, with an *-am* and a string of *ē*'s. Then you learn to tell *leget* from *legit* and *legēs* from *habēs*, two old mottos crack open, and the family plans tomorrow's trip to Rome for the cheese Iōannēs promised.*

!!! abstract "In this chapter"
    - **Saying:** *Librum legam.* · *Veniam!* · *Quid crās faciēmus?* · *Quandō veniēs?* · *Quaerite et inveniētis.*
    - **Grammar words, in plain English:** no new terms; tense, tense sign, imperfect, and future (Chapter 16) now cover all four verb families
    - **Forms:** *legēbam, audiēbam, capiēbam*; *legam, legēs, leget, legēmus, legētis, legent*; *audiam, audiēs…*; *capiam, capiēs…*
    - **Laws:** the Law of the Two Futures
    - **Words:** *quaerō, inveniō, vīvō, stertō, quandō?, perendiē, gladiātor, auxilium*
    - **Reading:** *Crās Rōmam!* (planning the trip to Rome)
    - **Speaking:** make plans: who will come, what we'll do, when we'll go

!!! tip "How to read this chapter"
    Same as always. Say your answer **out loud**, then tap the wax tablet to check. Keep two chants handy: Chapter 6's *legō, legis, legit, legimus, legitis, legunt*, and Chapter 16's **bō, bi, bi, bi, bi, bu**. Today you'll need to tell them apart from a third.

## 1. *Legēbam*

:: Quō puella dūcēbat. || Wherever the girl was leading. (Catullus 8.4)

Hodiē legō. Heri legēbam. Crās legam.

{#g-01}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 16 asked: can you put *-bō* on *legō* and say "*legābō*"? | **No.** The *-bō* future belongs to the *-āre* and *-ēre* families (and *eō*). Chapter 16 promised the rest today: *legō, audiō, capiō*. |
| Read the blurb: *Hodiē legō. Heri legēbam. Crās legam.* | "Today I read. Yesterday I was reading. Tomorrow I will read." |
| Look at *legēbam*. Which piece do you already know? | ***-bam***: Chapter 16's imperfect sign *-bā-* with the "I" ending *-m*, just like *amābam*. |
| What sits between *leg-* and *-bam*? | A long ***ē***: *leg-ē-ba-m*. *Amā-* and *habē-* bring their own long vowel to the sign. *Legō* has none of its own (*legere*'s e is short), so it borrows a long *ē*. |
| Now all six. | *Legēbam, legēbās, legēbat, legēbāmus, legēbātis, legēbant.* The *a* is short before final *-m, -t, -nt*, as in Chapter 16. |
| *Legēbam* and *habēbam* rhyme. Is that a problem? | Not really. Both are "was …ing." The dictionary form tells you the family (*legō, legere* · *habeō, habēre*); the meaning of the tense is the same. |
| "We were reading." "Y'all used to write." (*Scrībō*, Chapter 6) | *Legēbāmus. Scrībēbātis.* |
| "Marcus was running in the garden." (*Currō*) | *Marcus in hortō currēbat.* |
| "The kids were eating; Grandpa was drinking." (*Edō, bibō*) | *Līberī edēbant; Avus bibēbat.* |
| "Grandpa always used to say…" (*Dīcō*) | *Avus semper dīcēbat…* dee-KAY-bat. (Church Latin says dee-CHAY-bat.) |
| The motto, *Quō puella dūcēbat.* (*Quō*: where to, Chapter 8; *dūcō*: lead.) | ***Dūcēbat***, "was leading." Catullus, heartbroken, remembers the sunny days when he kept following "wherever the girl was leading." The imperfect: again and again. |

## 2. *Audiēbam, capiēbam*

:: Libenter eum audiēbat. || He liked to listen to him. (Mark 6:20)

Audiēbam, veniēbam, dormiēbam. Capiēbam, faciēbam.

{#g-02}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Audiō, audīre* (Chapter 6): which family? | The fourth conjugation, the ī family. |
| Guess "I was hearing." Hint: the everyday form isn't "*audībam*." | ***Audiēbam***: *audi-* + *ē* + *bam*. The same borrowed *ē* as *legēbam*, with the family's i kept in front. (Early Latin and the poets sometimes say *audībam*; you'll meet it rarely.) |
| All six. | *Audiēbam, audiēbās, audiēbat, audiēbāmus, audiēbātis, audiēbant.* |
| *Capiō, capere*, the in-between verb. Does its imperfect follow *legō* or *audiō*? | *Audiō*: it keeps its i. *Capiēbam, capiēbās, capiēbat…* And *faciō*: *faciēbam*. |
| Line up "I was …ing" across all four families and the in-between: *amō, habeō, legō, audiō, capiō*. | *Amā-bam, habē-bam, legē-bam, audiē-bam, capiē-bam.* Different vowels in front; ***-bam*** every time. |
| In Chapter 16 the story never let Grandpa sleep. Now: "Grandpa was sleeping." | ***Avus dormiēbat.*** dor-mi-AY-bat. (*Dormiō, dormīre*, Chapter 6.) |
| "We were coming home." (Chapter 8's *domum*) | *Domum veniēbāmus.* |
| "What were y'all doing?" | *Quid faciēbātis?* |
| "The dog used to grab the cheese." | *Canis cāseum capiēbat.* |
| The motto, *Libenter eum audiēbat.* (*Libenter*: gladly; *eum*: him, Chapter 19.) | ***Audiēbat***, "he used to listen": "he liked to listen to him." Again and again, so the imperfect. |
| Who is "he," and who is "him"? Mark 6:20: *Hērōdēs enim metuēbat Iōannem… et custōdiēbat eum… et libenter eum audiēbat.* (*Enim*: for; *metuō, metuere*: fear; *custōdiō, custōdīre*: guard, Chapter 13's *custōs*.) | King Herod, and your namesake, John the Baptist: "For Herod feared John… and kept him safe… and liked to listen to him." Three imperfects: *metuēbat* (little-i family), *custōdiēbat* and *audiēbat* (ī family). |

## 3. *Legam*

:: In hōc signō vincēs. || In this sign you will conquer. (Constantine's vision, as Latin tradition tells it)

Legam, legēs, leget. Legēmus, legētis, legent.

{#g-03}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Now the future. Is there a *b* anywhere in *legam*? | No *b* at all. ***Legam***: "I will read." The bō-bi-bu future isn't for this family. |
| Here's the whole chant: *legam, legēs, leget, legēmus, legētis, legent.* What vowel follows *leg-* each time? | ***a, ē, e, ē, ē, e***. "I" says ***-am***; everyone else has ***ē*** (short before final *-t* and *-nt*, the Chapter 5 habit). |
| So what's the future sign in this family? | ***-ē-***, with ***-am*** for "I." Chant it: **am, ēs, et, ēmus, ētis, ent**. |
| Chapter 16's fence, fixed: "I will read the book." | *Librum legam.* Not *legābō*. |
| "You will write." "Y'all will write." | *Scrībēs. Scrībētis.* |
| "They will run." | *Current.* Short e before *-nt*, like *legent*. |
| The trap: *legit* or *leget*? | *Legit*, he reads (now, short **i**). *Leget*, he will read (short **e**). One little vowel tells the time. |
| Pair them up: *legis / legēs · legimus / legēmus · legunt / legent.* | You read / you will read · we read / we will read · they read / they will read. Present has *i* (and *u* in "they"); future has *ē*. |
| *Legam* or *legēbam*? | *Legam*, I will read. *Legēbam*, I was reading. The ***b*** makes the past. No *b*, and the *-am* is "I will." |
| Chapter 13's motto: *Inter arma silent lēgēs*. Is *lēgēs* "you will read"? | No. ***Lēgēs*** (long first e) is "laws," from *lēx, lēgis*. ***Legēs*** (short first e) is "you will read." So *Lēgēs legēs*: "You will read the laws." Macrons matter. |
| "I will say." "What will you say?" (*Dīcō*) | *Dīcam. Quid dīcēs?* |
| The motto, *In hōc signō vincēs.* (*Hōc*: this, Chapter 19; *signum*: sign; *vincō, vincere*: conquer, win.) | ***Vincēs***, "you will conquer": WIN-kays (church Latin: VIN-chays). The story goes that before a battle in 312, the emperor Constantine saw a sign in the sky with these words. |
| Chapter 16 told Martial's joke in English. Now the Latin line: *Crās vīvēs? Hodiē iam vīvere, Postume, sērum est.* (*Vīvō, vīvere*: live; *sērum*: late.) | "You'll live tomorrow? Even today is too late to live, Postumus." ***Vīvēs***: you will live, the *ē* future of *vīvō*. |

## 4. *Audiam, capiam*

:: Quaerite et inveniētis. || Seek, y'all, and you will find. (Matthew 7:7)

Audiam, audiēs, audiet. Capiam, capiēs, capiet. Veniam!

{#g-04}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Guess the future of *audiō*, all six, from *legam*. | *Audiam, audiēs, audiet, audiēmus, audiētis, audient.* The i stays in front: *audi-am, audi-ēs*. |
| *Capiō* again sides with *audiō*. All six. | *Capiam, capiēs, capiet, capiēmus, capiētis, capient.* And *faciō*: *faciam, faciēs, faciet…* |
| *Audit* or *audiet*? *Audīs* or *audiēs*? *Audiunt* or *audient*? | *Audit* he hears, *audiet* he will hear. *Audīs* you hear, *audiēs* you will hear. *Audiunt* they hear, *audient* they will hear. |
| Chapter 2's motto was *Quaerite et inveniētis*. (*Quaerō, quaerere*: seek, look for; *inveniō, invenīre*: find, from *in* + *veniō*, "come upon.") Find the future. | ***Inveniētis***, "y'all will find": *inveni-ē-tis*. Chapter 2 gave you the English; now you can see the *-ē-*. *Quaerite* is a plural command (Chapter 6). |
| Someone calls *Venī hūc!* In Chapter 6 you answered *Veniō!* Now promise instead: "I'll come!" | ***Veniam!*** WE-ni-am. And "He will come": ***Veniet***. |
| From Psalm 121 (120 in the Latin Bible): *Unde veniet auxilium mihi?* (*Auxilium*: help.) | "Where will my help come from?" *Unde*, from where (Chapter 7); ***veniet***, will come. |
| "I'll sleep." "Will Grandpa sleep?" | *Dormiam. Dormietne Avus?* |
| "What will we do tomorrow?" | ***Quid crās faciēmus?*** fa-ki-AY-mus (church Latin: fa-chee-AY-mus). |
| "I'll grab the cheese. The bird won't grab the cheese." | *Cāseum capiam. Avis cāseum nōn capiet.* |
| Ovid's long poem, the *Metamorphōsēs*, ends with one word: ***vīvam***. | "I will live": the *-am* future of *vīvō*. He promises his poem will be read for all the ages, so he will live on. |

## 5. *Habēs an legēs?*

:: Vēritās līberābit vōs. || The truth will set y'all free. (the Gospel of John 8:32)

Habēs, legēs. Habet, leget. Habent, legent.

{#g-05}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| The title asks *Habēs an legēs?* (*An*: or, in a question.) *Habēs* (Chapter 5) and *legēs*: tense of each? | *Habēs*: you **have**, now. *Legēs*: you **will read**. The same ending, *-ēs*, and two different times! |
| Line up *habet, habēmus, habētis, habent* with *leget, legēmus, legētis, legent*. | Identical endings: *-et, -ēmus, -ētis, -ent*. The second conjugation's **present** looks just like the third conjugation's **future**. Only "I" differs: *habeō* vs *legam*. |
| So how do you know which is which? | The family, from the dictionary form. *Habeō, habēre*: the ē is its own family vowel, so *habēs* is now. *Legō, legere*: no ē of its own, so an *ē* means "will." Learn every verb with its infinitive. |
| *Vidēs* or *dīcēs*: which one is the future? | *Dīcēs*, you will say (*dīcō, dīcere*). *Vidēs* is "you see," now (*videō, vidēre*). |
| *Manet* or *curret*? | *Manet*, he stays, now (*maneō, manēre*). *Curret*, he will run (*currō, currere*). |
| The motto, *Vēritās līberābit vōs.* (*Vēritās*: truth; *līberō, līberāre*: set free; *vōs*: y'all, as the object.) Which kind of future? | ***Līberābit***: *-āre*, so the bō-bi-bu kind: "will set free." |
| The whole verse: *Et cognōscētis vēritātem, et vēritās līberābit vōs.* (*Cognōscō, cognōscere*: get to know.) | ***Cognōscētis***, "y'all will know": *-ere*, so the *ē* kind. "And you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free." Two futures, built two ways, in one verse. |
| Chapter 16's Lectiō motto: *Forsan et haec ōlim meminisse iuvābit.* Find the future. Which kind? | ***Iuvābit***, "it will please" (*iuvō, iuvāre*): bō-bi-bu. "Maybe someday it will be a pleasure to remember even this." |
| Sort: *amābit, leget, audiet, habēbit, capiet, ībit, erit.* | **bō kind:** *amābit, habēbit, ībit*. **ē kind:** *leget, audiet, capiet*. *Erit* is *sum*'s own (Chapter 16). |

!!! success "The Law of the Two Futures"
    **Which future? Ask the infinitive.**

    - ***-āre*** or ***-ēre*** with a long *ē* (*amāre, habēre*): **bō, bi, bi, bi, bi, bu**. *Amābō, amābis, amābit…*
    - ***-ere*** with a short *e*, or ***-īre***, and the *-iō* verbs (*legere, audīre, capere*): **am, ēs, et, ēmus, ētis, ent**. *Legam, legēs, leget…*
    - Two old rebels keep their own: *eō* (even though its infinitive is *īre*) says *ībō* (Chapter 16), and *sum* says *erō*.

    Rhyme: ***ā* and *ē* say bō, bi, bu; the rest say *am*, then *ē* right through.** The imperfect is easier: *-bā-* for everyone (*legēbam, audiēbam, capiēbam*).

!!! success "Three times, three verbs"
    | | present (now) | imperfect (was …ing, used to) | future (will) |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | I | *legō · audiō · capiō* | *legēbam · audiēbam · capiēbam* | *legam · audiam · capiam* |
    | you | *legis · audīs · capis* | *legēbās · audiēbās · capiēbās* | *legēs · audiēs · capiēs* |
    | he, she, it | *legit · audit · capit* | *legēbat · audiēbat · capiēbat* | *leget · audiet · capiet* |
    | we | *legimus · audīmus · capimus* | *legēbāmus · audiēbāmus · capiēbāmus* | *legēmus · audiēmus · capiēmus* |
    | y'all | *legitis · audītis · capitis* | *legēbātis · audiēbātis · capiēbātis* | *legētis · audiētis · capiētis* |
    | they | *legunt · audiunt · capiunt* | *legēbant · audiēbant · capiēbant* | *legent · audient · capient* |

## 6. *Quid crās faciēmus?*

:: Quid sit futūrum crās, fuge quaerere. || Don't ask what tomorrow will bring. (Horace, *Odes* 1.9.13)

Crās Rōmam ībimus. Quid ibi faciēmus? Quandō veniēmus?

{#g-06}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| A new question word for plans: ***quandō?***, "when?" (KWAN-doh). "When will you come?" | *Quandō veniēs?* |
| Answer it: "I'll come tomorrow morning." | *Crās māne veniam.* (Chapter 16's *māne*.) |
| ***Perendiē***: the day after tomorrow (pe-REN-di-ay). "We'll go to Rome the day after tomorrow." | *Perendiē Rōmam ībimus.* *Eō* keeps its bō future: *ībimus*. |
| "What will y'all buy?" "We'll buy cheese." (*Emō*, Chapter 6) | *Quid emētis? Cāseum emēmus.* |
| "Will Marcus eat the cheese?" (*Edō, edere*) | *Edetne Marcus cāseum?* |
| "Tomorrow I'll read, Dad will work, and Grandpa will sleep." | *Crās legam, Pater labōrābit, Avus dormiet.* Both kinds of future in one breath. |
| "Yesterday I was reading; tomorrow I'll write." | *Heri legēbam; crās scrībam.* |
| *Crās legēbam.* What's wrong? | Chapter 16's Law of the Clock: *crās* is tomorrow, *-bā-* is was. ***Crās legam.*** |
| *Heri audiam.* What's wrong? | *Audiam* is "I will hear." Yesterday wants ***Heri audiēbam***. |
| The motto, *Quid sit futūrum crās, fuge quaerere.* (*Fuge*, "flee, avoid": a command from *fugiō*, an *-iō* verb, built like *cape*. *Quaerere*: to ask.) | "Don't ask what tomorrow will bring." Horace says: enjoy today. (*Sit* and *futūrum* come much later.) We'll make plans anyway. |

## 7. Lectiō: *Crās Rōmam!*

:: Tantae mōlis erat Rōmānam condere gentem. || So huge a task it was to found the Roman people. (Vergil, *Aeneid* 1.33)

Vesperī est. Hodiē familia domī manēbat. Crās Rōmam ībunt.

Read each Latin line aloud. Guess the English, then tap to check. For the reverse drill, tap **Tege Latīna** and rebuild each line from the English. New words: *stertō, stertere*, "snore," and *gladiātor*, a swordsman (Chapter 10's *gladius*).

Māter | Vesperī est, līberī. Quid hodiē faciēbātis? || Mother | It's evening, kids. What were y'all doing today?

Marcus | Quīntus et ego in hortō currēbāmus. || Marcus | Quīntus and I were running around in the garden.
Quīntus | Et Avus in hortō dormiēbat! || Quīntus | And Grandpa was sleeping in the garden!
Avus | Nōn dormiēbam! Avēs audiēbam. || Grandpa | I wasn't sleeping! I was listening to the birds.
Avia | Et avēs tē audiēbant: stertēbās! || Grandma | And the birds were listening to you: you were snoring!
Pater | Ego māne labōrābam et vesperī cantābam. || Father | I was working in the morning and singing in the evening.
Līberī | Audiēbāmus, Pater… audiēbāmus. || The kids | We were listening, Dad… oh, we were listening.
Iōannēs | Sed crās? Crās Rōmam ībimus! || John | But tomorrow? Tomorrow we're going to Rome!
Marcus | Quid crās Rōmae faciēmus? || Marcus | What will we do in Rome tomorrow?
Iōannēs | Māne in forum veniēmus. Tum tabernam magnam quaerēmus. || John | In the morning we'll come into the forum. Then we'll look for a big shop.
Quīntus | Et cāseum emēmus? || Quīntus | And we'll buy cheese?
Iōannēs | Ita! Heri dīcēbam: "Cāseum magnum portābō." Et portābō! || John | Yes! Yesterday I kept saying, "I'll bring a big cheese." And I will!
Cornēlia | Ego Viam Sacram vidēbō et librōs emam. || Cornēlia | I'll see the Sacred Way and buy books.
Marcus | Et gladiātōrēs spectābimus? || Marcus | And will we watch gladiators?
Māter | Minimē, Marce. Gladiātōrēs nōn spectābimus. || Mother | No, Marcus. We will not be watching gladiators.
Quīntus | Et Cāseus? Veniet? || Quīntus | And Cheese? Will he come?
Avia | Cāseus mēcum domī manēbit. Ego legam; Cāseus dormiet. || Grandma | Cheese will stay home with me. I'll read; Cheese will sleep.
Cāseus | Bau! || Cheese | Woof!
Avus | Et ego? Ego quoque domī dormiam… || Grandpa | And me? I'll sleep at home too…
Iōannēs | Sed, Ave, cāseum tibi emam! Nōnne veniēs? || John | But, Grandpa, I'll buy the cheese for you! Won't you come?
Avus | Cāseum? Veniam! Veniam! Hodiē dormiēbam; crās vīvam! || Grandpa | Cheese? I'll come! I'll come! Today I was sleeping; tomorrow I'll live!
Pater | Bene. Et in viā cantābō! || Father | Good. And on the road I'll sing!
Līberī | Ēheu! || The kids | Oh, no!
Māter | Minimē, Cornēlī. In viā librum legēs. || Mother | No, Cornēlius. On the road you'll read a book.
Pater | Legam… et tacēbō. || Father | I'll read… and keep quiet.
Iōannēs | Quandō domum veniēmus? || John | When will we come home?
Māter | Vesperī. Et Avus in viā dormiet. || Mother | In the evening. And Grandpa will sleep on the way.
Avia | Et avis magna et alba? Cāseum iterum capiet? || Grandma | And the big white bird? Will it grab the cheese again?
Iōannēs | Minimē! Cāseum in capsā portābō. Avis cāseum nōn capiet! || John | No way! I'll carry the cheese in a box. The bird won't grab the cheese!
Avus | Avis nōn capiet… sed ego capiam! || Grandpa | The bird won't grab it… but I will!
Quīntus | Et perendiē? Ubi erit cāseus perendiē? || Quīntus | And the day after tomorrow? Where will the cheese be then?
Cornēlia | Perendiē cāseus erit in stomachō… || Cornēlia | The day after tomorrow the cheese will be in the stomach of…

{#g-07}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Quid hodiē faciēbātis?* Tense and person? And isn't *hodiē* a present-tense word? | Imperfect, "y'all": *faci-ē-bā-tis*, "what were y'all doing?" It's evening, so most of *hodiē* is already behind them; Mother asks about the day as a scene that was running. *Hodiē* goes with whatever part of today you mean (Grandpa's *Hodiē dormiēbam* too). |
| *Avēs tē audiēbant: stertēbās!* Who was listening to whom? | *Avēs* (plural, Chapter 15) is the subject of *audiēbant*: the birds were listening to **you** (*tē*), Grandpa. *Stertēbās*: *stertō, stertere* (little-i family) + *-ēbā-* + *s*. |
| *Audiēbāmus, Pater… audiēbāmus.* Why do the kids sound so tired? | They had to listen to Dad's singing all evening: "we were listening…" (still going on, so the imperfect, not a one-time "we heard"). Chapter 16's *Ēheu! Pater cantābit!* came true. |
| *Māne in forum veniēmus. Tum tabernam magnam quaerēmus.* Which kind of future, and why *in forum*? | The *ē* kind both times: *veni-ē-mus* (ī family), *quaer-ē-mus* (little-i family). *In forum* is "into the forum": motion, Chapter 8's Law of the Switch. |
| *Heri dīcēbam: "Cāseum magnum portābō." Et portābō!* How many tenses? | Two: *dīcēbam*, I kept saying (imperfect), and *portābō*, I'll bring (bō future, since *portāre*). Chapter 16's promise, kept. |
| *Avia: Ego legam; Cāseus dormiet.* Could *legam* be "I was reading"? | No: no *b*. *Legam* is "I will read." "I was reading" would be *legēbam*. |
| *Māter: In viā librum legēs.* Is that "you read" or "you will read"? | "You **will** read": *legō, legere* has no ē of its own, so *-ēs* is the future. (Compare *habēs*, you have.) A future said firmly works as an order, like Chapter 16's *Tacēbitis!* Dad gets the message: *Legam… et tacēbō.* |
| *Avis nōn capiet… sed ego capiam!* And where does the cheese end up? | "The bird won't grab it… but I will!" *Capiet, capiam*: the *-iō* future. And *perendiē*, as always: *in stomachō Avī*, Grandpa's stomach. |

## 8. Sound check

:: Audiam et dīcam. || I will listen, and I will speak.

Audī et dīc.

{#g-08}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *legēbam · legēbāmus · legēbant*? | le-GAY-bam · le-gay-BAH-mus · le-GAY-bant. A long second-to-last syllable takes the stress (emphasis). |
| *legam · legēs · leget · legēmus · legent*? | LE-gam · LE-gays · LE-get · le-GAY-mus · LE-gent. Short e in *leg-* every time. |
| *audiēbam · audiam · audiēmus*? | ow-di-AY-bam · OW-di-am · ow-di-AY-mus. *Au* sounds like "ow." |
| *capiēbam · capiam · faciēmus*? | ka-pi-AY-bam · KA-pi-am · fa-ki-AY-mus. Classical *c* is always "k"; church Latin says fa-chee-AY-mus. |

## Speak it aloud

:: Dīcendō discimus. || We learn by speaking.

1. Chant *legō* three ways, all six persons: present, imperfect, future. Then *audiō*, then *capiō*.
2. Two futures, fast: **bō, bi, bi, bi, bi, bu** with *amā-, habē-*; then **am, ēs, et, ēmus, ētis, ent** with *leg-, audi-, capi-*.
3. Now or later? Say each pair and give the time: *legit / leget · legis / legēs · legunt / legent · audit / audiet · habēs / legēs*.
4. Promises, out loud: *Veniam! · Librum legam. · Cāseum tibi emam. · Crās māne veniam. · Quid crās faciēmus?*
5. Yesterday, out loud: *Heri librum legēbam. Avus dormiēbat. Quid faciēbātis?*
6. Old mottos, cracked open: *Quaerite et inveniētis · In hōc signō vincēs · Vēritās līberābit vōs · Crās vīvēs?*
7. Read the *Lectiō* aloud, playing every part (Grandpa snoring too). Then hide the Latin and rebuild it.

# Caput LII: Tam Fessus Ut…

*Liber V, Modus Subiūnctīvus · Chapter 52*

*In which the subjunctive learns to look back, and to say "so … that." Three old promises come due today. Chapter 50 taught Nero's* Quam vellem nescīre litterās! *as a chunk and said* vellem *was "another tense of the subjunctive, which Chapter 52 builds." Chapter 51 said "a goal in a past story ('I gave so that you* would *give') needs a new form: Chapter 52," and twice it warned that* ut nōn *and* ut nēmō *"have a different job: Chapter 52." Here are all three. Today: a second set of subjunctive forms, built in one move from the infinitive; wishes that can't come true; goals in the past; and the "so … that" clause, which tells what happened* because *something was so big, so tired, so loud. Along the way: two bad emperors, a shepherd in Vergil, a Roman lawyer's cups, Caesar's envoys and Caesar's storm, a storm on the Sea of Galilee, John 3:16, and old Cato on a life well lived. Speaking goal: say how tired, hungry, or happy you were, and what came of it.*

!!! abstract "In this chapter"
    - **Saying:** *Utinam adessēs! · Quid facerem? · Dedī ut darēs · Avus tam fessus erat ut dormīret · … ut ambulāre nōn posset · … ut nēmō dormīre posset*
    - **Grammar words, in plain English:** imperfect subjunctive (the "infinitive + ending" subjunctive: the subjunctive for past stories and for "not really") · result clause (the "so … that" clause)
    - **Forms:** the imperfect subjunctive, active and passive, of every verb: *amārem, habērem, dūcerem, caperem, audīrem, essem, possem, vellem, īrem, ferrem, fierem; amārer, loquerer*
    - **Laws:** the Law of the Infinitive Plus · the Law of the Past Goal · the Law of So … That · the Law of the Two Nots
    - **Words:** *tam, ita* (new job), *tantus, tot* (as a word), *tempestās, flūctus*
    - **Reading:** *Tam fessus ut…* (day twenty: a storm in the night, a sleepy family, a missing cheese, and a crumb in Grandpa's beard)
    - **Speaking:** wish something were different now; say what you went somewhere to do; say how tired, hungry, or happy you were, and what came of it

!!! tip "How to read this chapter"
    Same as always. Say your answer **out loud**, then tap the wax tablet to check. Keep three chants close: Chapter 6's infinitive (*amāre, habēre, dūcere, capere, audīre*), the endings *-m, -s, -t, -mus, -tis, -nt*, and Chapter 30's passive *-r, -ris, -tur, -mur, -minī, -ntur*. Today you need nothing else to build the new forms.

## 1. *Vellem*, cracked

:: Quam vellem nescīre litterās! || How I wish I didn't know how to write! (Nero, in Suetonius, *Nero* 10.2; Chapter 50)

Velle + m.

{#g-01}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 50's Nero: *Quam vellem nescīre litterās!* *Vellem* was a chunk, "another tense of the subjunctive, which Chapter 52 builds." First: Chapter 18's infinitive of *volō*? | ***Velle***, "to want." |
| Stick the "I" ending ***-m*** on it. | ***Vellem.*** That's the whole trick: the **infinitive + an ending**. |
| Same move on *sum*. Its infinitive, plus *-m*? | ***Esse* → *essem***: "I were," "I would be." |
| And on *amō*? | ***Amāre* → *amārem***. |
| Grammar books call *vellem, essem, amārem* the **imperfect subjunctive**. Plain words? | **The "infinitive + ending" subjunctive.** Its jobs: subjunctive work in a **past** story, and wishes that are **not really** so. ("Imperfect" is just the old name, borrowed from Chapter 16's *amābam*; it doesn't mean "was …ing.") |

## 2. All six persons

:: Utinam adessēs! || If only you were here! (teaching line)

Amārem, amārēs, amāret…

{#g-02}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Put all six endings, *-m, -s, -t, -mus, -tis, -nt*, on *amāre*. | ***Amārem, amārēs, amāret, amārēmus, amārētis, amārent.*** |
| Which *e*'s are long? | Long ***ē*** in *amārēs, amārēmus, amārētis*; short before a final *-m, -t, -nt* (*amārem, amāret, amārent*): the Chapter 16 habit, exactly like *amābam, amābās, amābat*. |
| *Habeō*: infinitive, then the y'all form. | *Habēre* → ***habērētis***, "(that) y'all might have." |
| *Dō* has a short *a* in its infinitive, *dare* (Chapter 11). So: "I," "you," "he"? | ***Darem, darēs, daret.*** Short *a* all the way. |
| *Sum*, all six. | ***Essem, essēs, esset, essēmus, essētis, essent.*** |

## 3. Every family, no exceptions

:: Ab īnfīnītīvō incipe! || Start from the infinitive! (teaching line)

Dūcere + m.

{#g-03}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Dūcō* (infinitive *dūcere*) and *capiō* (*capere*): the "I" form? | ***Dūcerem*** and ***caperem***. The *e* before *-rem* is short, because it's short in *dūcere, capere*. |
| *Audiō*: "we." | *Audīre* → ***audīrēmus***. |
| Chapter 49's *-im* rebels, *sim, possim, velim, nōlim, mālim*. Do they rebel today? | **No.** *Esse, posse, velle, nōlle, mālle* + *-m*: ***essem, possem, vellem, nōllem, māllem***. Perfectly regular. |
| Chapter 32's *eō* and *ferō*. | *Īre* → ***īrem***; *ferre* → ***ferrem***. |
| *Fīō* is the one bump: its infinitive is *fierī*, which looks passive. Pretend it's *fiere*. | ***Fierem, fierēs, fieret…*** Short *i* before *e*, as in *fierī*. |

!!! success "The Law of the Infinitive Plus"
    The **imperfect subjunctive** (the "infinitive + ending" subjunctive) is the present infinitive **plus** the endings *-m, -s, -t, -mus, -tis, -nt*. Every verb, every family. The *ē* is long except before a final *-m, -t, -nt*.

    | Infinitive | I | you | he, she | we | y'all | they |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | *amāre* | *amārem* | *amārēs* | *amāret* | *amārēmus* | *amārētis* | *amārent* |
    | *dūcere* | *dūcerem* | *dūcerēs* | *dūceret* | *dūcerēmus* | *dūcerētis* | *dūcerent* |
    | *esse* | *essem* | *essēs* | *esset* | *essēmus* | *essētis* | *essent* |

## 5. "If only it were so" (but it isn't)

:: Utinam populus Rōmānus ūnam cervīcem habēret! || If only the Roman people had a single neck! (Caligula, in Suetonius, *Caligula* 30.2)

Utinam esset…

{#g-05}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 50: *Utinam veniat!* "If only he comes! May he come!" Can that still happen? | **Yes.** The present subjunctive wishes for something that **may still** come true. |
| Now: *Utinam venīret!* | "**If only he were coming!**" Said when he **isn't**. A wish that's **not really** so, right now. |
| English does the same trick: "I wish you **were** here," a past-looking form for "now, but not really." Say "If only you were here!" | ***Utinam adessēs!*** (*Adsum*, be present: *adesse* → *adessēs*.) |
| "If only I were young!" (*Iuvenis*.) | ***Utinam iuvenis essem!*** |
| "If only it weren't raining!" (*Pluit, pluere*, Chapter 50.) | ***Utinam nē plueret!*** *Nē* for a "not" wish, as in Chapter 50. |
| Suetonius, *Caligula* 30.2: angry at a crowd cheering against his favorites, Caligula shouted *Utinam populus Rōmānus ūnam cervīcem habēret!* (*Cervīx*: neck. Suetonius writes the people as *p. R.*, short for *populus Rōmānus*.) Translate. Why the imperfect? | "**If only** the Roman people **had** a single neck!" (to cut at one blow). A monstrous wish, and luckily not true: ***habēret***. |

## 6. "What was I to do?"

:: Quid facerem? || What was I to do? (Vergil, *Eclogues* 1.40)

Quid faciam? Quid facerem?

{#g-06}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 49's deliberative: *Quid faciam?* "What am I to do?" Asked now. Push it into the past. | ***Quid facerem?*** "**What was I to do?**" (*Facere* + *-m*.) |
| Vergil, *Eclogues* 1.40. The shepherd Tityrus explains why he went to Rome: *Quid facerem? neque servitiō mē exīre licēbat.* (*Servitium*: slavery; *exīre*: go out; *licēbat*: it was allowed, Chapter 44's *licet*.) | "**What was I to do?** It wasn't allowed for me to get out of slavery (any other way)." |
| "Where was I to go?" | ***Quō īrem?*** |

## 7. A goal in a past story

:: Dedī ut darēs. || I gave so that you would give. (teaching line; Chapter 51's *Dō ut dēs*)

Dō ut dēs… dedī ut darēs.

{#g-07}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 51: *Dō ut dēs*, "I give so that you may give." Tell it as a past story: "I **gave** so that you **would** give." | ***Dedī ut darēs.*** (*Dedī*, Chapter 25.) |
| Why not *Dedī ut dēs*? | In a **past** story the goal takes the **infinitive + ending** subjunctive. English shifts too: "may" → "**would**." |
| Paulus, the lawyer of Chapter 51, gives an example (*Digest* 19.5.5.1): *sī scyphōs tibi dedī, ut Stichum mihi darēs…* (*Scyphus*: cup; *Stichus*: a slave's name.) | "If I gave you cups **so that you would give** me Stichus…" (Paulus goes on to say who bears the risk for Stichus.) |
| "Yesterday I went to the forum to buy cheese." (*Iī*, Chapter 25; *emō, emere*.) | ***Herī ad forum iī ut cāseum emerem.*** |
| "I shut the cheese away so the dog wouldn't eat it." (*Edere*.) | ***Cāseum clausī nē canis eum ederet.*** *Nē* still marks a "not" goal. |
| Chapter 51's John 10:10, *ego vēnī ut vītam habeant*, kept the present. Why? | *Vēnī* there means "**I have come**," with the goal still ahead **now**. "Have done" counts as now; a past **story** ("I came," "I went," "I was going") takes the new form. (Chapter 54 gives this matching its full rules.) |

## 8. Caesar's envoys

:: Lēgātōs mittunt quī dīcerent. || They send envoys to say… (after Caesar, *Gallic War* 1.7.3)

Quī dīcerent.

{#g-08}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 51's sending *quī*: *Marcum mittō quī cāseum emat.* Past story: "I sent Marcus to buy cheese." | ***Marcum mīsī quī cāseum emeret.*** |
| Caesar, *Gallic War* 1.7.3. The Helvetians ask to march through Roman land: *lēgātōs ad eum mittunt … quī dīcerent sibi esse in animō sine ūllō maleficiō iter per prōvinciam facere.* (*Lēgātus*: envoy; *esse in animō*: to have in mind; *maleficium*: harm; *iter*: journey, route; *prōvincia*: province.) | "They send envoys to him **to say** that they had in mind to make a journey through the province without any harm." |
| *Mittunt* is present! Why *dīcerent*, not *dīcant*? | Caesar tells a **past** story in the present for vividness (the "historic present"). He knows it's past, so here he gives the goal the past form: ***dīcerent***. (Writers can go either way after a historic present; Chapter 54 sorts it out.) |
| "Grandma sent the boys to buy a new cheese." | ***Avia puerōs mīsit quī novum cāseum emerent.*** (Or *ut novum cāseum emerent.*) |

!!! success "The Law of the Past Goal"
    A goal (*ut, nē, quī*) takes the **present** subjunctive after a "now," "later," command, or "have done" verb; it takes the **imperfect** subjunctive in a **past story**.

    | Now | Past story |
    |---|---|
    | *Dō ut dēs.* I give so that you may give. | *Dedī ut darēs.* I gave so that you would give. |
    | *Cāseum claudō nē canis eum edat.* | *Cāseum clausī nē canis eum ederet.* |
    | *Marcum mittō quī cāseum emat.* | *Marcum mīsī quī cāseum emeret.* |

## 9. "So tired that…"

:: Avus tam fessus erat ut ad mēnsam dormīret. || Grandpa was so tired that he fell asleep at the table. (teaching line; the chapter's title)

Tam… ut…

{#g-09}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Tam* means "**so**" with an adjective or adverb: *tam fessus*, "so tired." *Avus tam fessus erat ut ad mēnsam dormīret.* (*Fessus*, Chapter 12.) Translate. | "Grandpa was **so tired that** he fell asleep at the table." |
| Did Grandpa *aim* to fall asleep? | **No.** It wasn't his goal; it **happened**, because he was *so* tired. That's a **result**. |
| Grammar books call *ut dormīret* a **result clause**. Plain words? | **The "so … that" clause**: it tells what came **of** something being *so* big, *so* tired, *so* loud. Old-school books say "**consecutive clause**," from *cōnsequor*, "follow": the result follows. You spot it early by a **signpost** in the main clause, here *tam*; the others come next. |
| He really did fall asleep. Why isn't *dormīret* a fact verb? | Latin's rule: a "so … that" clause takes the **subjunctive**, even when the result is true. Here the mood doesn't mean "maybe"; it marks the clause that hangs on "so." English uses a plain fact: "he **fell** asleep." |

## 10. The signposts

:: Quot capita, tot sententiae. || So many heads, so many opinions. (proverb; Chapter 14)

Tam, ita, sīc, tantus, tot.

{#g-10}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| ***Ita*** (Chapter 2's "yes, it is so") and ***sīc*** (Chapter 19) mean "**so, in such a way**," with verbs. *Avus ita clāmābat ut omnēs eum audīrent.* | "Grandpa was shouting **in such a way that** (so loudly that) everyone heard him." |
| ***Tantus, tanta, tantum***: "**so big, so great**." *Tantus erat cāseus ut eum vix portārēmus.* (*Vix*: barely.) | "The cheese was **so big that** we could barely carry it." |
| ***Tot***: "**so many**," Chapter 14's proverb. It never changes its ending. *Tot puerī aderant ut mēnsa plēna esset.* | "**So many** boys were there **that** the table was full." |
| Results happen now, too. "I'm so tired that I can barely stand." (*Stō, stāre*; *possum*.) | ***Tam fessus sum ut vix stāre possim.*** A "now" main verb, a present subjunctive: Chapter 49's forms. |

## 11. Real results

:: Sīc enim dīlēxit Deus mundum ut Fīlium suum ūnigenitum daret. || For God so loved the world that he gave his only-begotten Son. (John 3:16)

Sīc… ut daret.

{#g-11}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| John 3:16: *Sīc enim dīlēxit Deus mundum ut Fīlium suum ūnigenitum daret.* (*Dīligō, dīlēxī*: love; *ūnigenitus*: only-begotten. This is the Nova Vulgata; the older Clementine text has *Sīc enim Deus dīlēxit mundum*.) Signpost? | ***Sīc***, "so": "For God **so** loved the world **that** he gave his only-begotten Son." |
| Which form is *daret*? | Imperfect subjunctive of *dō*: ***dare* + *-t***, short *a*. The result of so much love. |
| Matthew 8:24, the disciples' boat: *Et ecce mōtus magnus factus est in marī, ita ut nāvicula operīrētur flūctibus.* (*Mōtus*: upheaval, storm; *nāvicula*: little boat; *operiō*: cover; *flūctus*: wave.) | "And look, a great storm arose on the sea, **so that** the boat **was being covered** by the waves." |
| *Operīrētur*: build it. | *Operīre* + passive *-tur* → ***operīrētur***, "it was being covered." |
| The verse ends: *ipse vērō dormiēbat.* (*Vērō*: but.) | "**But he himself was sleeping.**" Jesus slept through the storm. |

## 12. "So … that … not": *ut nōn, ut nēmō*

:: Ita ut nēmō posset trānsīre per viam illam. || So that no one could pass along that road. (Matthew 8:28)

Ut nōn… ut nēmō…

{#g-12}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chapter 51 warned twice: *ut nōn* and *ut nēmō* "have a different job." Here it is. "Grandpa was so tired that he could**n't** walk." | ***Avus tam fessus erat ut ambulāre nōn posset.*** A result's "not" is plain ***nōn***, with *ut* kept. |
| Why not *nē*? | ***Nē*** is for what someone **wants** (goals, wishes, "let's not"). A result isn't wanted; it just happens. So plain *nōn*. |
| "So that nobody…," "so that nothing…" in a result? | ***Ut nēmō*** and ***ut nihil***. (*Ut numquam*, "so that never," and *ut nūllus*, "so that no," too.) |
| Matthew 8:28: two men possessed by demons came out of the tombs, *saevī nimis, ita ut nēmō posset trānsīre per viam illam.* (*Saevus*: fierce; *nimis*: too, very.) | "Very fierce, **so that no one could** pass along that road." |
| Caesar, *Gallic War* 4.28.2, the ships carrying his cavalry to Britain: *tanta tempestās subitō coorta est ut nūlla eārum cursum tenēre posset.* (*Tempestās*: storm; *coorior, coortus sum*: arise; *eārum*: of them, the ships; *cursus*: course.) | "**So great a storm** suddenly arose **that none of them could** hold its course." Signpost *tanta*; "not" by *nūlla*. |
| Cicero's old Cato looks back on his life (*On Old Age* 84): *ita vīxī, ut nōn frūstrā mē nātum exīstimem.* (*Vīxī*: I have lived; *nātum* = *nātum esse*, been born; *exīstimō*: think.) | "I **have lived in such a way that** I do**n't** think I was born in vain." A "have done" verb and a result still true **now**: present ***exīstimem***. |

## 13. Goal or result?

:: Aliud est cōnsilium, aliud ēventus. || A plan is one thing, an outcome another. (teaching line)

Nē? Ut nōn?

{#g-13}

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Cāseum clausī nē canis eum ederet.* Goal or result? | **Goal**: "I shut the cheese away **so that the dog wouldn't** eat it." *Nē* = wanted. |
| *Cāseum tam bene clausī ut canis eum nōn ederet.* | **Result**: "I shut the cheese away **so well that the dog didn't** eat it." Signpost *tam*, "not" by *ut nōn*. |
| *Cantāvit ut Avus dormīret.* vs *Ita cantāvit ut Avus dormīret.* | First: "She sang **so that** Grandpa **would** sleep" (her goal). Second: "She sang **in such a way that** Grandpa fell asleep" (it happened). The signpost decides. |
| Purpose: "so that no one…." Result: "so that no one…." Latin? | Purpose: ***nē quis*** (Chapter 51). Result: ***ut nēmō***. |

!!! success "The Law of So … That"
    A **result clause** (the "so … that" clause) tells what came **of** something. A **signpost** in the main clause warns you: *tam* (so), *ita, sīc* (so, in such a way), *tantus* (so big), *tot* (so many). Then ***ut*** + subjunctive: present for a "now" result, imperfect in a past story.

    | Latin | English |
    |---|---|
    | *Avus tam fessus erat ut ad mēnsam dormīret.* | Grandpa was so tired that he fell asleep at the table. |
    | *Tam fessus sum ut vix stāre possim.* | I'm so tired that I can barely stand. |
    | *Sīc dīlēxit Deus mundum ut Fīlium daret.* | God so loved the world that he gave his Son. |

!!! success "The Law of the Two Nots"
    A **goal** that should not happen: ***nē*** (*nē quis, nē quid*). A **result** that did not happen: ***ut nōn*** (*ut nēmō, ut nihil, ut numquam, ut nūllus*).

    | Goal (wanted) | Result (happened) |
    |---|---|
    | *Cāseum clausī nē canis eum ederet.* | *Cāseum tam bene clausī ut canis eum nōn ederet.* |
    | *nē quis* · so that no one (I hope) | *ut nēmō* · so that no one (in fact) |

## 16. Lectiō: *Tam fessus ut…*

:: Utinam iuvenis essem! || If only I were young! (teaching line)

Tam… ut… Quid facerem?

Diē vīcēsimā: the day after Chapter 51. The new big cheese sits on the table; the old small one is shut in the cupboard. In the night, a storm. Read each Latin line aloud. Guess the English, then tap to check. For the reverse drill, tap **Tege Latīna** and rebuild each line from the English. Hunt every *tam, ita, tantus*, every *ut*, and every new *-rem* form. New in passing: *vīcēsimus*, twentieth; *barba*, beard (Chapter 35); *respondī*, I answered (*respondeō*, Chapter 5); *laetus*, happy; *tandem*, at last. Old friends: *armārium*, cupboard, and *expergīscor*, wake up (Chapter 51).

Quīntus | Et ego canem tenēbam, ut Marcus cāseum portāret. || Quintus | And I was holding the dog so that Marcus could carry the cheese.
Cornēlia | Fortasse canis…? || Cornelia | Maybe the dog…?
Marcus | Canis tam parvus est ut armārium aperīre nōn possit. || Marcus | The dog is so small that he can't open the cupboard.
Iōannēs | Avus expergīscitur. In barbā eius est pars parva cāseī. || John | Grandpa wakes up. In his beard is a small piece of cheese.
Avia | Ave! Tū cāseum nostrum ēdistī! || Grandma | Grandpa! You ate our cheese!
Avus | Quid facerem? Tanta erat tempestās ut dormīre nōn possem. || Grandpa | What was I to do? The storm was so great that I couldn't sleep.
Avus | Et tam ēsuriēbam ut stomachus meus clāmāret! || Grandpa | And I was so hungry that my stomach was shouting!
Avia | Sed armārium clausī, nē quis cāseum invenīret! || Grandma | But I shut the cupboard so that nobody would find the cheese!
Avus | Et ego armārium aperuī, ut cāseum invenīrem. Et invēnī! || Grandpa | And I opened the cupboard so that I would find the cheese. And I found it!
Avia | Utinam canis Avum custōdīret! || Grandma | If only the dog were guarding Grandpa!
Avus | Utinam iuvenis essem! Iuvenēs tam bene dormiunt ut nocte numquam ēsuriant. || Grandpa | If only I were young! Young people sleep so well that they're never hungry at night.
Avia | Cāseum magnum in mēnsā pōnō, nē Avus eum sōlus edat. || Grandma | I'm putting the big cheese on the table, so that Grandpa doesn't eat it alone.
Iōannēs | Subitō sōl lūcet. Tempestās abiit. Cornēlia ad lūdum it. || John | Suddenly the sun shines. The storm has gone. Cornelia goes to school.
Iōannēs | Post hōrās trēs Cornēlia redit. Tam laeta est ut cantet. || John | Three hours later Cornelia comes back. She's so happy that she's singing.
Avia | Cūr tam laeta es? || Grandma | Why are you so happy?
Cornēlia | Magister mē interrogāvit, et tam bene respondī ut mē laudāret! || Cornelia | The teacher quizzed me, and I answered so well that he praised me!
Avia | Optimē! Tibi partem cāseī magnī dō. || Grandma | Excellent! I'm giving you a piece of the big cheese.
Avus | Et mihi? || Grandpa | And me?
Avia | Tibi? Nocte satis ēdistī! || Grandma | You? You ate enough last night!
Avus | Ō, utinam nē tam ēsurīrem… || Grandpa | Oh, if only I weren't so hungry…
Iōannēs | Canis ad Avum venit et cāseum in barbā eius lambit. || John | The dog comes to Grandpa and licks the cheese in his beard.
Avus | Ecce! Canis tam bonus est ut mē adiuvet. || Grandpa | Look! The dog is so good that he helps me.
Iōannēs | Omnēs tam clārē rīdent ut canis quoque lātret. || John | Everyone laughs so loudly that the dog barks too.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Why could nobody sleep? Answer in Latin with the Lectiō's own words. | ***Tanta tempestās fuit ut nēmō dormīre posset.*** |
| Find two goals in a past story, with their new forms. | *nē quis eum **tangeret** · ut novum cāseum **emerem** · ut Marcus cāseum **portāret** · nē quis cāseum **invenīret** · ut cāseum **invenīrem** · ut omnia **scīrem**.* (Any two.) |
| Find a result with *ut nōn*. Who does it clear? | *Canis tam parvus est ut armārium aperīre **nōn possit***: it clears the **dog**. |

## Speak it aloud

:: Loquāmur Latīnē! || Let's speak Latin! (teaching line)

1. In your own words: how do you build the imperfect subjunctive, and what are its jobs today?
2. Chant *amārem, dūcerem, essem, possem, vellem, īrem, ferrem* in all six persons, and *amārer* in the passive.
3. Make three wishes that aren't true now (*Utinam … essem! Utinam … adessēs!*), one with *nē*.
4. Tell three past goals: where you went and why (*Herī ad forum iī ut…*), one with *nē*, one with a sending *quī*.
5. Say how tired, hungry, and happy you were, and what came of it, with *tam, tantus, tot, ita*; two with *ut nōn* or *ut nēmō*.
6. The four Laws in your own words: the Infinitive Plus, the Past Goal, So … That, the Two Nots.
7. The real lines: *Quam vellem nescīre litterās! · Utinam populus Rōmānus ūnam cervīcem habēret! · Quid facerem? neque servitiō mē exīre licēbat · sī scyphōs tibi dedī, ut Stichum mihi darēs · lēgātōs ad eum mittunt … quī dīcerent · Sīc enim dīlēxit Deus mundum ut Fīlium suum ūnigenitum daret · ita ut nāvicula operīrētur flūctibus · ita ut nēmō posset trānsīre per viam illam · tanta tempestās subitō coorta est ut nūlla eārum cursum tenēre posset · ita vīxī, ut nōn frūstrā mē nātum exīstimem.* Then read the *Lectiō* aloud, playing every part, and rebuild it from the English.
